Reference · How the Gemini API free tier works

Is the Gemini API free tier actually free?

Short answer: yes, it's really free — $0, no card. There are still real limits and a data trade-off worth knowing, so here's how it actually works, with every sentence linked to the specific Google page it comes from.

Last checked 2026-08-24. Google changes these numbers without much notice, so treat the linked pages as the source of truth, not this one.

$0 — no charge

Straight answer: this doesn't cost money. If Bedside English pointed you to this page and you're wondering whether the app quietly went paid — it didn't. A Gemini API key is issued for free, directly by Google, with no credit card and no trial clock attached.

Nothing here charges you automatically. You'd only ever be billed if you deliberately went into Google's own billing settings and turned on a paid tier yourself — and even then, only for usage above the free limits.

The one trade-off worth knowing: on the free tier, your prompts and responses may be used to improve Google's products. There are also three separate rate limits stacked on top of each other, they reset on a clock (not a countdown), and the quota belongs to a Google Cloud project — not to you personally. Details on each, below.

01Getting a key really is free

A Gemini API key can be created with a Google account alone — no billing profile, no card, no trial-expiry clock.[1]

The key is issued from Google AI Studio's API key screen, not from the general Cloud Console billing flow.[2]

You can attach the key to a brand-new Google Cloud project or to one you already have — either way, no billing account is required to receive a working free-tier key.[1]

02What "free" actually costs: your data

On the free (unpaid) tier, the prompts you send and the responses you get back may be used by Google to improve its products and machine-learning models.[3]

Once a project has a Cloud Billing account attached and moves to a paid tier, that same traffic is excluded from being used to improve Google's products.[3]

In other words, "free" here doesn't mean "no cost" — it means you're paying with data instead of money. That's worth knowing before you route anything sensitive through a free-tier key.

03Three limits apply at once, not one

Every free-tier call is checked against three independent ceilings simultaneously: requests per minute (RPM), tokens per minute (TPM), and requests per day (RPD).[4]

Hitting any one of the three — not just the daily count — is enough to get your next call rejected until that specific window clears.[4]

The exact numbers differ per model, and Google does not promise they'll stay the same — it states that limits depend on your tier and account status and can change automatically as those change.[4]

Not every model is included in the free tier at all — which models are free, and which are paid-only, is listed on Google's own pricing page rather than the rate-limit page.[5]

04The daily counter resets on a clock, not a timer

The requests-per-day count resets at midnight Pacific Time — a fixed point on the clock, not "24 hours after your first call of the day."[4]

Converted: that's roughly 4pm–5pm Korea time (KST, UTC+9), depending on whether the US is on daylight saving — so a key that runs dry in the Korean morning won't refill until Korean late afternoon, not at midnight KST.

05The quota belongs to the project, not the key or the person

Rate limits are enforced per Google Cloud project. Creating additional API keys inside the same project does not create additional quota — every key in that project draws from the same pool.[4]

A project's tier — Free, or Tier 1/2/3 once paid — is determined automatically from that project's billing status and cumulative spend history.[6]

06So does making more projects get you more free quota?

Structurally: yes — because the limit is attached to the project, a genuinely separate Google Cloud project starts with its own separate free-tier pool.[4]

But Google runs both automated and manual abuse monitoring across the Gemini API and AI Studio, and a project that keeps showing suspicious usage patterns can be flagged for manual review.[7]

Logs are retained separately for up to 55 days specifically for detecting and preventing violations of Google's usage policies.[7]

Exactly where "spin up a few personal projects" ends and "policy violation" begins isn't spelled out in one clean sentence anywhere — that line lives in the Terms of Service themselves, not in this summary, so read the source directly before you build anything that depends on it.

07Outgrowing the free tier

Attaching a Cloud Billing account to a project is what moves it off the Free tier onto usage-based paid tiers.[6]

Paid tiers raise the RPM/TPM/RPD ceilings substantially over the free tier, and a project can climb further (Tier 1 → 2 → 3) automatically as its billing history accumulates.[4]

Billing is metered — enabling it does not by itself charge you; it charges only for the usage that exceeds what the free tier would have covered, priced per Google's published rates.[5]
