Clinical breast examination — inspection and systematic palpation

Show consented symptom-directed inspection positions and systematic tissue coverage, not a substitute for indicated imaging or a screening schedule.

How to do it

  1. Explain the indication, obtain consent, offer a chaperone and provide privacy/draping. Ask about the patient’s concern and tenderness.
  2. Inspect seated with arms relaxed, then raised, then hands pressing on hips. Note new asymmetry, skin change, dimpling and nipple change, rather than interpreting ordinary asymmetry as disease.
  3. For palpation lie supine, placing the examined-side arm above the head and a small support beneath that shoulder as needed. Cover the opposite breast.
  4. Use the pads of the middle three fingers with small overlapping circles and graded light/medium/deep pressure as tolerated. Cover all tissue systematically; the illustrated option uses overlapping vertical strips including the axillary tail and tissue beneath the nipple.
  5. Assess regional nodes separately, supporting the relaxed arm for axillary palpation. Record a finding’s side, clock-face/quadrant, distance from nipple, size, texture, mobility and skin relation.
  6. Cover, allow dressing and explain the next assessment. A persistent suspicious symptom or mass needs appropriate imaging/referral even if palpation is reassuring.

What to say to the patient

  1. With your permission, I will look and then examine one breast at a time. Please tell me if you want me to stop.

A positive finding

Describe a new mass, focal thickening, dimpling, nipple retraction/discharge or nodes without diagnosing cancer from palpation alone.

A negative finding

A normal clinical examination does not exclude breast cancer or replace symptom-directed imaging and follow-up.

Evidence & limitations

Systematic coverage and finger-pad contact are emphasized. The map is an examination region, not a tumour diagram; no tissue stiffness or pressure depth is simulated.

Points to remember

  1. Do not pinch the breast or routinely squeeze a nipple to manufacture discharge.
  2. The enlarged right-breast map is anterior: axilla is screen-left and sternum screen-right. Nodes and actual pathological findings are not drawn.
