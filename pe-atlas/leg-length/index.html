Leg length: true and apparent

Use consistent endpoints to distinguish bony-length estimates from apparent asymmetry.

How to do it

  1. Supine, expose and identify both ASIS and medial malleoli with consent and draping. Align the pelvis and place both legs in comparable rotation and abduction.
  2. Estimate true length with a tape from each ASIS to the ipsilateral medial malleolus. Use the same endpoint and tape tension on both sides; repeat if inconsistent.
  3. For apparent length, measure from the umbilicus to each medial malleolus. Document the chosen method, both values and their difference.
  4. Consider contractures, pelvic obliquity and landmark error. If the difference matters clinically, use an appropriate standing block assessment or imaging rather than treating this estimate as definitive.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will find the bony points at the front of your hips and inside your ankles, then measure both legs.

A positive finding

A reproducible side difference suggests inequality needing context. Disagreement between true and apparent estimates can reflect pelvic or joint positioning.

A negative finding

Equal tape measurements do not exclude a clinically relevant discrepancy or functional asymmetry.

Evidence & limitations

Clinical tape measurements have limited accuracy; do not interchange medial and lateral malleolar endpoints or infer an imaging diagnosis.

Points to remember

  1. Correct unequal rotation or abduction before measuring; never force a contracted limb into alignment.
  2. Lines identify tape routes on a proportioned schematic. No measured centimetres, true scale for an individual, or pathological shortening are depicted.
