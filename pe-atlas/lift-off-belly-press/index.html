Belly-press test — subscapularis

Assess active shoulder internal rotation through the belly-press variant. This card does not demonstrate the behind-the-back lift-off test.

How to do it

  1. Explain and inspect active shoulder motion first; account for pain and restricted passive motion.
  2. With the patient sitting, place the palm on the abdomen, elbow bent and held anterior to the trunk, with the wrist straight.
  3. Ask the patient to press the palm into the abdomen through shoulder internal rotation while keeping that elbow position and a neutral wrist.
  4. Observe whether pressure can be maintained without the elbow dropping backward or the wrist flexing. Compare sides with similar positioning.
  5. Relax and record weakness, pain and compensations separately. Integrate other cuff tests and imaging when clinically appropriate.

What to say to the patient

  1. Press your palm into your belly and keep your elbow in front.
  2. Keep your wrist straight. Let me know if this hurts.

A positive finding

Inability to sustain internal-rotation pressure without posterior elbow drift or wrist-flexion substitution raises concern for subscapularis dysfunction. Pain inhibition and stiffness can mimic weakness.

A negative finding

Good performance does not exclude a partial subscapularis tear.

Evidence & limitations

A 2021 meta-analysis found limited sensitivity of individual subscapularis tests. Avoid treating one belly-press result as a definitive tendon diagnosis.

Points to remember

  1. The palm presses the abdomen; an examiner pushing the elbow is not required for this variant.
  2. Wrist flexion is a compensation, not the requested movement.
  3. Side-view examples make elbow drift visible; applied pressure, muscle contraction and tendon integrity are not measured.
