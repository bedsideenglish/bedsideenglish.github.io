Castell's splenic percussion sign

Screen for splenic enlargement by comparing the percussion note at one left lower-chest interspace during expiration and inspiration. This card teaches Castell's sign; it does not map Traube's space.

How to do it

  1. Explain and obtain consent. With the patient supine, expose the left lower chest and upper abdomen with appropriate draping.
  2. Find the lowest accessible intercostal space on the LEFT anterior axillary line, just above the costal margin; the exact space depends on the patient.
  3. At the end of a normal expiration, gently percuss this interspace and identify the baseline note.
  4. Ask for a deep breath and percuss the SAME point again during inspiration. Do not move down onto a rib or into the abdomen.
  5. Record the two notes. Complete splenic palpation; obtain imaging when enlargement remains clinically relevant or uncertain.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will tap gently between your lower ribs on the left. Breathe out normally first.
  2. Now take a deep breath in. I will tap in the same place.

A positive finding

A previously resonant/tympanitic point becoming dull on inspiration is a positive Castell sign and raises suspicion for splenic enlargement. Baseline dullness alone does not demonstrate this respiratory change.

A negative finding

A resonant note through inspiration does not exclude splenomegaly. Habitus, adjacent bowel/stomach contents and thoracic pathology can limit interpretation.

Evidence & limitations

This is a bedside clue used with palpation and the clinical picture. Published performance varies with population and reference standard, so no universal accuracy estimate is assigned here.

Points to remember

  1. Keep the strike in the interspace and the contact fixed between breaths; striking a rib can create false dullness.
  2. The front-view schematic projects the lateral contact above the left costal margin; it does not depict every lower rib, sound, or a measured splenic descent. The normal spleen outline is an orientation aid, not a positive result.
