Upper-arm blood pressure · technique and both-arm comparison

Obtain a reproducible adult arm-cuff measurement. The panels separate seated preparation, cuff placement, manual auscultation and the initial comparison of arms.

How to do it

  1. For a routine resting measurement, avoid exercise, caffeine and smoking for 30 minutes, empty the bladder and rest quietly for at least 5 minutes. Urgent assessment must not wait for this routine preparation.
  2. Support the back, keep feet flat and legs uncrossed, and support the bare arm so the cuff midpoint is at right-atrial level. Do not talk during measurement.
  3. Measure mid-upper-arm circumference and select the manufacturer’s matching cuff range. Place the lower edge about 2–3 cm above the antecubital crease with the artery marker aligned to the brachial artery.
  4. A validated automatic upper-arm device performs the measurement according to its instructions. For manual auscultation, first estimate systolic pressure by palpation, release the cuff, then reinflate 20–30 mmHg above that estimate. Place the stethoscope over the brachial artery below the cuff, without trapping it underneath.
  5. Deflate at about 2 mmHg per second; record the first Korotkoff sounds as systolic and disappearance as diastolic pressure in adults. If sounds persist to zero, document this and follow the appropriate protocol. The diagram supplies no sounds or patient readings.
  6. Initially measure both arms and repeat an unexpected difference with the same correct technique. For routine follow-up use the consistently higher arm per the applicable guideline; record arm, posture, cuff and repeat readings at least 1 minute apart. One reading does not diagnose hypertension or exclude aortic disease.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please rest your arm here and keep your feet flat. We will stay quiet while I measure, then check the other arm.

A positive finding

A repeatedly high, low or asymmetric pressure requires interpretation with symptoms and measurement conditions, and confirmation appropriate to the clinical context.

A negative finding

A normal arm pressure does not exclude shock early in its course, aortic dissection or episodic hypertension.

Evidence & limitations

Position, cuff fit and repetition affect accuracy. This is an adult routine-technique plate, not a diagnostic threshold chart or an obstetric/pediatric protocol.

Points to remember

  1. A cuff over clothes, an unsupported arm, talking and an undersized cuff can distort the result. Avoid limbs with a dialysis access or other relevant restriction.
