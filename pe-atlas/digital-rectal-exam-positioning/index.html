Rectal examination — consent, preparation and left-lateral position

Prepare respectfully for an indicated rectal examination; this is a positioning card, not an insertion or internal-palpation tutorial.

How to do it

  1. Explain why the examination is proposed, what it involves and the right to stop. Obtain consent and offer an appropriate chaperone.
  2. Arrange privacy to undress and cover with a drape. Ask about pain, prior difficulties and positioning limitations.
  3. Help the patient lie on the left side with head supported and hips/knees comfortably flexed toward the chest. Avoid forcing extreme flexion.
  4. Prepare gloves, lubricant and wipes. Expose only the required posterior area immediately before examination with renewed explanation.
  5. The subsequent indicated examination requires gentle supervised technique and ongoing consent; afterwards cover, offer cleaning/dressing privacy and explain/document findings.

What to say to the patient

  1. You can ask me to stop at any time. Would you like a chaperone? Please lie on your left side with your knees comfortably bent.

A positive finding

This preparation card has no positive diagnostic result. Record only actual subsequent findings from an indicated, consented examination.

A negative finding

Comfortable positioning or an unremarkable limited rectal examination does not exclude colorectal or prostate disease; follow symptom-specific evaluation.

Evidence & limitations

Left-lateral flexed positioning is a common option, with alternatives chosen for comfort, access and clinical needs. The draped figure does not portray anal insertion or findings.

Points to remember

  1. Do not proceed through refusal or severe pain. A chaperone does not replace consent.
  2. No reflex, anal tone, prostate size, stool test or cancer-screening result can be inferred from this illustration.
