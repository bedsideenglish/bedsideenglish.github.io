Elbow inspection and active range of motion

Distinguish a change around the elbow joint from superficial olecranon swelling and document movement. Inspect, feel, then ask the patient to move; this is not a fracture-exclusion rule.

How to do it

  1. Explain, obtain consent and expose both elbows. Compare skin, alignment, scars and the contours beside and behind the olecranon.
  2. Gently assess warmth and tenderness. Look for loss of the lateral joint recess contour; distinguish this from a superficial swelling over the olecranon tip.
  3. With the upper arm steady by the side, ask the patient to straighten and then bend the elbow as far as comfortable. Observe the arc from the side and record pain and any extension deficit.
  4. With the elbow bent about 90 degrees, also ask for palm-up and palm-down forearm rotation. Compare both sides; this rotation is described in text rather than animated.
  5. If appropriate, assess passive movement gently to clarify a limitation. Do not force an acutely painful, hot or traumatised joint; arrange the indicated further assessment.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please straighten your elbow, then bend it as far as feels comfortable.
  2. Keep your upper arm still. Tell me where it hurts.
  3. With your elbow bent, turn your palm up and then down.

A positive finding

Joint-region swelling, restricted movement or painful passive motion warrants evaluation for an intra-articular process. Posterior tip swelling may be bursitis. A hot painful joint needs prompt assessment for infection and other causes.

A negative finding

A painless full arc is reassuring but does not exclude tendinopathy, early disease or a fracture after trauma. Normal-looking movement alone does not decide whether imaging is needed.

Evidence & limitations

Joint recess swelling and olecranon bursal swelling have different locations. Record actual range rather than treating the demonstration angles as diagnostic thresholds.

Points to remember

  1. Inspect behind the elbow as well as the lateral side. Compare active and passive findings only when safe.
  2. The animation demonstrates active flexion/extension with no examiner contact. It does not show effusion, bursal swelling, palpation, forearm rotation or a goniometer measurement.
