Liver span by percussion

Estimate the vertical extent of hepatic dullness on the right midclavicular line. Find two sound transitions; a low palpable edge alone does not establish enlargement.

How to do it

  1. Explain, obtain consent and position the patient supine, with the right chest and abdomen exposed and the rest draped.
  2. Identify the right midclavicular line. Place a middle finger firmly on the skin and strike its distal joint briefly with the opposite middle fingertip; lift the other fingers off the skin.
  3. Begin over resonant lung around the third intercostal space. Percuss downward along the same line; mark the first sustained transition to dullness as the upper border.
  4. Restart below the umbilicus where the note is tympanitic. Percuss upward on that line; mark the transition to dullness as the lower border.
  5. Compare both borders at the same quiet respiratory phase and measure the vertical distance in centimetres. Describe the examination conditions; use ultrasound if the result matters or the borders are uncertain.

What to say to the patient

  1. I'll tap gently down your right chest, then up your tummy, to estimate the size of your liver.
  2. Please breathe normally. Tell me if anything hurts.

A positive finding

A wider-than-expected span supports enlargement, but adjacent lung consolidation or pleural fluid can imitate hepatic dullness. Interpret with body size, palpation and imaging.

A negative finding

A usual span does not exclude liver disease or small lesions. Downward displacement of both borders can occur without an enlarged span.

Evidence & limitations

Stanford gives a commonly taught adult right-midclavicular range of 6–12 cm. This is a bedside estimate, not an ultrasound cutoff; technique and habitus affect it.

Points to remember

  1. Use the right side and one vertical line for both searches. Do not measure only how far an edge lies below the ribs.
  2. The diagram marks illustrative sound boundaries, not a measured patient result. Tap sound and an actual measuring tape are not simulated.
