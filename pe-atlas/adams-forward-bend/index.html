Adams forward bend: positioning and inspection

Look for trunk rotational prominence during a controlled forward bend.

See the examination

Bend forwards; let both arms hang at equal height

At back height, compare the two sides from behind

Return slowly to standing

How to do it

  1. With consent, inspect the exposed back while the patient stands with feet together or comfortably close, equal weight bearing and knees straight.
  2. Ask the patient to bend forward slowly with arms relaxed together and palms facing each other. Stop if painful or unsteady.
  3. View along the back from behind at trunk height; compare right and left thoracic and lumbar contours. An anterior view may add information.
  4. If a prominence is seen, use a scoliometer when trained and arrange further assessment as appropriate. Return to standing slowly.

What to say to the patient

  1. Keep your knees straight and bend forward slowly, letting your arms hang together. Stop if you feel pain or dizziness.

A positive finding

A persistent unilateral rib or lumbar prominence raises suspicion of trunk rotation and scoliosis; it is not a Cobb-angle diagnosis.

A negative finding

A symmetric-looking back does not exclude a small curve or all spinal disease.

Evidence & limitations

This is an examination manoeuvre, not a universal screening recommendation or a substitute for indicated standing radiographs.

Points to remember

  1. Avoid unequal knee flexion or asymmetric arm position that can create apparent trunk asymmetry.
  2. The mannequin shows the approach and posture only. Its trunk is rigid: spinal segment flexion, a rib hump and a scoliometer reading are not simulated.
