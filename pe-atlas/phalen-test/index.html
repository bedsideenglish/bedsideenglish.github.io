Phalen test: unforced wrist-flexion variant

Try to reproduce the patient’s usual median-distribution symptoms during sustained wrist flexion.

How to do it

  1. Ask about baseline tingling and painful or restricted wrists before starting.
  2. Seat the patient with forearms raised and fingers relaxed. Let the wrists bend forward into comfortable full flexion without added pressure; the hands stay apart in this variant.
  3. Maintain for up to 60 seconds, stopping once familiar symptoms occur or if painful. Ask which fingers are affected and record the time.
  4. Return to neutral and allow symptoms to settle. Combine with the history, sensation, thenar examination and other relevant findings.

What to say to the patient

  1. Let your wrists bend gently. Tell me if your usual tingling starts and point to the affected fingers.

A positive finding

Familiar tingling or numbness in the thumb, index, middle or radial ring finger supports carpal tunnel syndrome in context. Wrist discomfort alone is not the target response.

A negative finding

No symptom reproduction does not exclude carpal tunnel syndrome.

Evidence & limitations

Provocation tests have variable accuracy and should not be used alone to diagnose or exclude entrapment.

Points to remember

  1. This is sustained flexion, not the prayer-position extension test or direct carpal compression.
  2. The animation compresses the 60-second observation and shows a comfortable example angle. It does not display numbness or map individual digital sensation.
