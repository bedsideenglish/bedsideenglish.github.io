Resisted wrist flexion for medial elbow pain

Load the common flexor origin while checking the location of familiar pain.

How to do it

  1. Support the arm palm up with the elbow comfortably near extension; locate the common flexor origin just distal/anterior to the medial epicondyle.
  2. Ask the patient to bend the wrist toward the palm. Add gradual opposing pressure at the palm without forcing the elbow straight.
  3. Ask about familiar focal medial elbow pain. Release the resistance, return to neutral and compare sides.
  4. Resisted pronation is a separate assessment of the flexor-pronator group; the figure isolates wrist flexion.

What to say to the patient

  1. Bend your wrist toward you and hold against my hand. Point to where it hurts.

A positive finding

Familiar pain localised to the common flexor origin supports medial elbow tendinopathy.

A negative finding

No reproduced pain does not exclude a tendon disorder, ulnar neuropathy or ligament injury.

Evidence & limitations

A clinical pain-provocation component, not a stand-alone diagnostic or severity score.

Points to remember

  1. Tingling into the ring/little finger suggests a different or coexisting problem; do not compress the ulnar nerve behind the epicondyle.
  2. The hand posture and opposing pressure are schematic; tendon strain, torque and grip strength are not measured.
