Testicular and epididymal palpation

Distinguish testis, epididymis and cord while gently locating tenderness or a lump.

See the examination

A · Right lateral cutaway: epididymal head is superior, body posterior and tail inferior; the vas ascends into the cord.

B · A gloved hand supports the less painful side. Relief with elevation does not distinguish epididymitis from torsion reliably.

C · Opposed gentle contacts assess the testis without squeezing; transparent anatomy explains structures not visible through skin.

D · Identify epididymis and ascending cord separately. Record exact tenderness or mass location without delaying urgent torsion assessment.

How to do it

  1. Confirm a specific indication. Explain the external and internal contacts separately, obtain consent, offer an appropriate chaperone, provide private undressing and draping, and agree on a stop signal. Recheck permission as the examination proceeds; stop for pain or withdrawal of consent. A learner needs supervised training.
  2. Inspect first. In acute scrotal pain, prioritize urgent torsion evaluation and avoid repeated painful testing. Begin with the less painful side and support the scrotum with a gloved hand.
  3. Gently palpate each testis individually between finger pads and thumb without squeezing. Assess contour, consistency, approximate size, tenderness and whether a lump is within the testis or separate.
  4. Identify the epididymal head superiorly, body along the posterior/posterolateral testis, tail inferiorly and the ascending cord/vas. Do not label the normal epididymis a testicular mass.
  5. Compare the other side and record exact tenderness and mass location. Pain relief with elevation and apparently normal palpation cannot exclude torsion. New intratesticular firmness or mass needs appropriate assessment.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will support the scrotum and gently feel each testicle, starting on the less painful side. Tell me if you want me to stop.

A positive finding

Localized epididymal tenderness, a testicular mass or cord abnormality helps direct investigation; no single palpation pattern establishes infection or excludes torsion.

A negative finding

A smooth or minimally tender testis does not exclude torsion, early disease or a lesion beyond the examiner’s reach.

Evidence & limitations

Right lateral cutaway labels posterior to screen-right and separates posterior epididymis from testis. Surface support and palpation contacts overlay transparent anatomy.

Points to remember

  1. No twisting, hard compression or Prehn-sign exclusion rule; consent and urgent clinical priorities remain central.
