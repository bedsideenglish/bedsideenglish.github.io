CN XI: shoulder shrug and resisted head turn

Assess trapezius and sternocleidomastoid strength supplied by the accessory nerve.

See the examination

How to do it

  1. Inspect the shoulders and neck for asymmetry or wasting.
  2. Ask the patient to shrug both shoulders while you apply downward resistance; compare sides.
  3. Ask them to turn the head against your hand on the cheek; palpate the opposite sternocleidomastoid.
  4. Repeat in the other direction; note weakness, pain and restricted range.

What to say to the patient

  1. Shrug your shoulders and do not let me push them down.
  2. Turn your head against my hand.

A positive finding

Weak shrug or contralateral head turn may indicate accessory-nerve or muscle dysfunction, interpreted with the rest of the exam.

A negative finding

Symmetric performance does not exclude a partial lesion or pain-limited dysfunction.

Evidence & limitations

Turning left primarily tests the right sternocleidomastoid, and vice versa.

Points to remember

  1. Use gentle resistance; do not force a painful or injured neck.
  2. The shrug and head turns illustrate positioning and resistance direction; compare actual strength bilaterally.
