Saddle sensation · selected perineal light-touch sites

Elicit subjective sensory change and, when indicated, compare selected perineal sites without using a normal result to exclude CES.

C · Record subjective change and exact examined sites, including limitations. Normal findings are not a CES exclusion test or a referral gate.

How to do it

  1. Ask specifically about new altered feeling with wiping, in the perineum/genitals, bladder filling/flow, rectal fullness and sexual function. A small or patchy sensory change can matter; a complete saddle-shaped deficit is not required.
  2. Confirm a specific indication. Explain the external and internal contacts separately, obtain consent, offer an appropriate chaperone, provide private undressing and draping, and agree on a stop signal. Recheck permission as the examination proceeds; stop for pain or withdrawal of consent. A learner needs supervised training.
  3. If appropriate, arrange safe positioning and draping with only the required area exposed. Demonstrate the stimulus, then compare corresponding sides with eyes closed using a clean light-touch stimulus. Do not use a sharp implement based on this figure.
  4. Ask whether contact is felt and whether it is equal or altered; avoid predicting the answer. Record tested sites, patient-reported change, cooperation and any declined/untested area. Root territories overlap and the illustrated blue area is not an exact S2–S5 border.
  5. Suspected CES requires the urgent local assessment/MRI pathway. Neither normal perineal findings nor the availability of this intimate examination should delay referral.

What to say to the patient

  1. Has the feeling changed when you wipe or around your genitals? If we need to examine sensation, I will explain each area and you can stop at any time.

A positive finding

New subjective or reproducible objective sensory change supports concern in context, including incomplete or patchy change.

A negative finding

Normal selected light-touch sites do not exclude CES or override new bladder, bowel or sexual symptoms.

Evidence & limitations

Inferior view explicitly labels anterior, posterior and patient sides. It is a simplified surface map, not a precise dermatome map or pinprick tutorial.

Points to remember

  1. Do not demand an objective deficit or a rectal examination before emergency referral for a compatible history.
