Precordial palpation: apex, heaves and thrills

Locate the apex impulse and feel for abnormal precordial movement.

How to do it

  1. Explain the examination, obtain consent and expose the precordium with appropriate draping.
  2. Use finger pads to locate the apex near the left fifth intercostal space at the midclavicular line; describe location and character.
  3. Feel along the left sternal edge for a sustained lift and over valve areas for a thrill.
  4. Relate findings to auscultation and the rest of the cardiovascular examination.

What to say to the patient

  1. May I feel the movement of your heart through your chest?
  2. Tell me if this is uncomfortable.

A positive finding

A displaced apex, sustained parasternal lift or thrill suggests cardiac pathology; body habitus and thoracic disease affect findings.

A negative finding

An impalpable apex or absent thrill does not exclude structural heart disease.

Evidence & limitations

These findings guide further evaluation rather than quantify chamber size or valve severity.

Points to remember

  1. The marked apex is an expected surface landmark, not the position in every patient.
  2. The diagram shows where to feel, not a simulated heave or thrill.
