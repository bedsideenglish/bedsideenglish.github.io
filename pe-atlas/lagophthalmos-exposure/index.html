Lagophthalmos · lid closure and corneal exposure

Separate incomplete eyelid closure, upward rotation and corneal surface injury from a facial motor-strength assessment.

See the examination

A · Complete gentle apposition example; observe spontaneous blinking and compare the other eye too.

B · Independent right-eye gap example. Measure the real gap when appropriate; arrows and drawing do not provide millimetres.

C · Separate upward-rotation example visible through incomplete closure; upper lid hides the iris. No forced opening is taught.

D · Separate open-eye cobalt-blue surface view: inferior punctate uptake is an example, not proof of exposure as the cause.

How to do it

  1. Explain observation and record acuity and ocular symptoms. Inspect resting lids and spontaneous blinking, comparing both eyes; ask about nocturnal symptoms, surgery, trauma and facial weakness.
  2. Ask the patient to close gently as for sleep. Observe actual lid apposition from a suitable angle without pushing the eye or forcing lids apart. Record the side, persistent gap and actual measured size when appropriate.
  3. If visible through incomplete closure, describe upward globe rotation (Bell’s phenomenon) separately. Do not provoke it by force; its presence varies and does not guarantee corneal protection or localise a lesion alone.
  4. Assess corneal clarity, redness, pain, photophobia and vision. If safe and indicated, use a separate fluorescein/slit-lamp examination for epithelial damage; inferior punctate uptake is compatible with exposure but has other causes.
  5. Document risk and arrange appropriate corneal protection and ophthalmic assessment. Pain, opacity, reduced vision or an exposed vulnerable cornea warrants prompt review; do not wait for a classic pattern.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please close your eyes gently as if sleeping. I will look for any gap without pressing on the eye.

A positive finding

Persistent incomplete apposition indicates lagophthalmos and risk of exposure; surface injury requires its own assessment.

A negative finding

Complete closure during one attempt or visible Bell’s phenomenon does not exclude incomplete blinking, nocturnal lagophthalmos or corneal disease.

Evidence & limitations

The gap example and upward-rotation example are independent. The iris is clipped behind the lid margin; no millimetre scale, blink frequency or treatment technique is simulated.

Points to remember

  1. Do not substitute a resisted forced-closure test for gentle apposition or infer a facial-palsy diagnosis from an exposed eye alone.
