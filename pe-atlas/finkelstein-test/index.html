Finkelstein test — open-finger variant

Assess familiar pain over the first dorsal wrist compartment (APL/EPB) using a gentle, explicitly defined Finkelstein variant.

See the examination

A · Forearm supported; fingers open, thumb free.

B · Small active ulnar deviation; stop for familiar radial pain.

C · Only if tolerated: gentle passive thumb flexion; no enclosing fist.

How to do it

  1. Explain the test, ask where the usual pain is and check for recent injury before provoking symptoms.
  2. Support the forearm at the table edge, thumb upward and the other fingers relaxed and open.
  3. Ask for a small active movement of the wrist toward the little-finger side; stop if the familiar pain occurs.
  4. If tolerated, gently hold the thumb and passively flex it toward the palm, as in the Wu/Elliott-described variant. Stop at the first familiar pain; do not force an endpoint.
  5. Return to neutral. Record the exact pain site and compare the opposite wrist if appropriate.

What to say to the patient

  1. Keep your fingers loose; do not make a fist around your thumb.
  2. Move your wrist a little toward your little finger.
  3. Tell me as soon as your usual pain appears.

A positive finding

Familiar pain at the radial styloid/first dorsal compartment supports de Quervain tenosynovitis in context; diffuse wrist pain is not the same finding.

A negative finding

A painless test does not exclude mild disease or another cause of radial wrist pain.

Evidence & limitations

Wu et al. studied 72 asymptomatic wrists: their variant caused fewer false positives than Eichhoff. That design did not establish sensitivity in patients with disease; the drawing does not prescribe a diagnostic angle.

Points to remember

  1. A thumb enclosed by the fingers followed by forced ulnar deviation is Eichhoff, not the variant shown here.
  2. Avoid repeated provocation, sudden traction and testing through severe pain or suspected fracture.
  3. The enlarged drawing shows surface relationships and examiner contact; tendon inflammation is not simulated.
