Valsalva maneuver and murmurs

The strain phase of the Valsalva maneuver reduces venous return and so lowers left ventricular volume. Most murmurs get softer, but the murmur of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) and the click-murmur of mitral valve prolapse (MVP) get louder or start earlier. It helps separate HCM from aortic stenosis at the bedside.

How to do it

  1. Sit the patient up at about 45 degrees. Find the murmur first with the diaphragm at the left sternal border or apex, and note its loudness at rest.
  2. Keep the stethoscope on the same spot. Ask the patient to take a breath and bear down hard, as if straining at stool, with the mouth closed, for 10 to 15 seconds.
  3. Listen DURING the strain phase and compare with rest. Do not judge the first beats after release.
  4. Ask the patient to relax and breathe normally; listen again as the murmur returns.
  5. Compare: louder = HCM or MVP pattern; softer = most other murmurs, including aortic stenosis.

What to say to the patient

  1. I'm going to listen while you do a breathing task. Take a breath in.
  2. Now bear down like you're straining on the toilet, and keep it up. Keep going... a little longer.
  3. And relax. Breathe normally. Thank you.

A positive finding

Murmur LOUDER (or the MVP click moves earlier and the murmur lengthens): HCM or MVP. A murmur that gets softer is more typical of aortic stenosis, mitral regurgitation, VSD and most other murmurs. Right-sided murmurs also soften in the strain phase and may return first on release.

A negative finding

No change. A murmur that does not change is not specific, and the maneuver is only useful if the patient strains well. A weak effort is the most common reason for a falsely reassuring test.

Evidence & limitations

In one series (Lembo et al.) a louder murmur on Valsalva strain was only moderately sensitive for HCM (roughly 60–70%) but highly specific (above about 90%). Treat this as a clue; echocardiography confirms the diagnosis. Values vary by study and examiner.

Points to remember

  1. Keep the stethoscope on the same spot; moving it changes loudness.
  2. Do not count the overshoot beats right after release; judge during the strain.
  3. Use caution in severe aortic stenosis, unstable angina, recent MI or heart failure. Do not ask for a maximal strain in a fragile patient.
