Radial pulse: rate and rhythm

Assess the peripheral pulse at the radial side of the wrist.

See the examination

Support the forearm; locate the radial pulse

Finger pads on the thumb side; count with a clock

Loading examination…

Drag to rotate freely

How to do it

  1. Support the relaxed forearm, palm upward.
  2. Place index and middle finger pads just proximal to the wrist crease on the thumb side; press gently.
  3. Count for a full minute if irregular; record rate, rhythm and pulse volume.
  4. Compare sides when indicated; obtain an ECG for a newly detected irregular rhythm.

What to say to the patient

  1. I am going to feel your pulse at your wrist.
  2. Please let your arm relax.

A positive finding

An irregular or unusually slow, fast or weak pulse needs clinical correlation; palpation cannot name an arrhythmia.

A negative finding

A regular radial pulse does not exclude intermittent arrhythmia or a pulse deficit.

Evidence & limitations

Use a timed count; assess rhythm with ECG when clinically indicated.

Points to remember

  1. Do not use your thumb or compress hard enough to obliterate the pulse.
  2. The figure shows contact and release; it does not display a pulse waveform.
