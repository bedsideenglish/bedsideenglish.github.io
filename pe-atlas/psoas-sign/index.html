Psoas sign

Looks for an inflamed structure lying on the iliopsoas muscle. A retrocecal appendix touching the right psoas hurts when the muscle is stretched by extending the hip, or when it contracts against resistance.

How to do it

  1. Ask the patient to lie on the LEFT side, knees slightly bent, so the right leg is free.
  2. Stand behind the patient and steady the pelvis with one hand on the right hip.
  3. With the other hand on the right thigh, slowly extend the right hip backward, keeping the knee straight.
  4. Ask about pain in the right lower quadrant or deep in the right flank; watch the face.
  5. Alternative (supine): put your hand on the right distal thigh and ask the patient to lift the thigh against your resistance; pain in the right lower quadrant is positive.

What to say to the patient

  1. I'd like to check something that can cause belly pain. Could you turn onto your left side?
  2. I'm going to move your right leg backward slowly. Please let it go loose.
  3. Do you feel any pain in your lower right belly or back?

A positive finding

Pain in the right lower quadrant or flank when the hip is extended. An inflamed retrocecal appendix, psoas abscess or retroperitoneal process becomes more likely. A positive sign is a clue, not a diagnosis.

A negative finding

No pain. Appendicitis is NOT excluded: a pelvic or ileal appendix does not touch the psoas, and most patients with appendicitis have a negative psoas sign.

Evidence & limitations

Sensitivity is low (about 16%) and specificity is fairly high (about 95%) in the studies pooled by Wagner et al., so a positive sign raises the probability somewhat while a negative sign changes little. Combine it with migration of pain, anorexia, rebound and fever, then image (CT or ultrasound) or call surgery as the setting requires.

Points to remember

  1. Do not force the hip into extension; stop at the first pain.
  2. The same pain can come from hip joint disease or lumbar spine problems, so ask where it hurts.
  3. Pair it with the obturator sign, Rovsing's sign and McBurney's point, and name them when you present.
