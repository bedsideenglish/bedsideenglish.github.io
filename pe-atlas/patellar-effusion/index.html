Knee effusion: bulge sign and patellar tap

Look and feel for intra-articular fluid, using two distinct techniques.

How to do it

  1. Supine, relax the quadriceps with the knee near extension. Compare both knees; identify the patella and adjacent recesses.
  2. Bulge test: sweep the medial recess proximally, lift and reposition, then stroke the lateral side distally. Watch for a returning bulge medially.
  3. Patellar tap is separate: gently maintain downward compression of the suprapatellar pouch and briefly press the patella backwards with the other hand. Feel for contact with the femur and release.
  4. Record which sign is present. Evaluate the clinical cause of a new effusion; a hot, painful joint requires timely assessment.

What to say to the patient

  1. Relax the thigh. I will gently sweep around the kneecap and then press it briefly.

A positive finding

A returning medial bulge or a ballotable patella supports an effusion, without identifying its cause.

A negative finding

Absent signs do not exclude a small effusion. The tap is less useful when the patella is not floating.

Evidence & limitations

The signs suit different amounts of fluid; neither is a quantitative volume measurement or a test that excludes septic arthritis.

Points to remember

  1. Do not confuse a superficial prepatellar bursal swelling with intra-articular fluid; avoid painful repeated bouncing.
  2. The right-knee diagram shows surface recesses and hand paths. No fluid shift, patellar displacement or positive result is animated.
