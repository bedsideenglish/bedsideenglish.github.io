Scrotal inspection · lie, asymmetry and skin

Inspect the scrotum and recognize concerning appearances without requiring a classic pattern before urgent assessment.

How to do it

  1. Confirm a specific indication. Explain the external and internal contacts separately, obtain consent, offer an appropriate chaperone, provide private undressing and draping, and agree on a stop signal. Recheck permission as the examination proceeds; stop for pain or withdrawal of consent. A learner needs supervised training.
  2. For acute pain, prioritize onset, severity and urgent torsion assessment. Inspect in the available comfortable position; stand only if safe and useful, and compare supine without delaying urgent care.
  3. Inspect both sides for asymmetry, swelling, skin changes and apparent testicular position/lie. Mild asymmetry may be usual; internal contours in the diagram are transparent projections.
  4. A high-riding or transverse testis with acute pain is concerning. Do not assume absence of either sign excludes torsion and do not teach detorsion by manipulating this illustration.
  5. Inspect skin and indicated surrounding perineum for erythema, edema, wounds or necrosis in context of pain and systemic illness. Escalate urgent concerns rather than finish a checklist; color alone is not diagnostic.

What to say to the patient

  1. I would like to look at the scrotum to assess the swelling and position. We can have a chaperone, and I will stop whenever you ask.

A positive finding

High or transverse lie, marked swelling, skin injury or necrosis may change urgency; they do not independently identify the cause.

A negative finding

Normal height, lie, skin or mild pain does not safely exclude testicular torsion or early invasive infection.

Evidence & limitations

Independent surface examples show high lie, transverse lie and skin change. Testes are shown transparently to explain position, not as directly visible organs.

Points to remember

  1. Never wait for the pictured pattern or delay an urgent surgical assessment to complete standing examination, reflex or imaging routines.
