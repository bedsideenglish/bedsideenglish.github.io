Temporal artery · branches, pulse and tenderness

Locate the superficial temporal artery and interpret tenderness, thickening and pulse changes in the clinical context of possible giant cell arteritis.

See the examination

A · The right superficial temporal trunk is anterior to the ear and branches above the zygomatic arch; branching is illustrative.

B · Finger pads lightly follow the artery; compare tenderness, thickening and pulse on both sides.

C · Unchanged normal-looking anatomy is retained: a normal pulse cannot remove urgency when new headache and visual symptoms suggest GCA.

How to do it

  1. Ask about new headache, scalp tenderness, jaw symptoms and visual change before touching. New visual symptoms with possible GCA need urgent specialist assessment; do not wait for an abnormal artery examination.
  2. Explain, obtain permission and seat the patient comfortably. Locate the superficial pulse just anterior to the tragus above the zygomatic arch with finger pads, not your thumb.
  3. Gently follow the frontal and parietal branches and compare sides, noting tenderness, thickening/nodularity, pulsation and asymmetry. Avoid repeated painful pressure.
  4. Document the actual findings alongside history, visual assessment and indicated inflammatory markers and specialist vascular testing/biopsy. Palpation alone neither confirms nor excludes GCA.

What to say to the patient

  1. May I gently feel the arteries at your temples? Tell me if an area is tender. Have you noticed any change in vision?

A positive finding

Tenderness, thickening, nodules or reduced pulsation raise concern in the appropriate history but are not specific for GCA.

A negative finding

Normal pulsation and a nontender artery do not exclude GCA or remove urgency from new visual symptoms.

Evidence & limitations

Right lateral view explicitly labels anterior to screen-left. Branching varies; red lines identify vessels, not histological inflammation.

Points to remember

  1. The figure does not teach ultrasound, biopsy or treatment; pursue the appropriate urgent pathway for a compatible presentation.
