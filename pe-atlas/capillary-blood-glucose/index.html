Capillary blood glucose · safe fingertip sampling

Obtain a point-of-care blood sample with a single-use safety lancet and recognise contamination, perfusion and device limitations. This is a sampling procedure.

See the examination

A · Wash and fully dry the sampling finger. Shown from the dorsal side so the nail and lateral pulp are distinct.

B · Safety-lancet tip meets the lateral pulp outside the nail; the contact is an enlarged placement example, not a depth scale.

C · Strip inlet contacts a freely forming drop. Meter screen contains no patient value; record the real value and units.

D · Clean gauze pressure and immediate sharps disposal; disinfect equipment and clean hands after removing gloves.

How to do it

  1. Confirm identity, explain and obtain consent as appropriate; in an emergency follow the local emergency protocol. Perform hand hygiene, wear new gloves and prepare a sharps container. Check meter, compatible unexpired strips and required quality control.
  2. Wash the patient’s hands with soap and water and dry completely. If local policy requires skin antisepsis, allow it to dry. Food/sugar, water and residual antiseptic can distort results.
  3. Use a single-use auto-disabling safety lancet on the lateral fleshy fingertip, avoiding the nail, damaged skin and the central pad. Select depth per the device. Never share lancing devices or reuse lancets.
  4. Allow a freely forming drop without hard squeezing. Follow the meter/local procedure on the first drop; bring the strip’s sample inlet to the drop and fill adequately, without smearing. Record the actual value, units and time.
  5. Dispose of the lancet immediately; apply clean gauze pressure. Clean/disinfect the meter as instructed, remove gloves and perform hand hygiene. Shared professional meters require disinfection after every patient.
  6. Assess unexpected/critical values immediately. Repeat with correct technique and obtain appropriate laboratory confirmation when indicated, especially with shock/poor perfusion or device interferences. Necessary emergency care must not wait for confirmation.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will take a small drop from the side of your fingertip with a new safety lancet, then press with gauze.

A positive finding

A low/high or clinically discordant result needs immediate interpretation with symptoms, sampling conditions and device limits.

A negative finding

A normal spot value does not exclude a previous episode or another cause of altered consciousness; a capillary meter alone is not a laboratory diabetes diagnosis.

Evidence & limitations

This procedure teaches specimen acquisition and infection prevention, not insulin dosing or a universal first-drop policy. Device instructions and clinical laboratory policy govern use.

Points to remember

  1. Do not use an OTC single-patient meter for shared professional testing, or add blood to an underfilled strip unless explicitly permitted.
