GCS motor response — graded pressure and separate examples

Use the structured GCS approach: check, observe, stimulate only as needed, then report each component and the best motor response.

See the examination

A · M6 example: obey a two-part command; a reflex grip is insufficient.

B · If needed: trained, graded trapezius pressure; observe and release promptly.

C · Separate M5 example: hand above the clavicle toward the head/neck stimulus.

D · Separate M4 example: rapid, variable flexion away from the body.

E · Separate M3 example: predominantly stereotyped flexion toward the chest.

F · Separate M2 example: arm extension; assess the actual whole response.

G · M1 only after adequate assessment with no limiting factor; otherwise record NT.

How to do it

  1. Prioritize resuscitation and check interference: sedation, paralysis, injury, language/hearing limitations and baseline neurological deficits.
  2. Observe both sides and use a clear two-part motor request such as squeeze then release. A reflex grip alone is not command-following.
  3. If needed, a trained clinician applies graded pressure using the structured protocol. Finger-tip pressure may assess eye opening/peripheral movement; central trapezius pressure helps distinguish localisation from flexion. This plate demonstrates the trapezius site only.
  4. Observe the best actual response and asymmetry, then promptly release. Localisation brings the hand above the clavicle toward a head/neck stimulus; normal flexion is distinguished from predominantly abnormal, stereotyped flexion.
  5. Report E, V and M separately, stimulus/site, asymmetry and confounders. Use NT for an untestable component rather than assigning 1 or an invented total.

What to say to the patient

  1. Can you squeeze my fingers and then let go?

A positive finding

Describe the observed motor category: M6 commands, M5 localisation, M4 normal flexion, M3 abnormal flexion, M2 extension or M1 no movement with no limiting factor. A lower response is a finding requiring context, not a lesion diagnosis.

A negative finding

Command-following does not exclude focal deficits, delirium or serious illness. Serial assessment and side-to-side comparison remain necessary.

Evidence & limitations

Uses the official six-level motor scale. Drawings are independent held examples, not a deteriorating patient, quantified pressure or simulation of response speed.

Points to remember

  1. Do not use a sternal rub; repeated excessive pressure can injure tissue. This is supervised clinical assessment, not a self-test.
  2. Posture alone cannot reliably classify normal versus abnormal flexion: speed, variability and the predominant response must be observed. Only upper-limb motor examples are drawn; eye and verbal components are assessed separately.
