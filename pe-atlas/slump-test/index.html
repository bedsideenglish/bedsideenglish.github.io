Seated slump — sequential loading and neck release

Assess familiar radiating symptoms and their modulation by changing neural loading; distinguish these from normal stretch responses.

See the examination

A · Supported sitting: establish the patient’s familiar symptom pattern.

B · Comfortable lumbar/thoracic slump and neck flexion; no forced head pressure.

C · Add knee extension, then tolerated ankle dorsiflexion.

D · Hold knee/ankle/trunk unchanged; release only the neck and compare symptoms.

How to do it

  1. Explain and record the usual symptom distribution. Seat safely with thighs supported and legs free; assess one side at a time.
  2. Ask for comfortable thoracic/lumbar slumping followed by neck flexion. Do not press the head downward or force a painful spine.
  3. Gradually extend the test knee, then add tolerated ankle dorsiflexion while maintaining the slumped trunk. Stop at familiar symptoms or the tolerated limit.
  4. Keep the knee, ankle and trunk unchanged and let the neck return toward neutral. Ask whether the same familiar symptom changes.
  5. Unload and return upright; compare sides as appropriate. Interpret with motor, reflex, sensory findings and history.

What to say to the patient

  1. Tell me if this brings on your usual leg symptoms. Keep the leg still while you gently lift your head.

A positive finding

Reproduction of familiar radiating symptoms that change with neck release supports neural mechanosensitivity in context; it does not alone prove a disc herniation or nerve-root compression.

A negative finding

A negative or equivocal response does not exclude radiculopathy. Normal subjects can feel posterior-thigh/calf stretch during this test.

Evidence & limitations

Sequence and interpretation vary across studies. This card explicitly uses knee extension then dorsiflexion; no angle threshold or diagnostic accuracy is assigned to that sequence.

Points to remember

  1. Do not call any stretch, back discomfort or neck-dependent change a positive test without matching the usual clinical complaint.
  2. The independent lumbar/thoracic silhouette is drawn in the plate; the rigid 3D mannequin shows seating only. Loading and neck-release panels retain identical distal joints.
