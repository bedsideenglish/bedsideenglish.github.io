Sustained handgrip and murmur response

Compare a murmur during resting, tolerable isometric effort and recovery as one part of a complete cardiac examination.

How to do it

  1. Explain the comparison; check that the patient can grip comfortably and is stable enough for exertion.
  2. Listen at the relevant site before the maneuver. Keep the chest piece and patient position unchanged for the comparison.
  3. Ask for a steady, tolerable squeeze around a soft roll or appropriate handgrip for about 20–30 seconds while breathing normally. Listen during sustained effort; use a real timer.
  4. Release and listen again. Stop for pain, dyspnea, dizziness or distress; do not demand maximal strain.
  5. Record the site and qualitative change alongside timing, radiation and other findings; investigate a suspected structural murmur appropriately.

What to say to the patient

  1. Keep breathing normally while you squeeze. Let go if you feel uncomfortable.

A positive finding

MR, AR and VSD murmurs often become louder with increased afterload; AS and obstructive HCM often soften. These are tendencies, not a stand-alone diagnosis.

A negative finding

No audible change does not exclude valve disease or HCM. Effort, acoustic conditions and mixed lesions affect the response.

Evidence & limitations

A qualitative dynamic-auscultation adjunct. This soft-roll illustration does not estimate maximal voluntary contraction, blood pressure or a diagnostic sound amplitude; playback is not the real hold time.

Points to remember

  1. Avoid breath-holding or straining that introduces a Valsalva response.
  2. The inset shows an apex listening example. Select the appropriate site from the separate auscultation-area card; no audio or force measurement is simulated.
