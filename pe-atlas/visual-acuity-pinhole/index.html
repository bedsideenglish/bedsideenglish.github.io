Monocular visual acuity and pinhole

Measure each eye’s central distance acuity with its correction recorded, then assess pinhole improvement when acuity is reduced. The drawn E chart is illustrative and cannot measure vision.

How to do it

  1. Use a calibrated chart at its specified distance, with appropriate lighting and distance correction. Ask about sudden loss, pain and usual vision; urgent eye assessment must not wait for completing the chart.
  2. Cover the left eye with an opaque occluder without pressing the globe; test the right eye. Prevent peeking and ask for the smallest line the patient can read, following the chart’s scoring rule.
  3. Record right-eye acuity, distance and correction; cover the right eye and repeat for the left. Do not report a binocular value as two monocular measurements.
  4. If reduced, keep the fellow eye covered, align the pinhole with the tested pupil and repeat using the same chart and distance. Record the result with PH and note no improvement when applicable.
  5. If the largest optotype cannot be seen, follow the low-vision protocol: document the actual distance for counting fingers, then hand motion and light perception as appropriate. These are not interchangeable acuity fractions.

What to say to the patient

  1. Keep this eye covered without pressing it. Read the smallest letters you can; we will test the other eye separately.

A positive finding

Reduced acuity that improves through a pinhole suggests an optical contribution, often refractive error. Residual loss and symptoms still require assessment.

A negative finding

No pinhole improvement does not identify a specific cause. Normal central acuity or improvement does not exclude field loss, retinal disease, glaucoma or another urgent disorder.

Evidence & limitations

Acuity depends on chart calibration, distance, lighting, correction and cooperation. Pinhole testing is not a refraction or complete eye examination.

Points to remember

  1. The opaque paddle covers the fellow eye; the tiny aperture belongs in front of the tested eye. Neither presses the eye.
