Lower-limb pinprick: selected L2–S1 key sensory points

Compare sharp/dull discrimination at selected standard lower-limb sensory points, then map any abnormal pattern. These are sample points, not dermatome boundaries.

How to do it

  1. Explain, obtain consent and demonstrate sharp versus dull on an area with known normal sensation. Use a disposable neurological sharp/dull tool and follow local infection-control practice.
  2. With the patient’s eyes closed, alternate sharp and dull gently and unpredictably without puncturing the skin. Ask whether it is sharp or dull and whether matched right/left points feel the same.
  3. This selected ASIA-point sequence is L2 anteromedial mid-thigh, L3 medial femoral condyle, L4 medial malleolus, L5 dorsum over the third MTP joint, and S1 lateral calcaneus. Locate the underlying landmarks before contact.
  4. If abnormal, repeat and map from reduced toward intact sensation; extend beyond these points. Record the modality and distribution, then correlate with strength and reflexes.

What to say to the patient

  1. Close your eyes. Does this feel sharp or dull? Does it feel the same on both sides?
  2. I will touch lightly without breaking the skin. Please tell me if anything hurts.

A positive finding

A reproducible dermatomal, peripheral-nerve, stocking or sensory-level pattern helps localisation. A single abnormal point does not establish a root lesion; confirm the distribution with other neurological findings.

A negative finding

Normal discrimination at these selected points does not exclude a sensory deficit elsewhere or radiculopathy. Dermatomes overlap and individual maps vary.

Evidence & limitations

Point placement follows the ASIA key-sensory-point document. This abbreviated teaching sequence is not a complete ISNCSCI examination and assigns no AIS grade or diagnostic accuracy.

Points to remember

  1. Do not reuse a sharp tip, puncture skin, use a visibly predictable sequence, or substitute pressure intensity for sharp/dull discrimination.
  2. L5 here is the ASIA third-MTP point, not the often-taught hallux point; S1 is the lateral calcaneus, not an arbitrary lateral-toe site. The dots do not outline full dermatomes.
