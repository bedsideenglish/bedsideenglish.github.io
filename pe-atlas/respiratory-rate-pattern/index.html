Respiratory rate · cycle, rhythm and effort

Count actual spontaneous breaths and describe depth, rhythm, symmetry and effort, with awareness and oxygenation assessed alongside.

See the examination

A · Inspiration schematic: observe chest and abdomen without prompting a deep breath. Arrows show directions, not a measured excursion.

B · Expiration completes the same breath. Do not count inspiration and expiration as two breaths.

C · One complete rise and fall is one breath. Use an actual 60-second timer; this replay is not a counting exercise.

D · Independent shallow and irregular examples. Curves have no time/volume scale; document rate, depth, rhythm and effort separately.

How to do it

  1. Explain general observation, maintain privacy and let the patient remain in the most comfortable safe position. Assess airway and urgent distress before routine counting.
  2. Observe spontaneous chest and abdominal movement without asking for deep or paced breaths. One complete inspiration and expiration is one breath; avoid counting the two phases separately.
  3. Use a real clock to count for a full 60 seconds for this teaching method; note interruptions, pauses and irregularity rather than extrapolating a brief glance. Record breaths/min and the clinical conditions.
  4. Describe depth, rhythm, symmetry, accessory-muscle use, speech and awareness. Compare sequential real observations in context; a rapidly falling rate in an exhausted patient can be deterioration.
  5. Assess SpO2 and oxygen support separately. Normal saturation, particularly with supplemental oxygen, does not establish adequate ventilation; assess CO2 when indicated. Apnoea or severe distress requires immediate response, without waiting out a minute.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please stay comfortable and breathe as you normally do while I observe your breathing.

A positive finding

Abnormal rate, pauses, increasing effort or altered awareness need prompt assessment; the pattern suggests physiology, not a unique diagnosis.

A negative finding

A usual-looking rate or normal SpO2 does not exclude early respiratory failure, fatigue or hypercapnia.

Evidence & limitations

Held inspiration/expiration drawings explain the counting unit. The pattern traces are conceptual, with no time, volume or diagnostic cutoffs; playback cannot be used to measure respiratory rate.

Points to remember

  1. Do not equate slowing with improvement without considering effort and awareness; do not let counting delay an urgent ABCDE assessment.
