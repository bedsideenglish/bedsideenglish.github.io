Pulsus paradoxus: manual cuff measurement

Measure an exaggerated inspiratory fall in systolic pressure by comparing two cuff-pressure thresholds. This is not the ordinary systolic–diastolic pulse-pressure difference.

See the examination

Manual cuff + brachial stethoscope. Observe normal spontaneous respiration.

P₁: first sounds in EXPIRATION ONLY. Example pressure 120 mmHg.

P₂: sounds in BOTH inspiration and expiration. Example pressure 104 mmHg.

Subtract the two systolic thresholds: 120 − 104 = 16 mmHg; not pulse pressure.

How to do it

  1. Assess the patient’s condition first. Explain and position comfortably with a supported arm and correctly sized manual cuff. Observe quiet spontaneous breathing.
  2. Locate the brachial artery and position the stethoscope distal to the cuff. Inflate above systolic pressure until Korotkoff sounds disappear.
  3. Deflate very slowly while observing respiration over several cycles. Record P₁ when sounds first occur only during expiration.
  4. Continue slow deflation. Record P₂ at which beats are audible throughout inspiration AND expiration. Calculate P₁ minus P₂ in mmHg.
  5. Repeat if breathing or sounds were uncertain; document the method and clinical context. Do not delay urgent assessment in a haemodynamically unstable patient.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please breathe normally; do not take deliberately deep breaths or hold your breath.
  2. The cuff will feel tight while I listen over several breaths.

A positive finding

A difference greater than 10 mmHg is conventionally abnormal during spontaneous inspiration. It may occur in tamponade or severe obstructive lung disease and is not specific to either.

A negative finding

A small difference does not independently exclude tamponade or serious disease. Clinical state, rhythm and ventilation conditions influence assessment.

Evidence & limitations

The two displayed pressures, 120 and 104 mmHg, are a worked example, yielding 16 mmHg. Beat bars only encode audibility; they are not arterial waveforms or real-time measurements.

Points to remember

  1. Deep coached breaths, rapid cuff deflation, arrhythmia and noisy breathing make thresholds unreliable.
  2. Ordinary automatic BP readings do not provide this measurement. Positive-pressure ventilation has different physiology; do not apply the spontaneous-breathing diagram unchanged.
