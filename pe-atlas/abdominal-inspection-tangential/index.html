Abdominal inspection — low viewpoint and tangential light

Supplement the general abdominal inspection with a low line of sight and oblique light to appreciate surface contour.

See the examination

A · Eye at abdominal-wall level: observe the contour without touching.

B · Separate lighting aid: shallow-angle illumination reveals surface shadows.

How to do it

  1. Explain; obtain permission to expose the abdominal wall while covering chest and groin.
  2. Position supine with head/knees supported as needed and arms relaxed beside the body. Inspect from above first.
  3. Lower your eye to approximately abdominal-wall level and look across the contour without touching or compressing it.
  4. Add shallow-angle light from the side when useful. Inspect localized versus generalized fullness, scars, skin change and visible respiratory or pulsatile movement.
  5. Record what is actually seen and integrate with the rest of the examination; an expansile pulsatile mass or acute symptoms needs appropriate prompt evaluation.

What to say to the patient

  1. Let your arms rest beside you and breathe normally while I look across your abdomen.

A positive finding

A contour asymmetry or visible movement is a descriptive finding. The view does not distinguish ascites, organ enlargement, obstruction or vascular disease by itself.

A negative finding

An unremarkable surface does not exclude abdominal disease. Habitual contour, obesity, posture and lighting affect visibility.

Evidence & limitations

Original view/lighting diagram, not a pathological response simulation. No mass, peristaltic wave or pulsation is animated.

Points to remember

  1. A tangential viewing direction and tangential illumination are complementary but different adjustments.
  2. This supplements the existing general-inspection card; skin palpation and interpretation of a pulsatile mass are not demonstrated.
