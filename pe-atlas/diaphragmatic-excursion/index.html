Diaphragmatic excursion by percussion

Estimate the change in the posterior lower lung border between full expiration and full inspiration, and compare sides. This is an indirect bedside estimate, not direct diaphragm imaging.

How to do it

  1. Seat the patient and expose the back with consent. Ask them to cross their arms to move the scapulae laterally; first confirm resonant lung above the expected base.
  2. Ask for a full breath out, then a brief hold only if comfortable. Percuss downward in interspaces along one scapular line until resonance becomes dull; mark that level E.
  3. Let the patient breathe normally and rest before repeating.
  4. Ask for a full breath in and a brief comfortable hold. Restart above the first border and percuss down the same line to dullness; mark this level I, normally below E.
  5. Release the breath hold promptly. Measure the vertical E–I distance in centimetres, repeat on the other side, and document the respiratory endpoints and any limitation.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please cross your arms in front of you.
  2. Breathe all the way out, then hold only while comfortable. Now breathe normally.
  3. After a rest, take a deep breath in and hold briefly. You can breathe again now.

A positive finding

A reproducibly reduced or asymmetric excursion prompts assessment for causes such as pleural effusion, atelectasis, hyperinflation or diaphragmatic dysfunction. Percussion alone cannot distinguish them.

A negative finding

A symmetric excursion does not exclude lung or diaphragm disease. Chest-wall thickness, effort and difficulty hearing the border can make the estimate unreliable.

Evidence & limitations

Reference ranges depend on the respiratory endpoints and technique. Record the actual measurement rather than treating the illustrated 4-cm separation as a universal normal cutoff.

Points to remember

  1. Do not prolong breath holding or perform maximal maneuvers in a distressed patient.
  2. The diagram teaches two separate searches, with a rest and restart. E and I are hypothetical borders; no percussion sound, measuring tape or actual diaphragm is simulated.
