Valgus and varus stress of the knee

Compare collateral-ligament pain, laxity and endpoint at slight flexion and near extension.

See the examination

Knee about 30°; support the distal thigh and ankle

Valgus: knee medially, ankle laterally; assess the medial side

Release the sideways load

Varus: knee laterally, ankle medially; assess the lateral side

Release before changing the knee angle

Repeat gently near extension; reassess the endpoint

How to do it

  1. Supine, first examine the uninjured knee. Support the test leg with the knee around 30 degrees and the muscles relaxed.
  2. Valgus: place the proximal hand at the lateral knee/distal femur and the distal hand at the medial ankle. Apply gentle opposite forces: knee medially, ankle laterally. Assess medial pain, opening and endpoint.
  3. Release fully and reverse the hand contacts for varus: medial knee and lateral ankle, pushing knee laterally and ankle medially. Assess the lateral side.
  4. Release before changing angle. Repeat carefully near full extension if tolerated; broader instability here raises concern for additional structures. Record pain separately from laxity and compare both sides.

What to say to the patient

  1. Let your leg relax while I support it. Tell me if this causes pain; I will stop.

A positive finding

Asymmetric opening or a soft endpoint supports collateral injury; pain without laxity is a different finding. Instability near extension suggests a more extensive injury.

A negative finding

A stable test does not exclude a partial injury, especially when pain or guarding prevents an adequate examination.

Evidence & limitations

Interpret with the mechanism, examination and imaging when indicated; no millimetre grade is inferred from this schematic.

Points to remember

  1. The mannequin uses enclosing support grips; the hands shift to the opposite borders after release. The text specifies the clinical side contacts.
  2. Arrows show the force couple. Coronal gapping and ligament strain are not modelled; hip rotation must not substitute for valgus/varus.
