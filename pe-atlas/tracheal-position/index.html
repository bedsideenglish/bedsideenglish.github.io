Tracheal position at the suprasternal notch

Compare the spaces beside the lower cervical trachea with the head and chest aligned; this is a midline assessment, not carotid palpation.

How to do it

  1. Explain and obtain consent. Sit the patient comfortably, expose only the lower neck and upper chest, and align the head with the sternum.
  2. Find the suprasternal notch and the tracheal rings just above it. Identify the medial borders of the sternocleidomastoid muscles.
  3. From behind the patient, gently feel the gap between one lateral tracheal wall and the adjacent SCM with a fingertip. Release, then compare the opposite gap.
  4. Record midline or the side of convincing displacement. Correlate with respiratory symptoms, chest findings and imaging when indicated.

What to say to the patient

  1. May I gently feel the front of your lower neck? Keep your head facing straight ahead.
  2. Tell me if this feels uncomfortable; I will compare one side and then the other.

A positive finding

Unequal gaps with reproducible tracheal displacement suggest mediastinal shift. Volume loss can pull toward the affected side; a large pressure or space-occupying process can push away. The sign alone does not establish a cause.

A negative finding

A central trachea does not exclude pneumothorax, pleural disease or another serious respiratory problem. In an unstable patient, urgent assessment must not wait for this sign.

Evidence & limitations

This is a qualitative surface examination; no universal normal gap in centimetres or stand-alone diagnostic accuracy is assigned.

Points to remember

  1. Head rotation, unequal shoulder position and chest deformity can create apparent asymmetry; realign before interpreting.
  2. Use light contact without squeezing the airway or both sides of the neck. The diagram projects the anterior anatomy while the examiner may stand behind.
  3. The drawing shows normal reference anatomy and sequential gap contacts; it does not simulate a displaced airway or measure its distance.
