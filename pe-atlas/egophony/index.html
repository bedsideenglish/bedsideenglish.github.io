Egophony: E-to-A voice transmission

Assess altered voice transmission over a suspected area of consolidation.

How to do it

  1. Identify an abnormal area during routine chest examination.
  2. Listen with the diaphragm on bare skin while the patient sustains an ee sound.
  3. Compare with the same level on the other side and nearby normal lung.
  4. Document an E-to-A change and assess it with the rest of the respiratory findings.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please keep saying eee while I listen.
  2. Now the same sound on the other side.

A positive finding

A nasal A-like sound instead of E supports altered transmission, often with consolidation or just above a pleural effusion.

A negative finding

Absent egophony does not rule out pneumonia or an effusion.

Evidence & limitations

It is an adjunctive sign, not a substitute for clinical assessment or indicated imaging.

Points to remember

  1. The patient says E, not A; the change is what the examiner hears.
  2. The illustrated sites are examples, not fixed diagnostic points. This card does not synthesize the sound.
