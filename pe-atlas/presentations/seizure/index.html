Prioritize the patient's condition. If convulsions continue, seizures recur without recovery of consciousness, head trauma is present, or respiratory depression or cyanosis is observed, stop further teaching examination immediately and provide urgent clinical care, including airway protection and emergency assessment.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Measure vital signs, temperature, and blood glucose

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Capillary blood glucose · safe fingerstick sampling ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Level of consciousness and neurologic examination: pupils, focal weakness, and reflexes

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Pupillary light reflex and swinging flashlight test (RAPD) ↗
    • Plantar response: flexor and extensor (Babinski) ↗
    • Pronator drift test ↗
  3. 03

    Assess for neck stiffness

    • Neck stiffness: gentle passive neck flexion ↗
  4. 04

    Inspect the sides of the tongue for bite marks and look for head or body injuries

    • Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Assess pulse regularity and auscultate the heart to help distinguish convulsive syncope

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
