Prioritize the patient's condition. Alarm symptoms such as dysphagia, weight loss, or signs of bleeding require further assessment. With acute chest pain, shortness of breath, or syncope, do not assume indigestion; prioritize urgent assessment.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check vital signs, including blood pressure and pulse

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  2. 02

    Check for conjunctival pallor suggesting anemia, and scleral icterus

    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
  3. 03

    Assess epigastric tenderness and palpable masses

    • Abdominal inspection ↗
    • Abdominal auscultation: bowel sounds ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Carnett sign ↗
  4. 04

    Right upper quadrant palpation and Murphy sign

    • Murphy sign ↗
  5. 05

    Palpate the left supraclavicular lymph nodes

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
