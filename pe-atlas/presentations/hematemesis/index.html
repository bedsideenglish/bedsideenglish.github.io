Prioritize the patient's condition. Major hematemesis or hemodynamic instability carries a high risk of airway obstruction and hypovolemic shock. Avoid forcing orthostatic testing and proceed to immediate emergency care, airway protection, and assessment for hemodynamic stabilization.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Assess blood pressure, pulse, and orthostatic changes for the extent of blood loss

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Orthostatic blood pressure and pulse measurement ↗
  2. 02

    Check for conjunctival pallor

    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
  3. 03

    Check the mouth and nose for bleeding sites

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Anterior rhinoscopy: nasal vestibule and visible landmarks ↗
  4. 04

    Signs of chronic liver disease, including jaundice, spider angiomas, and palmar erythema

    • Asterixis ↗
    • Inspect for skin signs of chronic liver disease: jaundice, spider angiomas, and palmar erythema Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Assess epigastric tenderness, hepatosplenomegaly, and ascites using shifting dullness

    • Abdominal inspection ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Shifting dullness ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
    • Splenic palpation in the right lateral position ↗
  6. 06

    Perform a digital rectal examination for melena if indicated

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: rectal wall and prostate ↗
    • Assess stool for features of melena Technique card pending
