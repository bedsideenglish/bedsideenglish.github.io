Physical examination checklistReferences
← Clinical presentations

Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent assessment for sudden severe headache, fever with neck stiffness, new neurologic findings, or altered consciousness. A normal examination is not reassuring on its own when the headache is new or has changed, or when risk factors such as cancer or immunosuppression are present.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Measure blood pressure and temperature

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess for meningeal signs, including neck stiffness

    • Neck stiffness: gentle passive neck flexion ↗
  3. 03

    Neurologic examination: cranial nerves, limb strength and sensation, reflexes, finger-to-nose testing, and gait

    • Pronator drift test ↗
    • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic positions of gaze ↗
    • Upper limb strength: shoulder and elbow ↗
    • Lower limb strength testing (nerve roots L2–S1) ↗
    • Light touch sensation: comparison of both sides ↗
    • Patellar reflex (L2–L4, primarily L4) ↗
    • Finger-to-nose test ↗
    • Gait and tandem gait ↗
    • Comprehensive neurologic assessment tailored to the symptoms Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Fundoscopy for papilledema, pupillary reflexes, and visual fields

    • Pupillary light reflex and swinging flashlight test (RAPD) ↗
    • Confrontation visual fields: four quadrants, one eye at a time ↗
    • Direct ophthalmoscopy: approach and viewing the fundus ↗
  5. 05

    Check for tenderness over the temporal arteries, sinuses, and neck or shoulder muscles

    • Frontal and maxillary sinus tenderness ↗
    • Temporal artery: branches, pulse, and tenderness ↗
