Prioritize the patient's condition. A persistent lump, new skin dimpling, or bloody nipple discharge requires follow-up even if palpation is reassuring. Explain why examination is needed and respect consent, privacy, choice about a chaperone, and requests to stop.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    With the patient seated, inspect and compare both breasts with the arms at the sides, raised, and with hands on the hips

    • Clinical breast examination — inspection and systematic palpation ↗
  2. 02

    With the patient supine, palpate the entirety of both breasts, assessing mass size, consistency, borders, mobility, and tenderness

    • Clinical breast examination — inspection and systematic palpation ↗
  3. 03

    Inspect the nipples and assess discharge

    • Clinical breast examination — inspection and systematic palpation ↗
  4. 04

    Palpate the axillary and supraclavicular lymph nodes

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Axillary lymph nodes: arm support and palpation by group ↗
  5. 05

    Measure temperature if mastitis or an abscess is suspected

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    For male breast enlargement, palpate the testes and assess for signs of liver disease

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
    • Testicular and epididymal palpation ↗
