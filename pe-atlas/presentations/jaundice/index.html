Prioritize the patient's condition. Acute cholangitis with fever or chills, jaundice, and right upper quadrant pain (Charcot triad), or acute liver failure with hepatic encephalopathy, can deteriorate rapidly with septic shock or cerebral edema. Prioritize immediate emergency care and specialist assessment over completing the examination checklist.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check temperature, blood pressure, and pulse to assess for cholangitis or sepsis

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    In good lighting, check for scleral icterus and conjunctival pallor

    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
  3. 03

    Spider angiomas, palmar erythema, scratch marks, and asterixis

    • Asterixis ↗
    • Inspect for skin signs of chronic liver disease, including spider angiomas, palmar erythema, and scratch marks from itching Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Assess right upper quadrant tenderness, Murphy sign, liver and spleen size, and a palpable gallbladder

    • Abdominal inspection ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
    • Murphy sign ↗
    • Splenic palpation in the right lateral position ↗
  5. 05

    Assess for ascites using shifting dullness

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Shifting dullness ↗
