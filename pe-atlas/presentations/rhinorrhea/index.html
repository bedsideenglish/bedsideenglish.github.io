Prioritize the patient's condition. Periorbital swelling, decreased vision, double vision, or an extreme headache with neck stiffness may indicate orbital cellulitis or an intracranial infectious complication of sinusitis and requires immediate emergency evaluation.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check temperature

    • Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Use a nasal speculum to assess mucosal color and appearance, including pallor, edema, or redness; secretions; polyps; and the septum

    • Anterior rhinoscopy: nasal vestibule and visible landmarks ↗
  3. 03

    Palpate the cheeks and forehead for sinus tenderness

    • Frontal and maxillary sinus tenderness ↗
  4. 04

    Look for postnasal drip and signs of tonsillar or adenoidal enlargement

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
  5. 05

    Assess periorbital swelling, eye movements, and vision

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic positions of gaze ↗
    • Visual acuity and pinhole testing, one eye at a time ↗
    • Inspect the orbits, lacrimal sacs, and areas around the eyes Technique card pending
