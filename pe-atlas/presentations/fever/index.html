Prioritize the patient's condition. With fever and decreased consciousness, shortness of breath, circulatory instability, or rapidly worsening overall condition, prioritize urgent assessment and calling for help over a detailed examination. The absence of clear focal findings does not rule out serious infection.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Assess temperature, blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation for signs of sepsis

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Respiratory rate, rhythm, and effort ↗
    • Pulse oximetry: placement, signal quality, and limitations ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Examine the pharynx, tympanic membranes, and cervical lymph nodes

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Adult otoscopy: safe approach and tympanic membrane landmarks ↗
    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
  3. 03

    Auscultate the lungs and check for a heart murmur

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Posterior lung auscultation sequence ↗
  4. 04

    Assess abdominal tenderness, hepatosplenomegaly, and costovertebral angle tenderness

    • Costovertebral angle tenderness by fist percussion ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
    • Splenic palpation in the right lateral position ↗
  5. 05

    Check for rash, eschars, petechiae, and wounds

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Meningeal signs, including neck stiffness

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Neck stiffness: gentle passive neck flexion ↗
