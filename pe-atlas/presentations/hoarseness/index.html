Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent evaluation of a possible upper airway problem if inspiratory noise or breathing difficulty is present, and do not force an oral examination. Persistent or worsening voice changes need further evaluation even if examination findings are subtle.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check respiratory rate and oxygen saturation and assess for inspiratory stridor

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Work of breathing, accessory muscle use, and tripod positioning ↗
    • Respiratory rate, rhythm, and effort ↗
    • Pulse oximetry: placement, signal quality, and limitations ↗
  2. 02

    Listen to the voice and assess maximum phonation time

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Inspect the mouth and pharynx

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
  4. 04

    Palpate cervical lymph nodes and the thyroid

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
  5. 05

    Explain that the vocal cords need to be visualized with laryngoscopy or an indirect laryngeal mirror examination

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Assess lower cranial nerve function, including palatal elevation and tongue movement

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Hypoglossal nerve: tongue at rest, protrusion, and side-to-side movement ↗
