Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent assessment for sudden or severe scrotal pain because of possible testicular torsion. A single reassuring finding or waiting for ultrasound must not delay specialist assessment.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check temperature, blood pressure, and pulse for fever or signs of sepsis

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Inspect the scrotum while standing and supine for asymmetry, testicular position, and skin color

    • Scrotal inspection: position, asymmetry, and skin ↗
  3. 03

    Starting on the unaffected side, palpate the testis and epididymis for the site of tenderness, a high-riding or transverse testis, and hard masses

    • Testicular and epididymal palpation ↗
  4. 04

    Stroke the inner thigh to assess the cremasteric reflex

    • Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Assess transillumination of the scrotal swelling

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    With the patient standing and straining, assess for varicocele or hernia; palpate the inguinal lymph nodes

    • Inguinal hernia examination: surface assessment ↗
    • Valsalva assessment for varicocele and inguinal lymph node palpation Technique card pending

The examination checklists are based on the existing CPX material used in the Android app and PWA. Each Atlas card provides the technique's steps, findings, precautions, and existing references.
