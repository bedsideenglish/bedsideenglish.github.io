Prioritize the patient's condition. Mastoid swelling or tenderness, facial weakness, or a severe headache may indicate an intracranial complication of otitis media and requires immediate specialist ENT assessment.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check temperature

    • Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess tenderness with pinna traction and tragal pressure; inspect and palpate the mastoid area for redness, swelling, or tenderness

    • External ear and mastoid · inspection and gentle touch ↗
  3. 03

    Use an otoscope to assess ear canal swelling, discharge, or foreign bodies, and tympanic membrane redness, bulging, or perforation

    • Adult otoscopy: safe approach and tympanic membrane landmarks ↗
  4. 04

    Assess facial nerve function with forehead wrinkling, eye closure, and smiling

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Facial movements: comparison of the upper and lower face ↗
  5. 05

    Use Weber and Rinne tuning fork tests to assess the pattern of hearing loss

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Weber test — sound lateralization ↗
    • Rinne test — comparing air and bone conduction ↗
  6. 06

    Examine the temporomandibular joints, teeth and mouth, pharynx, and cervical lymph nodes

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Examine the temporomandibular joints and teeth Technique card pending
