Prioritize the patient's condition. For findings suggesting cauda equina syndrome, such as saddle sensory loss, bowel or bladder dysfunction, or progressive leg weakness, or red flags such as fever or a history of cancer, avoid forceful spinal manipulation and proceed to immediate emergency assessment.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check vital signs, including temperature

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Inspect spinal alignment and assess the spinous processes and paraspinal muscles for tenderness to palpation or percussion

    • Midline spinal and paraspinal tenderness ↗
  3. 03

    Lumbar range of motion: flexion, extension, and side bending

    • Modified Schober test: 10 cm above and 5 cm below ↗
    • Lumbar extension and lateral flexion range of motion Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Use the straight leg raise to assess for nerve root irritation

    • Straight leg raise, including the Bragard maneuver ↗
  5. 05

    Lower limb strength and sensation, and knee and ankle reflexes

    • Lower limb strength testing (nerve roots L2–S1) ↗
    • Patellar reflex (L2–L4, primarily L4) ↗
    • Lower limb pinprick: key sensory points for L2–S1 ↗
    • Achilles reflex (primarily S1) ↗
    • Light touch sensation: comparison of both sides ↗
  6. 06

    If cauda equina syndrome is suspected, assess perineal sensation and anal sphincter tone

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Saddle sensation: selective light touch assessment of the perineum ↗
    • Anal sphincter tone: resting resistance and voluntary squeeze ↗
  7. 07

    Assess for a pulsatile abdominal mass and costovertebral angle tenderness

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Costovertebral angle tenderness by fist percussion ↗
    • Palpate for a pulsatile abdominal mass as a screen for abdominal aortic aneurysm Technique card pending
    • Assessment for a pulsatile abdominal mass Technique card pending
