Prioritize the patient's condition. Changes in a pigmented lesion's shape, border, or color, growth, ulceration, or bleeding require further assessment. Do not determine benignity or malignancy from appearance or a single criterion alone, and do not squeeze or repeatedly irritate a mass.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Inspect lesion location, size, color, shape, and surface, including ulceration, crusting, or telangiectasia

    • Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess pigmented lesions for asymmetry, border, color, diameter, and evolution (ABCDE)

    • Pigmented lesions: ABCDE features and documenting change ↗
    • Dermoscopy Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Palpate for consistency, tenderness, fluctuance, mobility, and fixation to underlying tissue

    • Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Assess transillumination with a light if indicated

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Palpate regional lymph nodes

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Axillary lymph nodes: arm support and palpation by group ↗
    • Palpate inguinal lymph nodes Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Examine the skin elsewhere on the body

    • Technique card pending

References

The examination checklists are based on the existing CPX material used in the Android app and PWA. Each Atlas card provides the technique's steps, findings, precautions, and existing references.

  • GMC · Intimate examinations and chaperones — Explanation of the indication and nature of an intimate examination, consent, privacy and draping, offering a chaperone, and the right to stop. This source does not validate every clinical indication for each examination.
  • DermNet · Examination of the skin — Assessment of skin lesion morphology and distribution, surrounding skin, and related areas. This source does not establish a definitive diagnosis of an individual mass or pigmented lesion.
