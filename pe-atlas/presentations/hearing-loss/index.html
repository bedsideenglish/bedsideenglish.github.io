Prioritize the patient's condition. Sudden hearing loss requires prompt specialist assessment even if otoscopy or tuning fork testing is normal. Prioritize urgent evaluation if neurologic symptoms or severe dizziness are also present.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Use an otoscope to check for cerumen, tympanic membrane perforation, and middle ear effusion, including bubbles or an air-fluid level

    • Adult otoscopy: safe approach and tympanic membrane landmarks ↗
  2. 02

    Screen hearing with the whispered voice test

    • Whispered voice test — one ear at a time ↗
  3. 03

    Use Weber and Rinne tuning fork tests to help distinguish conductive from sensorineural hearing loss

    • Weber test — sound lateralization ↗
    • Rinne test — comparing air and bone conduction ↗
  4. 04

    Assess facial sensation, facial nerve function, and cerebellar function

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Finger-to-nose test ↗
    • Facial movements: comparison of the upper and lower face ↗
    • Trigeminal nerve · light facial touch and chewing movements ↗
  5. 05

    If dizziness is present, assess nystagmus and perform the Romberg test

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Romberg test ↗
    • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic positions of gaze ↗
