Prioritize the patient's condition. Joint symptoms with fever, or a rapidly red and swollen single joint with pain so severe that movement is not possible, raise concern for septic arthritis, which can cause permanent joint damage. Arrange urgent clinical assessment without delay.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check vital signs, including temperature

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Compare both joints for swelling, redness, and deformity

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Palpate for warmth, tenderness, and joint effusion

    • Knee effusion: bulge sign and patellar ballottement ↗
  4. 04

    Active and passive range of motion, and pain with movement

    • Elbow inspection and active range of motion ↗
    • Lachman test ↗
    • McMurray test with supported tibial rotation ↗
    • Knee valgus and varus stress tests ↗
    • Patrick test (FABER) ↗
    • Active and passive range of motion of the affected joints Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Check other joints, nails, skin rash, and tophi

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Observe gait when a lower limb joint is affected

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Gait and tandem gait ↗
