Prioritize the patient's condition. With hemodynamic instability, sudden severe pain, or clear signs of peritoneal irritation, avoid repeated provocative maneuvers and arrange immediate emergency assessment. A normal examination does not rule out life-threatening conditions such as acute appendicitis or mesenteric ischemia.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check blood pressure, pulse, and temperature for signs of shock or fever

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
    • Possibility of pregnancy and pregnancy testing Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Inspect the abdomen for distention and surgical scars, and auscultate bowel sounds

    • Abdominal inspection ↗
    • Abdominal auscultation: bowel sounds ↗
  3. 03

    Starting away from the pain, palpate lightly and then deeply to locate tenderness

    • Abdominal percussion: tympany and dullness ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
  4. 04

    Assess for signs of peritoneal irritation, including rebound tenderness and rigidity

    • Rebound tenderness (Blumberg sign) ↗
    • Peritoneal irritation: coughing and gentle percussion ↗
  5. 05

    Perform targeted maneuvers, such as Murphy sign or McBurney point tenderness assessment, based on the suspected site

    • McBurney point tenderness ↗
    • Murphy sign ↗
    • Rovsing sign ↗
    • Psoas sign ↗
  6. 06

    Check for costovertebral angle tenderness on both sides

    • Costovertebral angle tenderness by fist percussion ↗
  7. 07

    Explain that a pelvic or digital rectal examination may be needed if clinically indicated

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: rectal wall and prostate ↗
    • Speculum examination: vaginal approach and cervical view ↗
    • Bimanual pelvic examination: cervix, uterus, and adnexa ↗
