Prioritize the patient's condition. In patients with oropharyngeal dysphagia, decreased consciousness, shortness of breath, or severe choking, an unsafe water swallow challenge risks acute airway obstruction and aspiration. Avoid oral provocation and arrange immediate clinical assessment.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Assess vital signs, weight, nutritional status, and hydration

    • Fluid status: oral mucosa and skin turgor ↗
    • Measure weight and assess nutritional status Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Inspect the mouth, including teeth, white coating, and tongue movements

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
  3. 03

    Palpate cervical and supraclavicular lymph nodes and the thyroid

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
  4. 04

    Assess cranial nerve function, including palatal elevation, tongue movements, speech, and ptosis

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Hypoglossal nerve: tongue at rest, protrusion, and side-to-side movement ↗
    • Assess speech articulation and ptosis Technique card pending
  5. 05

    When safe and clinically indicated, assess a small water swallow for coughing or voice change

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Palpate for an upper abdominal mass or hepatomegaly

    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
