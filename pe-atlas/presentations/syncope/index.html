Prioritize the patient's condition. A history of heart disease, syncope during exertion or while supine, severe palpitations or chest pain, or a family history of sudden death suggests high-risk cardiac syncope. Stop further teaching examinations and arrange urgent cardiovascular assessment even if some findings appear reassuring.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Measure supine and standing blood pressure and pulse to assess for orthostatic hypotension

    • Orthostatic blood pressure and pulse measurement ↗
  2. 02

    Assess pulse rate and regularity, and compare blood pressure in both arms

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  3. 03

    Auscultate the heart for murmurs, including aortic stenosis

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
  4. 04

    Brief neurologic examination: consciousness, facial symmetry, and limb strength

    • Upper limb strength: shoulder and elbow ↗
    • Lower limb strength testing (nerve roots L2–S1) ↗
    • Pronator drift test ↗
    • Facial movements: comparison of the upper and lower face ↗
  5. 05

    Check for head injury and bite marks along the side of the tongue

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Inspect and palpate for head trauma and scalp hematoma Technique card pending
