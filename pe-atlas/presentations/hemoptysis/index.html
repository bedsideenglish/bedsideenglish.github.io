Prioritize the patient's condition. With substantial hemoptysis, shortness of breath, altered consciousness, or circulatory instability, prioritize urgent assessment and calling for help over a detailed examination. Even apparently small-volume bleeding needs further assessment if unexplained or recurrent.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Check blood pressure, pulse, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and temperature for signs of major bleeding or hypoxemia

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Pulse oximetry: placement, signal quality, and limitations ↗
    • Respiratory rate, rhythm, and effort ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Inspect the nose and mouth for an upper airway source of bleeding

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Anterior rhinoscopy: nasal vestibule and visible landmarks ↗
  3. 03

    Palpate the cervical and supraclavicular lymph nodes

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
  4. 04

    Chest percussion and lung auscultation for crackles, wheezes, and focal reduction in breath sounds

    • Posterior lung auscultation sequence ↗
    • Posterior chest percussion with the arms crossed in front ↗
  5. 05

    Auscultate the heart for murmurs and assess the neck veins

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Jugular venous pressure (JVP) assessment ↗
  6. 06

    Check for clubbing, unilateral leg swelling, and calf tenderness

    • Clubbing and the Schamroth window ↗
    • Assessment of pitting edema ↗
    • Measure calf circumference and examine for signs of deep vein thrombosis Technique card pending
