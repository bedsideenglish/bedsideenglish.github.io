Prioritize the patient's condition. A rapid decrease in urine output accompanied by shortness of breath, altered mental status, severe edema, or circulatory instability requires urgent assessment before a detailed examination. Examination findings alone do not determine the cause or the need for treatment.

Physical examination checklists by clinical presentation

Examinations without a card remain in the checklist. An item that combines several examinations may include both available cards and specific techniques that are still pending. What you actually examine depends on the symptoms and clinical indications.

  1. 01

    Blood pressure, pulse, temperature, and orthostatic blood pressure changes

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Orthostatic blood pressure and pulse measurement ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess oral mucosa, skin turgor, and jugular veins

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Jugular venous pressure (JVP) assessment ↗
    • Fluid status: oral mucosa and skin turgor ↗
  3. 03

    Auscultate the lungs for crackles and assess leg edema

    • Posterior lung auscultation sequence ↗
    • Assessment of pitting edema ↗
  4. 04

    Palpate and percuss for suprapubic bladder distention

    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Suprapubic bladder assessment: gentle palpation and percussion ↗
  5. 05

    Assess for costovertebral angle tenderness; perform a digital rectal examination in men if indicated

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Costovertebral angle tenderness by fist percussion ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: rectal wall and prostate ↗
  6. 06

    Assess for rash, joint swelling, and muscle tenderness
