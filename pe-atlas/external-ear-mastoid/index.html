External ear and mastoid · inspection and gentle contacts

Distinguish pinna movement, tragal pressure and postauricular examination; recognise findings needing prompt assessment.

See the examination

A · Right lateral view with face/anterior to screen-left. Tragus is anterior to canal; mastoid contact region lies behind and below the pinna.

B · Gentle pressure meets the tragus, not the canal lumen. Ask whether familiar pain is reproduced.

C · Gentle pinna movement is a separate contact. Arrow shows direction only and is not a force scale or required displacement.

D · Inspect then gently palpate behind the ear. Blue shading is a contact locator, not swelling; redness, swelling, protrusion and systemic illness change urgency.

How to do it

  1. Explain, obtain consent and inspect both sides before touching. Note skin lesions/vesicles, discharge, deformity and ear position. Compare the postauricular sulcus and swelling.
  2. With warning and light pressure, touch the tragus over the front of the canal entrance, without inserting a finger. Ask whether this reproduces the familiar pain.
  3. Gently move the pinna separately and stop if painful. Do not pull traumatised tissue or assume that pain with traction alone establishes external otitis.
  4. Inspect then gently palpate the mastoid/postauricular region behind the ear. Record erythema, swelling, tenderness, sulcus loss or ear protrusion with fever, illness and neurological symptoms. Do not press deeply to prove pain.
  5. Postauricular swelling, ear protrusion, significant systemic illness or neurological signs require prompt assessment for complications. Follow with safe otoscopy and appropriate examination of referred-pain sources; this surface card cannot show the middle ear.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will gently touch the front of the ear, move the outer ear and check behind it. Tell me if it hurts and I will stop.

A positive finding

Tragal/pinna tenderness supports external-ear inflammation in context; postauricular inflammation or protrusion raises concern for mastoid complications and needs prompt evaluation.

A negative finding

Normal surface findings do not exclude middle-ear disease, early complications, referred otalgia or invasive disease in a high-risk patient.

Evidence & limitations

Right lateral projection labels anterior direction explicitly. Blue mastoid shading marks where to examine, not infection or the anatomical boundary of mastoid air cells. No otoscopic view is simulated.

Points to remember

  1. Do not confuse tragus with the canal or mastoid with the pinna; normal otoscopy does not end a persistent unexplained-otalgia evaluation.
