Bronchophony and whispered pectoriloquy

How to do it

  1. Explain and sit the patient comfortably. Expose each area with permission and place the diaphragm directly on skin.
  2. Ask for the same spoken phrase, for example ninety-nine. Compare matching lung areas left and right, avoiding scapular bone and the spine.
  3. Keep phonation consistent. If focal unusually loud/clear words are heard, repeat over matching sites using a whisper such as one-two-three.
  4. Extend systematically across posterior, lateral and anterior fields as indicated; the diagram shows selected posterior comparison sites only.
  5. Document the location and actual sound quality alongside breath sounds, percussion and clinical context.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please say ninety-nine in your normal voice. Now whisper one, two, three.

A positive finding

Focally enhanced spoken-word transmission is bronchophony; unusually intelligible whispered words are whispered pectoriloquy. Consolidation can cause either, but neither identifies an organism.

A negative finding

Muffled or absent enhancement does not exclude pneumonia. Obstruction, pleural fluid, chest wall thickness and weak phonation affect transmission.

Evidence & limitations

The finding is auditory; selected paired listening sites are drawn, with no synthesized normal/pathological waveform or sound.

Points to remember

  1. This is auscultation, not palpated tactile fremitus or an ear-hearing whisper test.
  2. Korean words are communication examples, not a validated diagnostic word list; avoid the English E-to-A rule here.
