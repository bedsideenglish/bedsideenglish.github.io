Weber test — sound lateralisation

Compare where a bone-conducted tone is perceived. Together with Rinne and otoscopy, this helps classify asymmetric hearing loss before audiometry.

See the examination

512 Hz: the footplate contacts the midline forehead; both ears remain open.

Interpret the reported side with Rinne; equal hearing also occurs with symmetric loss. No sound is simulated.

Example report: louder on the right. Consider R conductive OR L sensorineural loss; correlate with Rinne, history and otoscopy.

Example report: louder on the left. Consider L conductive OR R sensorineural loss; correlate with Rinne, history and otoscopy.

How to do it

  1. Explain the task while facing the seated patient; ask which ear seems worse and whether the change was sudden. Use a quiet room and remove hearing aids for the unaided test.
  2. Use an intact 512-Hz hearing-test fork. Hold its stem and activate it on a padded surface; do not hit metal or touch the vibrating tines.
  3. Place the footplate on the middle of the forehead with comfortable, steady contact. Support the back of the head if needed; keep both ear canals open.
  4. Ask whether the sound is central/equal, louder on the right, louder on the left, or not heard. Ask about sound, rather than the feeling of vibration.
  5. Repeat if the answer is uncertain, without suggesting a side. Record the patient's right/left and perform Rinne on each ear.
  6. Combine the history, ear examination and both fork tests; arrange audiometry when hearing loss is suspected.

What to say to the patient

  1. I'll place a vibrating fork on the middle of your forehead. It should not hurt.
  2. Where do you hear the sound: in the middle, more on your right, or more on your left?
  3. Please tell me if you cannot hear a sound, even if you can feel vibration.

A positive finding

Lateralisation is an asymmetric finding, not a diagnosis. Sound towards the poorer ear supports a conductive component on that side; sound towards the better ear supports sensorineural loss on the opposite side. For example, a rightward Weber can mean right conductive loss OR left sensorineural loss.

A negative finding

Central/equal perception can reflect normal hearing OR symmetric bilateral hearing loss. No perceived tone is not a normal result: check activation and understanding, then assess further.

Evidence & limitations

This is a qualitative comparison at the fork's frequency. Published diagnostic accuracy varies with technique and the pattern of loss; a central result cannot serve as a general hearing screen. Mixed or bilateral disease may not follow the simple unilateral rules.

Points to remember

  1. Occluding an ear can itself change bone-conducted loudness. Do not mask an ear during Weber.
  2. The diagram shows footplate placement and three possible reports, not measured loudness or a simulated patient response. The head-supporting hand is described in the steps, not drawn.
  3. Do not delay urgent assessment for a sudden unexplained hearing change because a fork result seems reassuring. NICE advises assessment within 24 hours for loss developing over at most 3 days within the preceding 30 days, without an external/middle-ear explanation.
