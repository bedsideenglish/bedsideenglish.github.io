Lower-limb power by nerve root (L2-S1)

Tests muscle power joint by joint, each linked to a main nerve root, so a pattern of weakness points to a level: hip flexion L2, knee extension L3-L4, ankle dorsiflexion L4-L5, ankle plantarflexion S1. Power is graded 0 to 5 on the MRC scale.

How to do it

  1. Patient lies supine, legs exposed. Inspect for wasting and fasciculations and check tone first. Compare right and left throughout.
  2. Hip flexion (L2): lift the straight leg off the bed, or hip flexed at 90 degrees, and push down on the thigh while the patient resists.
  3. Knee extension (L3-L4): with the knee bent, hold the lower leg and ask the patient to straighten the knee against your pull. (Knee flexion, S1-S2, is tested the other way.)
  4. Ankle dorsiflexion (L4-L5): ask the patient to pull the foot up; press down on the top of the foot. Great toe extension tests L5.
  5. Ankle plantarflexion (S1): ask the patient to push the foot down against your hand on the sole. Grade each movement 0 to 5 and test hip abduction (L5) and extension (S1) if needed.

What to say to the patient

  1. I'm going to test the strength in your legs. Please lie back.
  2. Lift your leg up and don't let me push it down. Hold it, hold it.
  3. Now straighten your knee against my hand. Now pull your foot up toward your nose, and now push your foot down like pressing a pedal.

A positive finding

Weakness is graded: 5 normal, 4 reduced against resistance (4-, 4, 4+ if you subdivide), 3 moves against gravity only, 2 moves with gravity eliminated, 1 flicker, 0 none. A pattern fitting one root (for example weak ankle dorsiflexion and great toe extension with L5 weakness) suggests a radiculopathy such as a disc herniation. Weakness in many roots on both sides suggests a cord, cauda equina or neuromuscular cause.

A negative finding

Full power (5/5) in every group on both sides. This argues against a significant motor radiculopathy or myelopathy, but sensory-only root lesions, a pain-limited effort and subtle weakness can be missed.

Evidence & limitations

Motor testing has good specificity but only moderate sensitivity for a compressed root. In lumbar disc herniation a motor deficit is found in only a minority of patients, and ankle dorsiflexion and great toe extension weakness are the most useful for L4-L5 level lesions. The root-to-muscle map is a simplification because muscles usually have two or more root supplies. MRC grades 4 and below are examiner-dependent.

Points to remember

  1. Pain, poor effort and fear make the patient give way. Ask about pain first and tell them to push as hard as they can.
  2. Always compare sides and match your effort to the patient's size; a 4 must be judged against what the muscle should do.
  3. Test one joint at a time and fix the joint above so other muscles cannot compensate.
  4. The root levels are the common textbook ones; some sources list slightly different levels, so say 'mainly L2' and the like.
