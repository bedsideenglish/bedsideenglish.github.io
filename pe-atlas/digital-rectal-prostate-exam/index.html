Digital rectal examination · rectal walls and prostate

Teach the actual reachable rectal and posterior prostate contacts separately from the existing positioning-only card.

How to do it

  1. Confirm a specific indication. Explain the external and internal contacts separately, obtain consent, offer an appropriate chaperone, provide private undressing and draping, and agree on a stop signal. Recheck permission as the examination proceeds; stop for pain or withdrawal of consent. A learner needs supervised training.
  2. Choose a comfortable appropriate position such as left lateral, preserve draping and inspect the perianal area first. Use a gloved lubricated index finger, warn the patient, and enter gently while relaxed along the anal canal. Never force against pain or resistance.
  3. Assess only reachable rectal walls for tenderness, masses and mucosal irregularity, moving gently. Document the extent and limits; this is not examination of the whole rectum or colon.
  4. In a patient with a prostate and an indication, the palpable posterior surface lies beyond the anterior rectal wall. Assess approximate size, symmetry, consistency, nodules, tenderness and median sulcus; do not infer exact volume or the unseen anterior gland.
  5. Do not massage an acutely tender prostate or repeatedly provoke pain. Withdraw gently, inspect the glove as relevant, offer tissues and privacy, and record findings and tolerance. DRE alone is not prostate-cancer screening or exclusion.

What to say to the patient

  1. I would like to examine the reachable rectum and, if relevant, the prostate with one lubricated gloved finger. It should not be sharply painful; tell me to stop if needed.

A positive finding

A mass, focal firmness, irregularity or tenderness requires assessment in context; examination cannot establish histology or reliably quantify gland size.

A negative finding

A smooth, nontender posterior surface cannot exclude prostate cancer, lesions beyond reach or other causes of urinary symptoms.

Evidence & limitations

Sagittal contacts distinguish rectum posterior from prostate anterior; a separate posterior-surface view shows the median sulcus. No finger enters the prostate or urethra.

Points to remember

  1. Positioning does not teach internal palpation. The reachable surface and patient tolerance determine the examination; no forced depth is taught.
