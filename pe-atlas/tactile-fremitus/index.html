How to do it

  1. Seat the patient with arms folded forward and expose the back with consent.
  2. Place the ulnar edge or palmar base of the same hand over corresponding lung fields, avoiding scapulae.
  3. Ask for the same phrase at the same loudness at each site; compare side to side and down to the bases.
  4. Correlate asymmetry with percussion and auscultation.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please say ninety-nine each time I place my hand on your back.
  2. Use the same volume each time.

A positive finding

Increased fremitus can accompany consolidation with an open bronchus; decreased fremitus can occur with pleural fluid or air.

A negative finding

Symmetric fremitus does not exclude pneumonia or pleural disease.

Evidence & limitations

Voice strength and chest-wall thickness affect transmission; compare like with like.

Points to remember

  1. Do not compare one site on a rib or scapula with another in an interspace.
  2. The diagram shows the contact sequence; hand-edge orientation and vibration are not animated.
