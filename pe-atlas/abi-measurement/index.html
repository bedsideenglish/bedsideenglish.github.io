Resting ankle–brachial index (Doppler)

Compare each leg’s highest ankle systolic pressure with the higher brachial systolic pressure. Obtain separate right and left ABIs; a pulse palpation alone is not an ABI measurement.

See the examination

Measure R and L brachial systolic pressures with Doppler; retain the higher arm value.

RIGHT DP: Doppler on dorsum, lateral to EHL; cuff proximal to malleoli.

RIGHT PT: same leg, medial view; probe posterior/inferior to medial malleolus.

LEFT DP: repeat the dorsal measurement on the opposite foot.

LEFT PT: repeat behind the opposite medial malleolus.

Worked example: each leg uses its higher ankle pressure and the SAME higher arm denominator.

How to do it

  1. Explain and rest the patient flat, supine, for 5–10 minutes in a comfortable room. Support limbs at heart level and select cuffs appropriate to limb circumference.
  2. Use gel and a handheld Doppler over each brachial artery, distal to the arm cuff. Inflate until the signal disappears; deflate slowly and record the first returning signal as systolic pressure. Measure both arms.
  3. Place the ankle cuff just proximal to the malleoli. Locate dorsalis pedis (DP) lateral to the extensor hallucis longus tendon on the dorsum; measure its systolic pressure by the same Doppler-return method.
  4. Measure posterior tibial (PT) pressure behind and below the medial malleolus. Repeat DP and PT on the other leg. Do not infer a zero pressure merely from failure to find a signal.
  5. For EACH leg: max(its DP, its PT) / max(right arm, left arm). Record all six pressures, both ABIs, any technical difficulty and clinical findings.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will measure pressures in both arms and both ankles while you lie flat.
  2. The cuffs will squeeze. Please keep still and tell me if it becomes painful.

A positive finding

2024 ACC/AHA categories: ABI ≤0.90 abnormal; 0.91–0.99 borderline; 1.00–1.40 normal; >1.40 noncompressible. A high noncompressible value does not mean excellent perfusion; toe pressure/TBI and waveforms may be needed.

A negative finding

A normal resting ABI does not exclude exertional PAD or chronic limb-threatening ischaemia. Symptoms, wounds, diabetes/CKD and waveform findings determine whether exercise ABI or further vascular testing is required.

Evidence & limitations

The worked example uses arms 132/140 mmHg, right DP/PT 112/126 and left DP/PT 140/154: right ABI 0.90 and left ABI 1.10. Values are teaching data; the same higher arm pressure, 140, is used for both legs.

Points to remember

  1. The dorsal-foot and medial-ankle panels are different views of the SAME tested limb; PT is not a dorsal-foot point.
  2. Do not average DP/PT or arm pressures, interchange legs, or use palpated pulse strength as pressure. The diagram does not simulate signal quality or cuff compression.
