Asterixis: lapses of sustained posture

Look for brief irregular failures to maintain wrist extension: negative myoclonus.

How to do it

  1. Seat the patient securely and ask them to stretch both arms forward.
  2. Ask for palms down, fingers spread and wrists extended as if signalling stop. Observe for several seconds without asking the patient to flap.
  3. Look for sudden brief flexion lapses and return to extension; compare the two sides and distinguish a regular oscillation.
  4. If present, assess consciousness, medication exposure and the clinical cause; document unilateral or bilateral involvement.

What to say to the patient

  1. Keep your hands still like this, with your wrists bent back. Do not wave them.

A positive finding

Irregular postural lapses are compatible with asterixis. Bilateral findings can accompany metabolic or drug-related encephalopathy; a unilateral finding can suggest a focal lesion.

A negative finding

Absence does not exclude encephalopathy, including hepatic encephalopathy.

Evidence & limitations

Asterixis is not specific to liver disease and is not a rhythmic tremor.

Points to remember

  1. An unable or poorly cooperative patient may not sustain the required posture.
  2. The unequal right/left lapses are illustrative, not diagnostic timing. Individual finger spreading and loss of muscle activity are not simulated.
