Anterior rhinoscopy · vestibule and visible landmarks

Inspect the anterior nasal cavity safely, distinguishing septum, inferior turbinate and floor. Anterior inspection is limited and does not visualize the whole nose, nasopharynx or sinuses.

B · Right anterior cavity enlargement: lateral inferior turbinate, medial septum and floor.

C · Inspect visible mucosa and floor; compare the opposite side separately. This is not a sinus or posterior-nose view.

How to do it

  1. Seat the patient, explain and provide a focused light. Inspect the outside and entrance first. Active major bleeding, airway compromise or concerning trauma takes priority over a routine view.
  2. Use a clean appropriate nasal speculum or suitable wide otoscope tip with training in that device. Stabilize the examining hand against the face and position the head gently.
  3. Enter only the vestibule under direct vision. With a nasal speculum, open gently in its proper plane, avoiding pressure on the sensitive septum. Never force, lever against the septum or advance blindly.
  4. In each cavity identify the medial septum, lateral inferior turbinate and nasal floor. Describe discharge, crust, bleeding point, swelling, perforation or a mass only when actually seen. Adjust head angle gently for a better anterior view.
  5. Withdraw under vision without pinching nasal hairs and repeat the other side. A normal anterior view does not exclude a posterior source, a foreign body out of view or sinus disease; arrange appropriate further assessment when indicated.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will gently look just inside each nostril. Tell me if it is painful; I will not push against the middle wall.

A positive finding

Describe the actual abnormality and its side. Swollen turbinates or discharge alone do not distinguish all allergic, infectious and structural causes.

A negative finding

A limited normal view cannot exclude posterior bleeding, a hidden lesion or sinus disease. This does not replace nasal endoscopy when indicated.

Evidence & limitations

The access panel shows a short wide tip resting only at the entrance; the next panels separately enlarge anterior landmarks. Blade mechanics and an internal endoscopic passage are not simulated.

Points to remember

  1. In the patient’s right cavity, the septum is medial (screen-right on the frontal enlargement); the turbinate is lateral (screen-left).
