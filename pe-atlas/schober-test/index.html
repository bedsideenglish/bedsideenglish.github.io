Modified Schober test — 10 cm above / 5 cm below

Measure change in lumbar skin-mark separation during forward flexion, using the explicitly defined 15-cm modified Schober method.

See the examination

A · Upright: PSIS reference; upper +10 cm and lower −5 cm.

B · Same skin marks separate during comfortable forward flexion.

C · Example only: 20 − 15 = 5 cm increase; record actual values.

How to do it

  1. Explain, obtain permission to expose the lower back and have the patient stand comfortably upright.
  2. Locate both posterior superior iliac spines (PSIS). Mark the midline where the line joining them crosses it.
  3. Along the midline, place an upper mark 10 cm above and a lower mark 5 cm below this reference; verify the total resting span is 15 cm.
  4. Ask the patient to bend forward as far as comfortably possible with knees straight, without bouncing or forcing pain.
  5. Remeasure between the SAME upper and lower skin marks along the back surface. Record the flexed span minus 15 cm, symptoms and the method used.
  6. Allow supported return to standing. Interpret alongside other spinal/hip findings and the clinical history.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will put three small marks on your lower back.
  2. Keep your knees straight and bend forward only as far as comfortable.

A positive finding

A smaller increase indicates restricted flexion for this method; it is not specific for axial spondyloarthritis and may reflect pain, age, effort or other restrictions.

A negative finding

A preserved increase cannot exclude early axial spondyloarthritis or isolate individual lumbar segment motion.

Evidence & limitations

Methods called Schober, modified Schober and modified-modified Schober use different marks. This card uses 10 cm above plus 5 cm below; do not transfer a threshold from another method. Report the actual change rather than declaring ankylosing spondylitis.

Points to remember

  1. Keep the original skin marks; do not find a new PSIS reference after bending.
  2. Measure the surface path, not a straight line through air.
  3. The enlarged 15→20 cm example is illustrative; the small mannequin has a rigid spine and cannot demonstrate lumbar segmental flexion.
