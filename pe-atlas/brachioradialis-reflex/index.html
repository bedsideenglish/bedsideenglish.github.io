Brachioradialis reflex (mainly C6)

Assess the radial-nerve stretch-reflex arc, C5–C6 and mainly C6, by tapping the distal radial forearm and observing elbow flexion, sometimes with slight supination.

See the examination

Brief tap to the distal radial forearm, then rebound

Example response: elbow flexion; accompanying supination is not drawn

How to do it

  1. Explain the examination and seat the patient. Support the relaxed arm with the elbow partly flexed and the forearm in a neutral or slightly pronated position.
  2. Palpate the brachioradialis tendon on the radial side a few centimetres proximal to the radial styloid; locate it on the patient rather than striking the wrist joint.
  3. Give the identified distal radial forearm/tendon region a brief, controlled hammer tap and allow immediate rebound.
  4. Watch for brachioradialis contraction and involuntary elbow flexion; slight supination may accompany it. Do not mistake voluntary withdrawal or isolated finger flexion for the expected response.
  5. Compare sides, repeat with better relaxation if needed, and document the response with biceps/triceps reflexes, strength and sensory findings.

What to say to the patient

  1. Rest your arm in my hand and let it relax.
  2. I will gently tap the thumb side of your forearm, just above the wrist.
  3. Let the movement happen by itself.

A positive finding

An asymmetric depression can support dysfunction of the C6-predominant or radial-nerve reflex arc. An unexpectedly brisk or inverted response needs interpretation with the full neurologic examination.

A negative finding

A symmetric response does not exclude cervical radiculopathy or neuropathy. An absent response is not interpretable until positioning, relaxation and the target have been checked.

Evidence & limitations

Use the localised response pattern rather than diagnosing from a single reflex. Common teaching assigns C5–C6, with C6 predominance.

Points to remember

  1. Avoid the radial styloid itself, the wrist joint and repeated forceful blows.
  2. The target is a schematic distal radial surface contact. The model cannot independently pronate/supinate the forearm, so only elbow flexion is animated; the timing and angle are not measured physiology.
