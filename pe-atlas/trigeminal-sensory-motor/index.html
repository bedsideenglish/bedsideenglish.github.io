CN V · facial light touch and jaw motor

Compare selected V1/V2/V3 sensory sites and distinguish jaw motor testing from sensation and corneal-reflex testing.

See the examination

A · Selected V1 forehead, V2 cheek and V3 lower-face sites. The angle of the jaw is mainly C2–C3; color does not define exact borders.

B · Demonstrate first, then compare light touch with eyes closed; the stimulus contacts cheek skin, never the cornea.

C · Palpate masseter contraction on both sides; temporalis is assessed separately in the actual patient.

D · A separate jaw-opening task against gentle upward chin resistance. Do not force a painful jaw or diagnose from deviation alone.

How to do it

  1. Explain and demonstrate a light touch with the patient looking, then ask them to close the eyes. Use a clean soft stimulus at corresponding forehead, cheek and lower-face/chin sites; do not touch the eye.
  2. Ask whether the stimulus is felt and equal on both sides. Compare sites systematically, adapting for symptoms and recording untested areas. The jaw angle is mainly upper cervical, not a V3 test site.
  3. Ask for a gentle clench and relaxation while palpating the masseters and temporalis for bulk and contraction. Avoid loading a painful jaw.
  4. If appropriate, ask for opening against gentle resistance under the chin; compare strength and deviation. Pain, temporomandibular disease and cooperation can alter performance.
  5. A corneal reflex or pinprick is a separate indicated examination requiring suitable training and equipment. Do not add routine corneal contact to this light-touch card.

What to say to the patient

  1. Close your eyes. Does this feel the same on both sides? Now gently clench your teeth, then relax.

A positive finding

Reproducible sensory asymmetry or jaw weakness may suggest dysfunction, but the distribution and associated neurological and mechanical findings determine interpretation.

A negative finding

Normal selected sites and jaw movement do not establish normality of every trigeminal function or exclude a central lesion.

Evidence & limitations

Colored spots identify sample sites, not exact nerve boundaries. Resistance arrows show direction without force calibration.

Points to remember

  1. Use corresponding sides and similar stimulus strength; avoid leading the patient toward a presumed deficit.
