Lower-lid conjunctiva and ocular icterus

Locate the palpebral conjunctiva for pallor and the conjunctiva over the white of the eye for yellowing. These are visual clues that require laboratory and clinical correlation.

How to do it

  1. Explain, clean hands and inspect in neutral daylight or good white light. Ask about pain, injury and contact lenses; do not manipulate an eye with suspected open-globe injury.
  2. Ask the patient to look up. Place a clean finger on the skin just below the lower lid and gently draw it down, exposing the lower palpebral conjunctiva without touching or pressing the globe.
  3. Compare both sides and note pallor of the normally vascular inner-lid surface. Restore the lid gently; inflammation, lighting and observer variation affect colour judgment.
  4. Inspect the exposed white of each eye for diffuse yellowing rather than calling a focal yellow conjunctival lesion jaundice. Record the observed site and correlate with symptoms and bilirubin when indicated.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please look up. I will gently lower the eyelid from the skin below it without pressing your eye.

A positive finding

Conjunctival pallor increases suspicion of anemia; diffuse ocular yellowing supports possible hyperbilirubinemia. Neither establishes the cause or severity.

A negative finding

Normal apparent colour does not exclude anemia or elevated bilirubin. Do not estimate a hemoglobin or bilirubin value from these colour examples.

Evidence & limitations

Conjunctival pallor has imperfect observer agreement and cannot replace a blood count. The traditional term “scleral icterus” refers clinically to yellowing over the ocular white; the displayed colour is schematic.

Points to remember

  1. Keep the inner-lid surface distinct from the ocular white. These held examples do not show one patient developing anemia or jaundice.
