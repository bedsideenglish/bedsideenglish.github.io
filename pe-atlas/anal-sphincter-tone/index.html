Anal sphincter tone · resting resistance and squeeze

Distinguish resting resistance from voluntary squeeze when an internal examination is indicated; understand its major diagnostic limits in suspected CES.

See the examination

A · After external inspection and specific permission, a lubricated gloved finger follows the anal canal gently; no forced straight deep insertion.

B · The finger stays still while the patient relaxes. Assess resting resistance separately from voluntary contraction.

C · The same finger remains in position while the patient squeezes and relaxes. Arrows point inward, not along a push-out maneuver.

D · No examination contact is demanded by this panel: DRE is not a prerequisite for CES referral, and normal tone cannot exclude it.

How to do it

  1. Do not make DRE a prerequisite to emergency referral for suspected CES. The GIRFT pathway states DRE is not necessary for referral documentation; record subjective perianal sensation and follow urgent assessment when symptoms warrant it.
  2. Confirm a specific indication. Explain the external and internal contacts separately, obtain consent, offer an appropriate chaperone, provide private undressing and draping, and agree on a stop signal. Recheck permission as the examination proceeds; stop for pain or withdrawal of consent. A learner needs supervised training.
  3. When separately indicated, position safely, inspect externally, use gloves and lubricant, and warn before one finger enters gently while the patient relaxes. Follow the anal canal without force; stop for pain or withdrawn permission.
  4. With the finger still, ask for relaxation and assess resting resistance. Then ask the patient to squeeze around the same finger and relax again; distinguish this from bearing down.
  5. Record rest and squeeze separately, including pain/cooperation and examination limits. Withdraw gently, offer tissues and privacy, and explain next steps. Tone assessment is subjective; normal tone cannot exclude CES.

What to say to the patient

  1. If this examination is needed, I will use one lubricated gloved finger. First relax, then gently squeeze around my finger. You can ask me to stop at any time.

A positive finding

Reduced resistance or squeeze can be clinically relevant, but pain, cooperation and examiner variability limit interpretation; it is not a stand-alone CES diagnosis.

A negative finding

Normal resting tone or voluntary squeeze cannot exclude cauda equina compression and must not delay urgent assessment or MRI when indicated.

Evidence & limitations

Sagittal schematic shows one finger within the lumen and a surrounding sphincter ring. Squeeze arrows are conceptual; no pressure or diagnostic cutoff is simulated.

Points to remember

  1. This card does not teach anal reflexes or manometry. Do not equate a clinician’s impression of tone with a quantitative pressure measurement.
