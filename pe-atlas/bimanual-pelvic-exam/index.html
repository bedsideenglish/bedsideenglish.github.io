Bimanual pelvic examination · cervix, uterus and adnexa

Show how vaginal fingers and an abdominal hand cooperate, without confusing cervical, uterine and adnexal assessment.

See the examination

A · Sagittal section: one or two lubricated gloved fingers as tolerated follow the vaginal route; the speculum is absent.

B · Fingertips identify the cervix and, if indicated, gently assess movement-related pain without entering its canal.

C · The external hand is over the suprapubic abdomen and internal fingers support below the cervix; gently assess the uterus between them.

D · Separate anterior cutaway: both hands assess the patient-right side (screen-left), then the other side. A nonpalpable ovary is not proof of no disease.

How to do it

  1. Confirm a specific indication. Explain the external and internal contacts separately, obtain consent, offer an appropriate chaperone, provide private undressing and draping, and agree on a stop signal. Recheck permission as the examination proceeds; stop for pain or withdrawal of consent. A learner needs supervised training.
  2. Confirm the indication separately from speculum examination. With speculum removed, appropriate draping and a comfortable supported position, use lubricated gloved finger(s), one or two as tolerated, and warn before gentle vaginal entry. Do not force through pain.
  3. Locate the cervix and note its position. If indicated, move it only gently while asking about reproduced pelvic pain; stop rather than repeatedly provoking marked pain. Cervical motion tenderness is not specific for PID.
  4. Place the external hand above the pubis and gently approximate it with the internal supporting fingers to assess uterine position, approximate size, mobility and tenderness. Fingers remain in the vagina/fornix, not inside the cervix or uterus; orientation varies.
  5. Shift internal fingers toward a lateral fornix and the abdominal hand to the same lower-abdominal side to assess adnexal tenderness or mass; repeat on the opposite side. Do not forcibly capture an ovary, which may not be palpable.
  6. Withdraw gently, offer tissues/privacy and document findings and examination limits. Acute severe pelvic pain, pregnancy-related concerns or systemic illness may need urgent assessment independent of normal palpation.

What to say to the patient

  1. With your permission, I will use one or two fingers inside the vagina and a hand on the lower abdomen to gently check the pelvic organs. Tell me if it hurts or you want me to stop.

A positive finding

Cervical, uterine or adnexal tenderness, reduced mobility or a mass can guide investigation but does not by itself establish PID, torsion, pregnancy location or histology.

A negative finding

Normal palpation or an impalpable ovary does not exclude PID, ectopic pregnancy, adnexal torsion or a small/deep lesion.

Evidence & limitations

Sagittal example uses an anteverted uterus; the separate anterior adnexal panel labels patient sides. Contact arrows are conceptual, not force, depth or mandatory displacement.

Points to remember

  1. No simultaneous speculum, no entry into uterine/cervical cavities, and no repeated force to produce a positive sign.
