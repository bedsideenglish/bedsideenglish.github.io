Trendelenburg sign — stance-side hip control

Observe pelvic control during single-leg stance, identifying the weight-bearing hip rather than labelling the lifted leg as the tested side.

How to do it

  1. Explain, ask about pain and falls, and assess whether unsupported single-leg stance is safe. Guard closely near a stable support.
  2. Observe from behind with both feet down and identify a transverse pelvic reference; note pre-existing asymmetry.
  3. Ask the patient to lift the left foot, standing on the right leg. Observe both pelvic sides and the trunk; do not push the pelvis down.
  4. Note whether the left, unsupported side remains level/slightly rises or drops. Also note lean toward the right stance hip, pain and need for support.
  5. Return both feet to the floor, then compare the other side if safe. Record stance side, pelvic behaviour, trunk compensation and support.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will stay beside you. Lift your left foot if you feel steady.
  2. Put it down at once if you feel pain or lose balance.

A positive finding

Drop of the unsupported left hemipelvis during right stance is a positive sign for impaired right hip abductor mechanism/control. Causes include weakness, pain and altered hip mechanics; this is not proof of isolated superior gluteal nerve injury.

A negative finding

A level pelvis does not exclude abductor pathology, especially if trunk lean or hand support unloads the hip.

Evidence & limitations

Research protocols vary in stance duration and pelvic-angle criteria, and the test correlates imperfectly with isolated strength. This card assigns no universal angle, time cutoff or strength grade.

Points to remember

  1. Always name the stance hip: left pelvic drop on right stance concerns the right side.
  2. A trunk lean can mask pelvic drop; inspect both, and document assistance.
  3. The posterior-view examples independently tilt the pelvis; they are categorical examples, not a measured gait trace.
