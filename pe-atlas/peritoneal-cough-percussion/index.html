Peritoneal irritation · cough and gentle percussion

Assess pain provoked by small abdominal movement while minimising repeated painful examination; record the location reported by the patient.

See the examination

A · One gentle cough if tolerated, without examiner pressure. Ask for familiar pain and observe; do not insist.

B · Start away from maximal pain: gentle finger-on-finger percussion. This is not deep compression followed by release.

C · Patient indicates the reported site; stop when the finding is clear. Site is illustrative, not a diagnosis or mandatory contact.

How to do it

  1. Assess stability and urgent features first. Explain, obtain consent, drape appropriately and support the patient supine with the abdomen relaxed as tolerated. Ask where the pain is worst before touching.
  2. If tolerated, ask for one gentle cough and ask whether it causes the familiar abdominal pain and where. Observe expression and protective movement; do not insist or repeat if clearly painful.
  3. For gentle percussion, begin away from the worst pain. Rest a finger lightly on the abdominal wall and tap it gently with the other hand, comparing only as tolerated. The illustrated contact is an example, not a mandatory point.
  4. Record the exact site and provoking action; distinguish tenderness from nonspecific discomfort. Stop once the finding is clear. Repeated forceful rebound or deep pressure is not required to demonstrate pain.
  5. Interpret with history, vital signs, other findings and indicated investigation; obtain prompt assessment for suspected peritonitis or instability. Analgesia and urgent care should not wait for completion of manoeuvres.

What to say to the patient

  1. Could you give one small cough if comfortable? Tell me whether it brings on your usual pain and point to where you feel it. We can stop.

A positive finding

Local familiar pain on coughing or gentle percussion supports peritoneal irritation but does not identify an inflamed organ or prove a surgical diagnosis.

A negative finding

Absent provoked pain does not exclude peritonitis, appendicitis or ischemia, especially with subtle presentations.

Evidence & limitations

These lower-provocation observations complement clinical assessment; no isolated sensitivity or exclusion claim is assigned. The drawn pain site is patient-selected, not an appendix map.

Points to remember

  1. Do not press a pulsatile mass, escalate force to obtain a sign or treat a negative cough test as clearance.
