Pigmented lesion · ABCDE and documented change

Describe a pigmented lesion systematically and identify concerning change. The drawings illustrate features, not proven melanoma, a dermoscopy image or a diagnostic score.

How to do it

  1. Ask about onset, change, symptoms, prior photographs and personal/family skin-cancer history. With consent expose the area, use good light and compare with the patient’s other lesions.
  2. A: compare the two halves for asymmetry. B: describe an irregular or poorly defined border. These features can also occur in benign lesions.
  3. C: describe variation in colour within the lesion, including pink or depigmented areas. Melanoma can be lightly pigmented or amelanotic, so absence of dark colour is not reassuring by itself.
  4. D: measure actual greatest diameter in millimetres, with a perpendicular dimension when useful. Lesions smaller than 6 mm can be melanoma; do not use a size threshold to dismiss concern.
  5. E: document change in size, shape, colour or symptoms over time, using comparable photographs with separate consent when appropriate. A new or different lesion, bleeding or persistent change warrants clinical assessment.
  6. Record site and findings. Trained dermoscopy and biopsy/referral may be needed; do not delay a concerning lesion until every ABCDE feature is present.

What to say to the patient

  1. Has this spot changed? With your permission I will look closely and measure it, then compare with any earlier photograph.

A positive finding

One or more concerning features, a changing lesion or an ugly-duckling lesion supports further assessment. These features do not establish malignancy.

A negative finding

A small, symmetric or uniformly coloured lesion can still be malignant. ABCDE does not capture every melanoma, particularly some nodular or amelanotic presentations.

Evidence & limitations

A–D are independent feature examples; E uses two separate timepoint drawings. The ruler is schematic and the display cannot measure a real lesion. No dermoscopic network or histology is shown.

Points to remember

  1. Do not count ABCDE features as a validated risk score or make a benign/malignant diagnosis from the artwork. Include palms, soles and nails in a broader indicated skin examination.
