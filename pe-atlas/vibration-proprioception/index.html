Hallux vibration and joint position sense

Test two distinct large-fibre sensory modalities at a distal bony site, then move proximally if needed.

See the examination

Vibration: footplate on the dorsal hallux IP joint; ask when buzzing stops.

Position sense: stabilise proximally and grip the sides of the distal phalanx.

Side inset: small upward movement; ask the direction with eyes closed.

How to do it

  1. Explain both sensations with the patient watching first. Support the foot. For vibration, use a 128-Hz tuning fork, not the 512-Hz hearing fork.
  2. Ask the patient to close their eyes. Place the vibrating footplate on the bony dorsal hallux interphalangeal joint; ask whether vibration is felt and when it stops. Compare sides and the examiner’s perception as appropriate.
  3. For position sense, stabilise the proximal phalanx and hold the distal phalanx by its medial and lateral sides. Demonstrate up/down, then test with eyes closed using small, unpredictable passive movements.
  4. Keep the rest of the foot still and ask for the direction after each movement. If impaired, test more proximal sites and integrate the sensory distribution, reflexes and motor findings.

What to say to the patient

  1. This is vibration, rather than just pressure. Close your eyes and tell me when the buzzing stops.
  2. I will move your big toe a little. With your eyes closed, tell me whether it moved up or down.

A positive finding

Reproducibly reduced vibration or incorrect movement-direction reports suggest large-fibre sensory pathway dysfunction. Peripheral nerves and central pathways are possible sites; the finding alone does not localise a lesion.

A negative finding

Intact hallux testing does not exclude small-fibre dysfunction, patchy sensory loss or an early neurological disorder. Vibration and position sense should be assessed separately.

Evidence & limitations

The two modalities share much of the dorsal-column pathway but have different receptors. No age-independent normal vibration duration or fixed toe-angle cutoff is assigned here.

Points to remember

  1. Do not grip the top and bottom of the toe: pressure cues can reveal the direction. Do not move the whole foot instead of the distal toe.
  2. The dorsal diagram shows the bony fork contact and side grips; the separate side inset shows illustrative movement. It does not simulate vibration, an answer or a measured range. Randomise direction in real testing.
