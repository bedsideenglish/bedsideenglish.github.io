Radial, brachial and carotid pulse sites

Locate upper-limb arterial pulses and examine each carotid separately, distinguishing the pulse from adjacent tendons and muscles.

See the examination

Compare the radial pulses at the thumb-side volar wrists.

Repeat on the left; supported arms stay relaxed.

Brachial: just medial to the biceps tendon; repeat opposite.

Right carotid: light pressure on one side only.

Release the right side completely before changing sides.

Left carotid: the right side is free of pressure.

How to do it

  1. Explain the examination and support the relaxed, supinated forearms. Compare radial pulses on both sides with the pads of the index and middle fingers.
  2. At the volar wrist, palpate just proximal to the base of the thumb. Adjust pressure gently; too much pressure can erase a weak pulse.
  3. With the elbow relaxed and slightly flexed, find the biceps tendon at the antecubital fossa. Palpate just medial to it for the brachial pulse; compare sides.
  4. For the carotid, relax the neck and use light finger-pad contact between the trachea and SCM. Palpate one side only, release completely, then examine the other side.
  5. Describe presence, symmetry and contour; count rate and rhythm with a clock when needed. Document sites that cannot be confidently palpated.

What to say to the patient

  1. Let your arms rest here with your palms up. I will feel the pulses in your wrists and elbows.
  2. I will gently check one side of your neck at a time. Tell me if you feel faint or uncomfortable.

A positive finding

A weak, absent, unequal or unusual pulse warrants confirmation and assessment for local arterial obstruction or altered haemodynamics. No single pulse contour establishes a diagnosis.

A negative finding

Palpable symmetrical pulses do not exclude arterial disease. Pulse palpation cannot reliably supply a systolic blood-pressure value.

Evidence & limitations

Surface locations follow clinical examination anatomy. Rate, amplitude and timing must be observed on the patient; the atlas contains no pulse waveform simulation.

Points to remember

  1. Do not use your own thumb pulse as the reference, press hard, massage the carotid sinus, or compress both carotids together.
  2. A tendon is not an artery. The faint tendon line at the elbow lies lateral to the brachial contact. The whole-body projection is schematic, not a millimetre placement guide.
