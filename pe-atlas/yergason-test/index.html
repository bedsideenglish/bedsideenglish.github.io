Yergason test: resisted forearm supination

Assess familiar bicipital-groove pain or palpable snapping during resisted supination.

How to do it

  1. Seat the patient with the upper arm at the side and elbow bent 90 degrees.
  2. Start with the forearm partly pronated. Palpate the long-head biceps tendon at the groove and hold the distal forearm/wrist with the other hand.
  3. Ask the patient to turn the palm up while you oppose that turn gradually. Keep the elbow angle and upper-arm position stable; the figure shows the supination component.
  4. Ask about focal pain and note any palpable snap. Release the load before returning to the starting position.

What to say to the patient

  1. Turn your palm up against my hand, keeping your elbow here. Do you feel your usual pain at the front of the shoulder?

A positive finding

Familiar groove pain supports biceps tendon involvement; a palpable snap raises concern for instability but needs further assessment.

A negative finding

No pain or snap does not exclude biceps or labral pathology.

Evidence & limitations

Published diagnostic performance is limited. A positive finding is not proof of rupture or a specific labral lesion.

Points to remember

  1. Do not confuse forearm supination with flexing the elbow or externally rotating the whole upper arm.
  2. This is the resisted-supination variant. Axial forearm rotation is drawn; tendon movement, measured torque and a snap are not simulated.
