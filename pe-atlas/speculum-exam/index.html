Speculum examination · vaginal route and cervical view

Distinguish external landmarks, closed-blade insertion, controlled opening, the cervical view and safe withdrawal.

See the examination

A · External surface with anterior at top. Vaginal introitus lies below the urethral opening; identify the route before insertion.

B · Sagittal section: closed blades follow the vaginal axis toward the posterior direction, avoiding the urethra. No fixed depth or angle is prescribed.

C · Open gently only after adequate insertion. Blades separate within the vagina and stop outside the cervical canal.

D · Separate direct cervical view: the external os is visible between the blades. Sampling uses separate indicated equipment; no sampling technique is simulated.

E · Unlock under observation, clear the cervix, close gently without trapping tissue, and withdraw while inspecting the walls.

How to do it

  1. Confirm a specific indication. Explain the external and internal contacts separately, obtain consent, offer an appropriate chaperone, provide private undressing and draping, and agree on a stop signal. Recheck permission as the examination proceeds; stop for pain or withdrawal of consent. A learner needs supervised training.
  2. Clarify the indication and any planned sample, discuss alternatives when appropriate, and use an appropriate size and comfortable position with supported legs. Emptying the bladder can improve comfort. Inspect external anatomy first; identify urethra, vaginal opening and anus separately.
  3. Use adequate light, clean gloves and an appropriately prepared warmed speculum with lubricant compatible with the intended testing and device/local instructions. Separate the labia gently and introduce the closed blades along the posterior vaginal axis, avoiding urethral pressure. Do not force a fixed depth or angle from the drawing.
  4. After adequate insertion, open gently only enough to visualize the cervix without pinching or over-opening. The blades remain in the vagina, outside the cervical canal. Pause, adjust or stop for discomfort.
  5. Observe the cervix, discharge/bleeding and vaginal walls and collect only indicated samples with the proper separate equipment. Speculum visualization is not bimanual palpation or a universal requirement for all discharge testing.
  6. Release the lock while observing tissue, clear the cervix, allow the blades to close gently without trapping tissue, and withdraw slowly while inspecting the walls. Offer privacy/tissues and record the findings, samples and tolerance.

What to say to the patient

  1. I would like to use a speculum to see the vaginal walls and cervix. I will explain each step, and we can pause or stop whenever you want.

A positive finding

Visible inflammation, discharge, bleeding or a lesion guides further testing; appearance alone does not establish a pathogen or exclude upper-tract disease.

A negative finding

A normal cervical and vaginal appearance does not exclude infection, pelvic inflammatory disease or a lesion outside the view.

Evidence & limitations

External, sagittal and direct cervical views are separate labelled projections. No instrument enters the cervical canal; depth, angle and opening are schematic.

Points to remember

  1. Do not base need for examination solely on sexual history, or force examination to complete a study checklist. Supervised practical training is necessary.
