Head & neck

4 techniques. Examine the neck, thyroid, lymph nodes and sinuses.

  • Thyroid palpation from behind, with a swallowFinds goitre and thyroid nodules and describes them: size, consistency, tenderness, whether the gland moves up with swallowing. The thyroid is fixed to the larynx and trachea, so it rises when the patient swallows; a lump that does not rise is probably not thyroid.Head & neck↗
  • Cervical lymph node palpationLooks for enlarged cervical lymph nodes and describes them, which helps separate reactive nodes (infection) from nodes that suggest lymphoma, metastasis or chronic disease. Each chain drains a known area, so the site of a node points to where to look.Head & neck↗
  • Frontal and maxillary sinus tendernessLocate facial tenderness over the frontal and maxillary regions when sinus symptoms warrant examination. Tenderness alone cannot identify bacterial sinusitis.Head & neck↗
  • Temporal artery · branches, pulse and tendernessLocate the superficial temporal artery and interpret tenderness, thickening and pulse changes in the clinical context of possible giant cell arteritis.Head & neck↗
