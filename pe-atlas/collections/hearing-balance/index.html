Hearing & balance

8 techniques. Explore bedside hearing tests and vestibular maneuvers.

  • Dix-Hallpike maneuverDiagnoses posterior-canal benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV). Turning the head and lying the patient back with the head hanging moves loose otoconia in the posterior canal and provokes vertigo with a characteristic burst of nystagmus.Hearing & balance↗
  • Head impulse test (HINTS)Checks the vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR) in a patient with acute continuous vertigo. A quick small head turn should keep the eyes locked on the target. If a catch-up saccade is needed, the VOR on that side is impaired, which points to a peripheral vestibular lesion; a normal impulse in a patient with acute vestibular syndrome is worrying for stroke.Hearing & balance↗
  • Weber test — sound lateralisationCompare where a bone-conducted tone is perceived. Together with Rinne and otoscopy, this helps classify asymmetric hearing loss before audiometry.Hearing & balance↗
  • Rinne test — air versus bone conductionCompare air-conducted (AC) with bone-conducted (BC) sound for each ear, looking for a conductive component. This card uses a rapid loudness comparison, not a timed sound-decay test.Hearing & balance↗
  • Whispered voice — one ear at a timeScreen functional hearing in a cooperative adult using whispered speech with the other ear masked. This detects possible hearing impairment; it does not determine its type or audiometric threshold.Hearing & balance↗
  • Supine roll test: horizontal-canal BPPVObserve positional horizontal nystagmus when horizontal-canal BPPV is suspected.Hearing & balance↗
  • Epley manoeuvre — right posterior-canal BPPVA clinician-assisted repositioning sequence for confirmed right posterior-canal BPPV. This is a treatment, not a general dizziness screen or the horizontal-canal roll test.Hearing & balance↗
  • Alternate cover test · vertical refixation (skew)Observe vertical refixation of the newly uncovered eye. In acute vestibular assessment this is one part of HINTS performed by trained clinicians in the appropriate continuous vestibular syndrome, not a stand-alone stroke screen.Hearing & balance↗
