Neurological

29 techniques. Explore strength, sensation, reflexes, coordination and meningeal signs.

  • Kernig's signA sign of meningeal irritation (meningitis, subarachnoid hemorrhage): with the hip flexed, straightening the knee stretches the inflamed lumbosacral nerve roots, so the patient resists because of pain.Neurological↗
  • Brudzinski's neck signA sign of meningeal irritation. Bending the neck forward stretches the inflamed meninges and nerve roots, so the patient flexes the hips and knees reflexly to relieve the traction.Neurological↗
  • Lower-limb power by nerve root (L2-S1)Tests muscle power joint by joint, each linked to a main nerve root, so a pattern of weakness points to a level: hip flexion L2, knee extension L3-L4, ankle dorsiflexion L4-L5, ankle plantarflexion S1. Power is graded 0 to 5 on the MRC scale.Neurological↗
  • Romberg testTests whether balance depends on proprioception (dorsal columns, peripheral nerves) rather than on vision. Standing steadily needs two of three inputs: vision, proprioception and the vestibular-cerebellar system. Closing the eyes removes vision, so a patient with a position-sense deficit starts to fall.Neurological↗
  • Finger-to-nose testTests upper-limb coordination. The patient alternately touches the examiner's finger and their own nose. A cerebellar lesion makes the movement overshoot (dysmetria) and the hand shake as it nears the target (intention tremor).Neurological↗
  • Heel-to-shin testTests lower-limb coordination. The patient puts one heel on the opposite knee and slides it smoothly down the shin to the ankle. A cerebellar lesion makes the heel wander off the shin (ataxia).Neurological↗
  • Patellar tendon reflex (L2-L4, mainly L4)Assess the quadriceps stretch reflex through the femoral nerve.Neurological↗
  • Achilles tendon reflex (mainly S1)Assess the ankle stretch reflex through the tibial nerve.Neurological↗
  • Ankle clonusLook for rhythmic involuntary contractions after sudden passive ankle dorsiflexion.Neurological↗
  • Biceps tendon reflex (C5-C6)Assess the biceps stretch reflex through the musculocutaneous nerve.Neurological↗
  • Light-touch sensation: compare sidesScreen for a reproducible pattern of altered light-touch sensation.Neurological↗
  • CN XI: shoulder shrug and resisted head turnAssess trapezius and sternocleidomastoid strength supplied by the accessory nerve.Neurological↗
  • Upper-limb power: shoulder and elbowCompare proximal upper-limb strength before completing wrist and hand testing.Neurological↗
  • Nuchal rigidity — gentle passive neck flexionAssess abnormal resistance to gentle passive neck flexion as one possible sign of meningeal irritation. This is an adjunct to the overall assessment of an adult with a concerning illness, not a test that can clear meningitis.Neurological↗
  • Triceps tendon reflex (mainly C7)Assess the radial-nerve stretch-reflex arc, mainly C7 with C8 contribution, by observing triceps contraction and involuntary elbow extension after a tendon tap.Neurological↗
  • Brachioradialis reflex (mainly C6)Assess the radial-nerve stretch-reflex arc, C5–C6 and mainly C6, by tapping the distal radial forearm and observing elbow flexion, sometimes with slight supination.Neurological↗
  • Side-lying hip abduction strengthAssess the hip abductors, principally gluteus medius/minimus supplied by the superior gluteal nerve (L4–S1). Stabilise the pelvis so trunk or pelvic movement does not substitute for hip abduction.Neurological↗
  • Pronator driftScreen for subtle asymmetric upper-limb motor dysfunction by observing both pronation and downward drift.Neurological↗
  • Rapid alternating forearm movementsAssess the speed, regularity and symmetry of alternating forearm rotation.Neurological↗
  • Asterixis: lapses of sustained postureLook for brief irregular failures to maintain wrist extension: negative myoclonus.Neurological↗
  • Plantar response: flexor versus extensor (Babinski)Elicit the plantar response with a controlled lateral-sole stroke and identify the first sustained movement of the great toe.Neurological↗
  • Hallux vibration and joint position senseTest two distinct large-fibre sensory modalities at a distal bony site, then move proximally if needed.Neurological↗
  • Lower-limb pinprick: selected L2–S1 key sensory pointsCompare sharp/dull discrimination at selected standard lower-limb sensory points, then map any abnormal pattern. These are sample points, not dermatome boundaries.Neurological↗
  • Trendelenburg sign — stance-side hip controlObserve pelvic control during single-leg stance, identifying the weight-bearing hip rather than labelling the lifted leg as the tested side.Neurological↗
  • Gait and tandem walkingObserve ordinary walking before narrowing the base to heel-to-toe tandem walking, with close fall protection.Neurological↗
  • Jolt accentuation of headacheAn optional meningeal-irritation adjunct in an alert patient with baseline headache; not a rule-out test for meningitis.Neurological↗
  • Facial motor examination · upper and lower faceCompare facial movement at rest and on command, describing the distribution rather than diagnosing Bell’s palsy or stroke from one facial pattern.Neurological↗
  • CN XII · tongue at rest, protrusion and lateral movementSeparate observation of tongue bulk and involuntary activity from movement tasks, and describe deviation using the patient’s side.Neurological↗
  • CN V · facial light touch and jaw motorCompare selected V1/V2/V3 sensory sites and distinguish jaw motor testing from sensation and corneal-reflex testing.Neurological↗
