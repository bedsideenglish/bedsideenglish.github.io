Respiratory

9 techniques. Follow chest inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation.

  • Chest expansion (posterior, thumbs)Compares how much the two sides of the lower chest move during a deep breath. Reduced or delayed movement on one side points to disease of that lung or pleura; reduced movement on both sides points to restrictive or hyperinflated chest disease.Respiratory↗
  • Posterior chest percussion (arms crossed)Uses the note from tapping on the back to tell air-filled lung (resonant) from fluid or solid lung (dull) and from hyperinflation or pneumothorax (hyperresonant). Crossing the arms in front of the chest moves the scapulae out of the way so more lung can be reached.Respiratory↗
  • Lung auscultation sequence (posterior)Listens to breath sounds and added sounds (crackles, wheeze, rhonchi, pleural rub) over all lung zones on the back, in a ladder pattern that compares the left and right side at each level.Respiratory↗
  • Anterior and lateral lung auscultationCompare anterior lung fields and lateral lower zones, including areas poorly sampled from the back.Respiratory↗
  • Tactile vocal fremitusCompare transmitted voice vibrations through matching areas of the chest wall.Respiratory↗
  • Egophony: E-to-A voice transmissionAssess altered voice transmission over a suspected area of consolidation.Respiratory↗
  • Diaphragmatic excursion by percussionEstimate the change in the posterior lower lung border between full expiration and full inspiration, and compare sides. This is an indirect bedside estimate, not direct diaphragm imaging.Respiratory↗
  • Tracheal position at the suprasternal notchCompare the spaces beside the lower cervical trachea with the head and chest aligned; this is a midline assessment, not carotid palpation.Respiratory↗
  • Bronchophony and whispered pectoriloquyCompare transmission of spoken and whispered words through the chest; complements the existing egophony card.Respiratory↗
