General examination

13 techniques. Start with the patient’s overall appearance, breathing and vital signs.

  • General inspection from the end of the bedRecognize distress before starting a detailed examination.General examination↗
  • Radial pulse: rate and rhythmAssess the peripheral pulse at the radial side of the wrist.General examination↗
  • Orthostatic blood pressure and pulseCheck the blood-pressure response to standing in a patient who can stand safely.General examination↗
  • Work of breathing and tripod postureObserve respiratory effort and the posture the patient chooses before disturbing them. Leaning forward with the arms braced can be a response to breathlessness; the posture does not identify its cause.General examination↗
  • Finger-pulp capillary refill timeAssess peripheral perfusion by blanching a fingertip and timing colour return after release. This card uses the finger-pulp method.General examination↗
  • Clubbing and Schamroth's windowInspect nail-fold profile and distal finger shape, then look for the window between opposed corresponding fingernails.General examination↗
  • Pulsus paradoxus: manual cuff measurementMeasure an exaggerated inspiratory fall in systolic pressure by comparing two cuff-pressure thresholds. This is not the ordinary systolic–diastolic pulse-pressure difference.General examination↗
  • GCS motor response — graded pressure and separate examplesUse the structured GCS approach: check, observe, stimulate only as needed, then report each component and the best motor response.General examination↗
  • Upper-arm blood pressure · technique and both-arm comparisonObtain a reproducible adult arm-cuff measurement. The panels separate seated preparation, cuff placement, manual auscultation and the initial comparison of arms.General examination↗
  • Pulse oximetry · placement, signal and limitationsEstimate peripheral oxygen saturation while checking whether the optical pulse signal is trustworthy. Oxygenation and ventilation require separate assessment.General examination↗
  • Capillary blood glucose · safe fingertip samplingObtain a point-of-care blood sample with a single-use safety lancet and recognise contamination, perfusion and device limitations. This is a sampling procedure.General examination↗
  • Respiratory rate · cycle, rhythm and effortCount actual spontaneous breaths and describe depth, rhythm, symmetry and effort, with awareness and oxygenation assessed alongside.General examination↗
  • Fluid status · oral mucosa and skin recoilUse oral moisture and a gentle skin-recoil observation as parts of a wider assessment of intake, losses and perfusion.General examination↗
