Eyes, ears, mouth & throat

13 techniques. Explore focused eye, ear and oral examinations and their limitations.

  • Pupillary light reflex and swinging-light testAssess direct/consensual light responses, then compare afferent input between eyes. A relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD) is a response asymmetry, not a difference in resting pupil size.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic gaze positionsObserve binocular conjugate movement and symptoms in the principal fields of action of CN III, IV and VI. The H diagram below the eyes is the target path; both drawn irises actually change position.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Confrontation visual fields: each eye, four quadrantsScreen monocular peripheral visual fields against the examiner’s field. The oval is the PATIENT’S field view, not a retinal map; the arrow shows a target entering a quadrant.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Adult otoscopy: safe approach and tympanic landmarksInspect the external canal and tympanic membrane (TM), distinguishing the insertion technique from the separate enlarged view of the membrane. This card teaches the adult technique.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Direct ophthalmoscopy: approach and fundus orientationTeach same-side hand/eye alignment, the temporal approach and an organised fundus survey. The enlarged retinal map is separate from the top-view approach and larger than one direct-ophthalmoscope field.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Mouth, pharynx and palatal movementInspect oral/pharyngeal structures and compare palatal elevation during phonation. Voice, palatal movement and swallowing symptoms contribute to CN IX/X assessment; gag is a separate, selective manoeuvre.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Monocular visual acuity and pinholeMeasure each eye’s central distance acuity with its correction recorded, then assess pinhole improvement when acuity is reduced. The drawn E chart is illustrative and cannot measure vision.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Lower-lid conjunctiva and ocular icterusLocate the palpebral conjunctiva for pallor and the conjunctiva over the white of the eye for yellowing. These are visual clues that require laboratory and clinical correlation.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Anterior rhinoscopy · vestibule and visible landmarksInspect the anterior nasal cavity safely, distinguishing septum, inferior turbinate and floor. Anterior inspection is limited and does not visualize the whole nose, nasopharynx or sinuses.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Corneal fluorescein · epithelial defects and leak recognitionShow safe dye placement and distinguish focal, linear and punctate epithelial staining from a separate aqueous-leak example.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Penlight anterior-eye inspection · surface, cornea and shadowIdentify the structures seen with a penlight and the observations requiring a slit lamp, pressure measurement or gonioscopy.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • Lagophthalmos · lid closure and corneal exposureSeparate incomplete eyelid closure, upward rotation and corneal surface injury from a facial motor-strength assessment.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
  • External ear and mastoid · inspection and gentle contactsDistinguish pinna movement, tragal pressure and postauricular examination; recognise findings needing prompt assessment.Eyes, ears, mouth & throat↗
