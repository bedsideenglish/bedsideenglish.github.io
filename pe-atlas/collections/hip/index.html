Hip

6 techniques. Compare hip positioning, range of motion and provocative tests.

  • FABER (Patrick's) testProvokes pain from the hip joint and the sacroiliac joint. The hip is flexed, abducted and externally rotated into a figure-4 position (the foot rests on the opposite knee), then the knee is pressed toward the bed while the opposite pelvis is steadied.Hip↗
  • FADIR: hip flexion, adduction and internal rotationProvoke familiar hip pain during flexion, adduction and internal rotation.Hip↗
  • Hip log roll — passive rotation and painGently rotate the relaxed, extended leg as a unit to see whether hip rotation reproduces familiar pain. This card examines pain with passive rotation, rather than the separate dial-test interpretation of capsular laxity.Hip↗
  • Thomas test (hip flexion contracture)Assess whether the examined hip can reach extension while the opposite hip is flexed to control pelvic/lumbar compensation. This is the supine, on-table Thomas test, not the modified hanging-leg version.Hip↗
  • Ober test: supported hip adductionAssess passive hip adduction with the pelvis stabilised in side lying.Hip↗
  • Leg length: true and apparentUse consistent endpoints to distinguish bony-length estimates from apparent asymmetry.Hip↗
