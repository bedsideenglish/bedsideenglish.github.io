Pronator drift

Screen for subtle asymmetric upper-limb motor dysfunction by observing both pronation and downward drift.

See the examination

Eyes open: both palms up and arms level

Close eyes; observe for about 10 s (time compressed)

Illustrative right pronation AND downward drift

Open eyes and restore the starting posture

Drag to rotate freely

How to do it

  1. Seat the patient securely. Check that shoulder pain or restricted movement will not prevent the starting posture.
  2. Ask for both arms straight ahead at shoulder height, elbows extended and palms up. Establish symmetry with eyes open.
  3. Ask the patient to close the eyes and maintain the position for about 10 seconds. Observe both palms and arm height without pushing the arms.
  4. Record the side and whether pronation accompanies downward drift; then let the patient lower the arms.

What to say to the patient

  1. Hold both arms out with your palms up. Now close your eyes and keep them there.

A positive finding

Pronation with downward drift supports corticospinal dysfunction. Interpret the pattern with power, reflexes and sensation; new focal weakness needs prompt assessment.

A negative finding

No drift during this observation does not exclude a small or transient motor deficit.

Evidence & limitations

This is a pattern-recognition examination, not an isolated diagnosis or an NIHSS motor-arm score.

Points to remember

  1. Separate fatigue, pain and poor understanding from neurological drift.
  2. The right-sided drift is an illustrative abnormal example. Animation time is compressed; its angle and speed are not diagnostic cutoffs.
