Jolt accentuation of headache

An optional meningeal-irritation adjunct in an alert patient with baseline headache; not a rule-out test for meningitis.

See the examination

A · Establish headache at rest; the patient must be able to describe it.

B · Patient actively turns toward their right; stop if uncomfortable.

C · Alternate toward the left and ask about worsening of the original headache.

How to do it

  1. Assess urgent illness first. Do not delay meningitis evaluation/treatment for this maneuver.
  2. Confirm alertness, ability to report baseline headache and a safe comfortable neck; avoid with suspected cervical injury or inability to cooperate.
  3. Ask the patient to actively rotate the head horizontally left and right at about two to three rotations per second, briefly and only if tolerated.
  4. Ask whether the original headache becomes worse, then stop and return to neutral. Stop immediately for neck pain, dizziness or distress.
  5. Record baseline headache and the response or why untestable. Integrate with the whole clinical assessment.

What to say to the patient

  1. Only if comfortable, gently turn your head side to side. Does your usual headache get worse?

A positive finding

Worsening of the pre-existing headache is the described positive response; it is not specific to bacterial meningitis.

A negative finding

No worsening does not exclude meningitis; absence of baseline headache or inability to report makes the maneuver uninformative rather than negative.

Evidence & limitations

The Cochrane review found heterogeneous, very-low-certainty accuracy and inadequate sensitivity for exclusion. No numerical rule-out claim is made here.

Points to remember

  1. No examiner-imposed rapid head shaking.
  2. These are held top-view directions, not a real-speed animation, vestibular head-impulse test or a diagnostic nystagmus demonstration.
