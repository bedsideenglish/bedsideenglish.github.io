Mouth, pharynx and palatal movement

Inspect oral/pharyngeal structures and compare palatal elevation during phonation. Voice, palatal movement and swallowing symptoms contribute to CN IX/X assessment; gag is a separate, selective manoeuvre.

How to do it

  1. Explain, obtain consent and ask about oral pain, voice change and swallowing symptoms. Use good light, hand hygiene and appropriate precautions.
  2. Ask the patient to open the mouth. Inspect lips, teeth/gums, buccal mucosa, tongue, floor of mouth, palate, tonsillar region and posterior pharynx in an organised sequence.
  3. If a tongue blade is needed, gently depress the anterior tongue, avoiding unnecessary posterior contact. Ask for a sustained “ah” and compare both palatal arches and the uvula.
  4. Listen for hoarseness or a nasal voice and ask about choking/nasal regurgitation. Palatal asymmetry needs correlation with the remainder of the cranial-nerve examination.
  5. Only if indicated and tolerated, explain a separate gag test and gently stimulate each posterior pharyngeal side. A gag response does not establish safe swallowing. The final panel is a separate right-weakness example.

What to say to the patient

  1. Please open your mouth, then say “ah” for a few seconds.
  2. Tell me if you feel pain or need a break.

A positive finding

On unilateral vagal palatal weakness the affected side elevates less and the uvula is generally pulled toward the intact side. Structural lesions can also distort the palate; describe the finding before localising it.

A negative finding

Symmetric elevation and a midline uvula are reassuring for this component. They do not exclude all bulbar dysfunction; bilateral absence of gag can occur in healthy adults.

Evidence & limitations

Gag afference is predominantly IX and palatal/pharyngeal efference X. Gag is neither a routine requirement for every sore throat nor a stand-alone aspiration screen. No test-performance number is assigned here.

Points to remember

  1. In suspected epiglottitis or significant airway compromise, avoid provocative throat examination and prioritise airway assessment.
  2. The drawing shows a schematic open mouth, not actual tissue colour, tonsil size, larynx or a simulated gag.
