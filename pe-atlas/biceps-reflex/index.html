Biceps tendon reflex (C5-C6)

Assess the biceps stretch reflex through the musculocutaneous nerve.

See the examination

Support the relaxed arm; place your thumb over the biceps tendon

Tap the supporting thumb

Example response: biceps contraction and elbow flexion

Loading examination…

Drag to rotate freely

How to do it

  1. Support the relaxed arm with the elbow partly flexed.
  2. Locate the biceps tendon in the antecubital fossa and place your thumb over it.
  3. Strike your thumb with a reflex hammer; observe or feel biceps contraction and elbow flexion.
  4. Compare sides and verify relaxation before recording absence.

What to say to the patient

  1. Let your arm go loose in my hand.
  2. I will tap my thumb over the tendon at your elbow.

A positive finding

Asymmetric loss can reflect a C5-C6 reflex-arc lesion; briskness needs comparison with other neurologic signs.

A negative finding

A normal biceps reflex does not exclude radiculopathy or peripheral nerve disease.

Evidence & limitations

Use the pattern across several reflexes rather than one isolated response to localize a lesion.

Points to remember

  1. Do not hit the patient’s antecubital fossa directly with force.
  2. Strike briefly and allow rebound. The movement is an illustrative response, not a reflex grade or measured amplitude.
