Alternate cover test · vertical refixation (skew)

Observe vertical refixation of the newly uncovered eye. In acute vestibular assessment this is one part of HINTS performed by trained clinicians in the appropriate continuous vestibular syndrome, not a stand-alone stroke screen.

See the examination

A · Abnormal sequence: R is opaque-covered; L fixes on the target. Covered-eye position is not visible.

B · Transfer cover to L: newly uncovered R makes a downward corrective movement (arrow).

C · R has refixed; L stays covered. Hold the target still.

D · Transfer cover to R: newly uncovered L makes an upward correction (arrow).

E · L has refixed. Repeat alternation and document real findings.

F · Separate comparison: no vertical refixation observed. This does not rule out stroke.

How to do it

  1. Establish the timing and triggers of dizziness and examine for nystagmus. HINTS is for trained clinicians assessing acute continuous vestibular syndrome with nystagmus; it is not for every brief, positional or resolved dizzy spell.
  2. Keep the head still and ask the patient to fix on a stationary target. Use an opaque paddle without pressing the globe.
  3. Cover one eye, then transfer the cover directly to the other eye, maintaining dissociation. Watch the eye just uncovered, not the covered one, for an upward or downward corrective movement.
  4. Alternate several times, allowing fixation after each switch. Distinguish vertical correction from horizontal phoria, nystagmus or movement caused by following the paddle. Record the direction and affected eye.
  5. A central or equivocal HINTS pattern needs urgent stroke-directed assessment. If untrained, fixation is inadequate or the test is uninterpretable, do not call it normal or use it to reassure.

What to say to the patient

  1. Keep looking at the fixed target while I move this cover from one eye to the other.

A positive finding

Vertical corrective movement is evidence of vertical ocular misalignment. In the appropriate acute vestibular context it raises concern for central disease; pre-existing strabismus and peripheral causes can also confound interpretation.

A negative finding

No visible skew does not exclude posterior-circulation stroke. The complete trained examination, hearing and clinical context remain necessary.

Evidence & limitations

The abnormal sequence exaggerates reciprocal vertical refixation: R down after uncovering, then L up. The final no-vertical-movement panel is a separate example; no diagnostic accuracy is claimed for this drawing.

Points to remember

  1. This is alternate cover testing, not the pupillary swinging-flashlight test. The covered iris is hidden and its position must not be inferred from a transparent paddle.
