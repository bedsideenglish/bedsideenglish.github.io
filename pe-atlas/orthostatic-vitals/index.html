Orthostatic blood pressure and pulse

How to do it

  1. Have the patient lie supine for 5 minutes; measure blood pressure and pulse.
  2. Assist the patient to stand, keeping support available.
  3. Repeat blood pressure and pulse after 1 and 3 minutes standing, supporting the cuffed arm at heart level; record symptoms.
  4. Stop and help the patient lie down if faint or unsafe; assess persistent symptoms even if measurements are normal.

What to say to the patient

  1. I will check your blood pressure lying down, then standing.
  2. Tell me straight away if you feel dizzy; I will help you sit or lie down.

A positive finding

A sustained fall of at least 20 mmHg systolic or 10 mmHg diastolic within 3 minutes supports orthostatic hypotension; symptoms without this fall still need assessment.

A negative finding

Normal readings do not exclude an early, delayed or intermittent postural disorder.

Evidence & limitations

Compare with the supine baseline; this protocol alone does not diagnose POTS.

Points to remember

  1. Do not test standing if unsafe. Stop and assist the patient if symptomatic.
  2. Animation time is compressed: use a real timer. The seated transfer is for positioning; it does not replace the supine baseline or start the standing timer.
