You know the medicine. Then someone asks “Quickly — how's bed 12?” and the English will not come out.

Before you download

The awkward questions.

Can I try it without an API key?

Yes. Demo Mode ships with three scripted patients and runs immediately — no key, no account, no network setup. It is there so you can see whether the flow suits you before deciding anything.

Why do I have to bring my own Gemini key?

Because the alternative is a subscription. Google's free tier covers casual practice, the key takes about two minutes to create, and it is stored on your phone only — it never reaches a server of ours, because there is no server of ours.

I am not a medical student. Is this useful?

Survival English is a whole mode of everyday scenes — a café, a phone call, a movie night — with the same live voice, the same scoring, and the same spaced review. The clinical side is simply the part that needed a checklist.

Where do my recordings and transcripts go?

They stay on your device. Speech is sent to Google under your own key for analysis, and nothing else leaves the phone: no account, no ads, no telemetry. The source is public if you would rather check than take our word for it.

Is this a medical device?

No. It is an English-speaking practice tool. The clinical checklists exist to give the conversation a shape, not to teach medicine or support any real clinical decision.
