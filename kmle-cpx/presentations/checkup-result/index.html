Prioritize the patient's condition. An abnormal screening value alone does not establish an emergency. Acute chest pain, shortness of breath, new neurologic abnormalities, or circulatory instability requires urgent assessment before discussion of the results.

Abnormal screening results: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Which test was abnormal, the specific abnormality and value, and how the patient learned the result

    What to say to the patient

    Which test was abnormal, and what were you told was wrong? Do you remember the number?

  2. 02

    Comparison with previous screening results: new finding or previously present

    What to say to the patient

    Were you told about the same finding at a previous checkup?

  3. 03

    Circumstances at testing: fasting status, anxiety, recent coffee or smoking, recent cold, or strenuous exercise

    What to say to the patient

    Were you fasting for the test? Had you had a cold or done strenuous exercise around that time?

  4. 04

    Repeat measurements at home or elsewhere, such as home blood pressure or glucose

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you checked your blood pressure or blood sugar again at home or at a pharmacy?

  5. 05

    Associated symptoms: headache, fatigue, thirst or increased urination, and weight changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had headaches, fatigue, unusual thirst, or frequent urination lately?

  6. 06

    Symptoms of complications: chest pain, shortness of breath, visual changes, unilateral weakness or slurred speech, and foamy urine

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had chest pain, shortness of breath, weakness on one side, or slurred speech?

  7. 07

    Symptoms relevant to the results: stones, bone pain or constipation; cold intolerance or weight gain; edema or urinary changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had any stones or aching bones? Have you been unusually sensitive to cold or noticed swelling?

  8. 08

    Weight loss, night sweats, fever, palpable lumps, or hemoptysis

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost weight without trying or had night sweats? Have you noticed any lumps?

  9. 09

    Urinary symptoms, recent prostatitis or urinary tract infection, and ejaculation or cycling just before testing

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: male patients

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had difficulty urinating or a recent urinary tract infection? Did you have sex or ride a bicycle the day before the test?

  10. 10

    Clues to secondary causes: young age at onset, poor control despite medication, muscle weakness or cramps, palpitations and sweating, or snoring

    What to say to the patient

    Is your blood pressure still high despite medication? Do you often have leg cramps or weakness?

  11. 11

    Medications that may affect the results: NSAIDs, steroids, birth control pills, diuretics, calcium or vitamin D supplements, and cold medicines

    What to say to the patient

    Do you regularly take any pain relievers, steroids, cold medicines, or supplements?

  12. 12

    Eating habits (salty or fatty foods and sugary drinks), physical activity, and recent weight gain

    What to say to the patient

    What do you usually eat? Do you eat salty foods or drink sugary drinks often? How much do you exercise?

  13. 13

    Smoking duration and amount in pack-years, and occupational exposure to dust or asbestos

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    How many years have you smoked, and how much per day? Have you been exposed to dust or asbestos at work?

  14. 14

    Fractures, loss of height, age at menopause, and long-term steroid use

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever broken a bone after a fall? Has anyone told you that you've lost height?

  15. 15

    Contact with tuberculosis, previous tuberculosis treatment, and immunocompromise, including diabetes or immunosuppressant use

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been around anyone with tuberculosis, or been treated for it yourself?

  16. 16

    Family history of early heart disease or stroke, diabetes, cancer, or kidney disease

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family had heart disease or a stroke at a young age? What about cancer or kidney disease?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Repeat blood pressure after rest and compare both arms if indicated

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  2. 02

    Height, weight, BMI, and waist circumference

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Auscultate heart sounds, murmurs, and neck and abdominal bruits; palpate peripheral pulses, including femoral pulses

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Lower limb peripheral pulses: femoral, popliteal, and pedal ↗
    • Auscultate for carotid and abdominal bruits Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Palpate the thyroid and lymph nodes, and assess for hepatosplenomegaly

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
    • Splenic palpation in the right lateral position ↗
    • Axillary lymph nodes: arm support and palpation by group ↗
    • Palpate inguinal lymph nodes Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Assess spinal tenderness and observe for kyphosis

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Midline spinal and paraspinal tenderness ↗
  6. 06

    In male patients, perform a digital rectal examination if indicated to assess prostate size and nodules

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: rectal wall and prostate ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
