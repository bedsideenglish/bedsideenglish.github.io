Prioritize the patient's condition. Drooling, tripod positioning, trismus, or inspiratory stridor are critical signs of upper airway obstruction and raise concern for acute epiglottitis or deep neck infection. Do not stimulate the pharynx with a tongue depressor. Prepare immediately for airway management and emergency clinical assessment.

Sore throat: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset and course

    What to say to the patient

    When did your throat start hurting? Is it getting worse?

  2. 02

    Pain severity; whether one side is particularly painful

    What to say to the patient

    How bad is the pain? Is it about the same on both sides, or much worse on one side?

  3. 03

    Painful swallowing; inability to swallow water or saliva; drooling

    What to say to the patient

    Is it hard even to swallow saliva or water? Are you drooling because you can't swallow?

  4. 04

    Difficulty breathing, noisy breathing, or a muffled voice

    What to say to the patient

    Is it hard to breathe, or do you hear a noisy or whistling sound when you breathe? Does your voice sound muffled, as if you're talking with something in your mouth?

  5. 05

    Difficulty opening the mouth

    What to say to the patient

    Is it hard to open your mouth wide?

  6. 06

    Fever

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever? How high has your temperature been?

  7. 07

    Cough, runny nose, nasal congestion, or conjunctivitis

    What to say to the patient

    Do you also have a cough, a runny nose, or a stuffy nose?

  8. 08

    Cervical lymph node swelling or tenderness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed any swelling or tender areas along the sides of your neck?

  9. 09

    Skin rash, including a sandpaper-like rash

    What to say to the patient

    Have you developed a rash anywhere on your body?

  10. 10

    Severe fatigue or left upper abdominal pain

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been very tired or low on energy? Have you had pain in the upper left part of your abdomen?

  11. 11

    Pain radiating to the ear

    What to say to the patient

    Does the pain reach your ear?

  12. 12

    Similar illness among contacts at home, school, or work

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone at home, school, or work had similar symptoms?

  13. 13

    Frequency of recurrent pharyngitis or tonsillitis

    What to say to the patient

    How many times a year does your throat get this sore?

  14. 14

    Heartburn, a lump sensation in the throat, or throat clearing in chronic symptoms

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have heartburn or feel as if something is stuck in your throat? Do you clear your throat often?

  15. 15

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about oral sex and new partners

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone these questions because they can affect your care. Have you recently had sex or oral sex with a new partner?

  16. 16

    Immunocompromise; immunization status in children

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Are your immunizations up to date? Do you have any condition or take any medicine that weakens your immune system?

  17. 17

    Medication allergies, especially penicillin

    What to say to the patient

    Are you allergic to any antibiotics or other medications?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
