Prioritize the patient's condition. Rapid cognitive decline, fluctuations in consciousness over the day, recent head trauma, a new severe headache, or one-sided neurologic deficits require prompt comprehensive assessment to evaluate for emergencies such as acute delirium, chronic subdural hematoma, or stroke.

Memory loss: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    With the patient's consent, also ask accompanying family members about the symptoms

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Would it be all right if I also asked the person who came with you about how you usually are?

  2. 02

    Onset and progression: gradual, stepwise deterioration, or sudden onset

    What to say to the patient

    When did you first notice the memory problems? Have they gotten steadily worse, or did they worsen suddenly?

  3. 03

    Specific memory problems: forgetting recent events, repeating questions, or misplacing belongings

    What to say to the patient

    What kinds of things do you forget? Recent events? Do you ask the same question several times?

  4. 04

    Other cognitive difficulties: getting lost, word-finding problems, or trouble with calculations or managing money

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get lost in familiar places or have trouble finding words when you speak?

  5. 05

    Changes in daily functioning: cooking, managing medications, driving, or using public transportation

    What to say to the patient

    Can you still cook, manage your medications, and handle your banking on your own as you used to?

  6. 06

    Sudden deterioration; fluctuations in mental clarity over the day

    What to say to the patient

    Have they suddenly become confused over the past few days, or does their mental clarity change over the course of a day?

  7. 07

    Personality or behavioral changes, visual hallucinations, or suspiciousness and delusions

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed personality changes, seeing things that aren't there, or unusual suspicion of others?

  8. 08

    Depressed mood, reduced interest, or changes in sleep or appetite

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been feeling down or lost interest in things you used to enjoy?

  9. 09

    Slowed or shuffling gait; urinary incontinence

    What to say to the patient

    Has your walking slowed down, have you been dragging your feet, or have you had trouble controlling your urine?

  10. 10

    Recent head injury or new headache

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently fallen or hit your head? Have you had a headache?

  11. 11

    One-sided limb weakness or slurred speech

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had weakness in an arm or leg on one side, or slurred speech?

  12. 12

    Cold intolerance, constipation, weight gain, or tingling in the hands and feet

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been more sensitive to cold, constipated, or had tingling in your hands or feet lately?

  13. 13

    Infectious symptoms such as fever, urinary symptoms, or cough

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever, discomfort when urinating, or a cough?

  14. 14

    Recently added medications, such as sleep aids, anticholinergics, or pain medications

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently taken sleeping pills or started any new medication?

  15. 15

    Amount and duration of alcohol use

    What to say to the patient

    How often do you drink alcohol, and how much?

  16. 16

    History of hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, or stroke

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have high blood pressure, diabetes, or high cholesterol? Have you ever had a stroke?

  17. 17

    Safety concerns: leaving the stove on, getting lost, or driving accidents

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever left the stove on and forgotten about it, or gotten lost while driving?

  18. 18

    Family history of dementia

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family had dementia?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
