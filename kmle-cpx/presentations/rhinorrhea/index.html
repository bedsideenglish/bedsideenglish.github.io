Runny or stuffy nose: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset and duration, including the 10-day and 12-week thresholds

    What to say to the patient

    When did the runny or stuffy nose start? How long has it been going on?

  2. 02

    Initial improvement followed by worsening

    What to say to the patient

    Did it seem to improve, then get worse again?

  3. 03

    Nasal discharge color and character: clear, yellow or green, or foul-smelling

    What to say to the patient

    Is the discharge clear and watery, or thick and yellow? Does it have a bad smell?

  4. 04

    Unilateral or bilateral symptoms; severity and alternating sides of nasal obstruction

    What to say to the patient

    Are both nostrils blocked, or just one? Is it always the same side?

  5. 05

    Bloody nasal discharge or nosebleeds, progressive unilateral nasal obstruction, or facial numbness

    What to say to the patient

    Is there blood in your nasal discharge, or do you get nosebleeds? Is blockage on one side getting worse, or has your face felt numb?

  6. 06

    Sneezing; itchy nose or eyes; tearing

    What to say to the patient

    Do you sneeze repeatedly or have an itchy nose, itchy eyes, or watery eyes?

  7. 07

    Seasonal or morning worsening; triggers such as dust, pollen, animals, or cold air

    What to say to the patient

    Are symptoms worse in a particular season or in the morning? Do dust, pollen, or animals make them worse?

  8. 08

    Cheek or forehead pain or pressure, worse with bending forward; upper tooth pain

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have a heavy, aching feeling in your cheeks or forehead that gets worse when you bend forward? Do your upper teeth hurt?

  9. 09

    Reduced sense of smell

    What to say to the patient

    Is your sense of smell worse than it used to be?

  10. 10

    Fever

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever? How high has your temperature been?

  11. 11

    Periorbital swelling, decreased vision or double vision, severe headache, or neck stiffness

    What to say to the patient

    Is the area around your eyes swollen, or is your vision blurry or doubled? Have you had a severe headache or a stiff neck?

  12. 12

    Snoring, sleeping with the mouth open, or witnessed apneas

    What to say to the patient

    Do you snore or sleep with your mouth open? Has anyone said you stop breathing in your sleep?

  13. 13

    Postnasal drip, cough, or wheezing

    What to say to the patient

    Does mucus run down the back of your throat? Have you had a cough or wheezing?

  14. 14

    Duration of nasal decongestant spray use

    What to say to the patient

    Do you use a nasal decongestant spray? For how long?

  15. 15

    Asthma; worsening breathlessness or nasal congestion after aspirin or NSAIDs

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have asthma? Have aspirin or pain relievers ever made you short of breath or made your nose more blocked?

  16. 16

    History of nasal trauma or nasal or sinus surgery; clear watery discharge after trauma

    What to say to the patient

    Have you injured your nose or had nasal surgery? Has clear fluid been draining since an injury?

  17. 17

    Recent dental treatment or upper tooth infection

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had work done on your upper teeth, or had a toothache?

  18. 18

    Personal or family history of eczema, asthma, or allergic rhinitis

    What to say to the patient

    Do you or any family members have hay fever, asthma, or eczema?

  19. 19

    For children: foul-smelling discharge from one nostril, possible nasal foreign body

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child have smelly discharge from just one nostril? Could they have put something in their nose?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
