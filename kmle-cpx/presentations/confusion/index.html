Prioritize the patient's condition. With an acute change in consciousness, prioritize breathing, circulation, and immediate checks such as blood glucose and oxygen saturation. Avoid forceful neck movement if trauma is possible, and do not delay urgent evaluation to finish a detailed neurologic examination.

Altered consciousness: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Baseline cognition and daily functioning, including preexisting dementia

    What to say to the patient

    What are they usually like? Were they able to eat on their own and hold a conversation without difficulty?

  2. 02

    Onset and speed: sudden, over days, or over weeks; fluctuations throughout the day

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Was it sudden or gradual? Does it get better and worse during the day?

  3. 03

    Level of consciousness: persistent drowsiness or delayed responses; attention and orientation; visual hallucinations

    What to say to the patient

    Have they been very sleepy or slow to respond when called? Have they had trouble recognizing people or places, or seen things that aren't there?

  4. 04

    Recent falls or head trauma, including weeks earlier; anticoagulant or antiplatelet use

    What to say to the patient

    Have you fallen or hit your head in the past few weeks? Do you take any blood thinners?

  5. 05

    One-sided weakness, slurred speech, facial asymmetry, or abnormal gait

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had weakness in an arm or leg on one side, slurred speech, or a crooked smile?

  6. 06

    Headache or vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Have they complained of a headache or vomited?

  7. 07

    Convulsions, tongue biting, urinary incontinence, or episodes of staring and unresponsiveness

    What to say to the patient

    Have they had any shaking or seizures? Have they bitten their tongue or lost control of their urine?

  8. 08

    Fever, cough or sputum, urinary symptoms, or changes in urine odor or color

    What to say to the patient

    Have they had a fever or cough? Have they had pain when urinating, or has their urine smelled unusually strong?

  9. 09

    Neck stiffness, photophobia, or red spots on the skin

    What to say to the patient

    Have they complained of a stiff neck or been bothered by bright light? Have you noticed any red spots on their skin?

  10. 10

    Diabetes treatment; hypoglycemia risks, such as missed meals, insulin, or sulfonylureas; thirst and increased urination

    What to say to the patient

    Do they have diabetes? If they use insulin or take diabetes medicine, have they missed any meals? Have they been drinking a lot of water?

  11. 11

    Food and fluid intake, vomiting or diarrhea, or reduced urine output

    What to say to the patient

    Have they been eating and drinking normally? Have they had diarrhea or vomiting, or been urinating less?

  12. 12

    Recently started or changed medications, such as sleep aids, pain medications, anticholinergics, antipsychotics, or diuretics; medication errors

    What to say to the patient

    Have they recently started or changed any medications? Is there any chance they took too much medication at once?

  13. 13

    Alcohol intake and time of last drink; possible medication overdose, including empty pill bottles

    What to say to the patient

    How much alcohol do they drink? When was their last drink? Were there any empty pill bottles nearby?

  14. 14

    Poor nutrition, unsteady gait, or double vision

    What to say to the patient

    Have they been eating very little lately? Have they been unsteady or mentioned seeing double?

  15. 15

    Heater or gas exposure with similar symptoms in household members; pesticides; hot environments

    What to say to the patient

    Do you use a charcoal briquette or gas heater at home? Has anyone else there had a headache or felt dizzy?

  16. 16

    History of liver or kidney disease; yellow eyes or skin; abdominal swelling

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever been told you have liver or kidney problems? Have your eyes turned yellow or has your abdomen become swollen?

  17. 17

    Psychiatric history or similar previous episodes

    What to say to the patient

    Has this happened before? Have you ever received mental health treatment?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Check vital signs and oxygen saturation, and obtain a fingerstick glucose immediately

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Capillary blood glucose · safe fingerstick sampling ↗
    • Respiratory rate, rhythm, and effort ↗
    • Pulse oximetry: placement, signal quality, and limitations ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess level of consciousness using the GCS, orientation, and attention

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • GCS motor response — graded pressure and separate response examples ↗
    • Assess GCS eye and verbal responses, orientation, and attention Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Assess pupil size and light responses, eye movements, limb strength, and one-sided weakness

    • Pupillary light reflex and swinging flashlight test (RAPD) ↗
    • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic positions of gaze ↗
    • Plantar response: flexor and extensor (Babinski) ↗
    • Upper limb strength: shoulder and elbow ↗
    • Lower limb strength testing (nerve roots L2–S1) ↗
  4. 04

    Assess for meningeal signs, including neck stiffness

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Neck stiffness: gentle passive neck flexion ↗
  5. 05

    Look for evidence of head trauma and tongue biting

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Assess for dehydration, including a dry tongue; jaundice; alcohol or ketone breath odor; and asterixis

    Select as clinically indicated

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Asterixis ↗
    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
    • Fluid status: oral mucosa and skin turgor ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
