Prioritize the patient's condition. Prompt obstetric assessment is needed for vaginal bleeding accompanied by severe abdominal pain or syncope; severe headache or visual changes; or decreased fetal movement. Do not routinely perform a digital vaginal examination for unexplained bleeding during pregnancy.

Concerning symptoms during pregnancy: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Gestational age, estimated due date, and whether prenatal care has been received

    What to say to the patient

    How many weeks pregnant are you now? Have you been having regular prenatal checkups?

  2. 02

    Symptom onset and precipitating events, such as intercourse, a fall or abdominal trauma, or overexertion

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Before it began, had you fallen, hit your abdomen, or had sex?

  3. 03

    Amount of bleeding (number of pads), color, and passage of clots or tissue

    What to say to the patient

    How much are you bleeding? How many pads have you soaked? Have you passed anything like clots?

  4. 04

    Pattern and intervals of abdominal pain or uterine contractions, and whether the abdomen stays persistently firm

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have abdominal pain? Does your abdomen tighten and relax at regular intervals, or does it stay hard and painful?

  5. 05

    Vaginal fluid leakage: amount, color, and odor

    What to say to the patient

    Are you leaking watery fluid from your vagina? How much is there, and what color and smell does it have?

  6. 06

    Decreased or absent fetal movement

    What to say to the patient

    Are you feeling the baby move as usual? Has the movement decreased?

  7. 07

    Severe headache, visual changes, epigastric or right upper quadrant pain, and suddenly worsening edema

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a severe headache or seen flashing lights? Have you had pain in the upper middle part of your abdomen or sudden swelling of your face and hands?

  8. 08

    Dizziness, syncope, palpitations, and shortness of breath to help assess blood loss

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel dizzy or like you might pass out? Is your heart racing, or are you short of breath?

  9. 09

    Fever, foul-smelling discharge, and abdominal tenderness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or foul-smelling discharge?

  10. 10

    Previous ultrasound findings: placental location, cervical length, multiple gestation, and fetal size

    What to say to the patient

    Were you told at an earlier ultrasound that the placenta was low or your cervix was short?

  11. 11

    Prenatal test results: blood pressure, gestational diabetes testing, blood type and Rh status, and urine protein

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been told anything about your blood pressure, blood sugar, or blood type at your prenatal visits?

  12. 12

    Thirst and increased urination, eating habits, and weight gain during pregnancy

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been drinking a lot or urinating more often? How much weight have you gained during pregnancy?

  13. 13

    Previous pregnancies and deliveries: cesarean delivery, miscarriage or preterm birth, preeclampsia, or gestational diabetes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been pregnant before? Did you have a cesarean delivery, miscarriage, preterm birth, or blood pressure problems?

  14. 14

    If postpartum: delivery method, duration of labor, birth weight, and whether the placenta was delivered completely

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    How did you deliver? How long was labor, and how much did the baby weigh in kilograms?

  15. 15

    Smoking or drug use, multiple gestation, and chronic hypertension or diabetes

    What to say to the patient

    Do you smoke, or are you carrying twins? Did you have high blood pressure or diabetes before pregnancy?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
