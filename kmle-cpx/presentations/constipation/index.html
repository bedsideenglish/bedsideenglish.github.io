Prioritize the patient's condition. Inability to pass gas with severe distention, vomiting, or persistent abdominal pain requires prompt assessment for serious causes such as bowel obstruction. Do not delay assessment by repeating examinations or completing a rectal examination.

Constipation: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Bowel movement frequency, stool consistency, and degree of straining

    What to say to the patient

    How often do you have a bowel movement? Is the stool hard, and do you have to strain a lot?

  2. 02

    Onset and whether symptoms are new

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Were things normal before that?

  3. 03

    Incomplete evacuation and need for manual assistance to pass stool

    What to say to the patient

    Does it feel as though you haven't finished after a bowel movement? Do you ever need to use your fingers to help pass stool?

  4. 04

    Change in stool caliber

    What to say to the patient

    Have your stools become narrower than before?

  5. 05

    Hematochezia and melena

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed blood on your stool, or had black stools?

  6. 06

    Abdominal pain or bloating and relief after defecation

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have abdominal pain or bloating? Does a bowel movement make it better?

  7. 07

    Alternating diarrhea and constipation

    What to say to the patient

    Does diarrhea alternate with constipation?

  8. 08

    Inability to pass gas and vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Are you unable to pass gas, or have you been vomiting?

  9. 09

    Unintentional weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently lost weight without trying?

  10. 10

    Avoidance of defecation because of anal pain

    What to say to the patient

    Do you put off having a bowel movement because your anus hurts when you go?

  11. 11

    Cold intolerance, weight gain, fatigue, and dry skin

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been unusually sensitive to the cold, gaining weight, or feeling tired lately?

  12. 12

    Thirst and polyuria, considering hypercalcemia or diabetes

    What to say to the patient

    Are you often thirsty, or do you urinate a lot?

  13. 13

    Medications that cause constipation, including opioid analgesics, iron, or calcium; laxative use

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take pain relievers or iron supplements? How often do you use laxatives?

  14. 14

    Fiber and fluid intake, and activity level

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get enough vegetables and fluids? Are you physically active?

  15. 15

    Habit of suppressing the urge to defecate, changes in routine, and stress

    What to say to the patient

    Do you tend to hold off going even when you feel the urge? Have there been any recent changes in your routine or stress?

  16. 16

    Stool-withholding behavior and timing of toilet training

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child cross their legs or hide in a corner to avoid having a bowel movement?

  17. 17

    Timing of meconium passage after birth

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    When did your baby pass their first stool after birth?

  18. 18

    Previous colonoscopy findings and family history of colorectal cancer

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had a colonoscopy? Has anyone in your family had colorectal cancer?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Assess abdominal distention, bowel sounds, and palpable stool or other masses

    • Abdominal inspection ↗
    • Abdominal auscultation: bowel sounds ↗
    • Abdominal percussion: tympany and dullness ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
  2. 02

    Inspect the anus for fissures or hemorrhoids and perform a digital rectal examination for fecal impaction, masses, and sphincter tone

    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Anal sphincter tone: resting resistance and voluntary squeeze ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: rectal wall and prostate ↗
    • Detailed external anal inspection for fissures and hemorrhoids Technique card pending
    • Assessment for fecal impaction Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Palpate the thyroid and assess skin dryness and ankle reflexes

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
    • Achilles reflex (primarily S1) ↗
    • Observe for dry skin and myxedema Technique card pending

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
