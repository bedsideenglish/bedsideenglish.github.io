Prioritize the patient's condition. Unexplained weight loss with dysphagia, bleeding, persistent fever or night sweats, or a lump requires further assessment. With shortness of breath, altered consciousness, or circulatory instability, prioritize urgent assessment.

Unintentional weight loss: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Amount and duration of weight loss, including weight records and looser clothing

    What to say to the patient

    How much weight have you lost, and over what period? Have your clothes become looser?

  2. 02

    Intentional weight loss through dietary change or exercise

    What to say to the patient

    Have you intentionally cut back on food or exercised to lose weight?

  3. 03

    Changes in appetite and food intake, including increased appetite

    What to say to the patient

    How has your appetite been? Are you eating less, or more than usual?

  4. 04

    Dysphagia and odynophagia

    What to say to the patient

    Is it difficult to swallow food, or does it feel as though food gets stuck?

  5. 05

    Heartburn, early satiety, abdominal pain, and vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had heartburn or felt full after eating just a little? Any abdominal pain or vomiting?

  6. 06

    Changes in bowel habits, hematochezia, or melena

    What to say to the patient

    Have your bowel habits changed, or have you noticed blood or black stools?

  7. 07

    Diarrhea and greasy stools

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had diarrhea or noticed oil or grease in your stool?

  8. 08

    Palpitations, heat intolerance, and hand tremor

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a racing heartbeat, felt unusually hot, or noticed shaky hands?

  9. 09

    Polydipsia, polyuria, and polyphagia

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been thirsty and urinating often?

  10. 10

    Fever and night sweats

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or drenching night sweats?

  11. 11

    Cough, sputum, and hemoptysis

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a persistent cough or phlegm, or noticed blood when coughing?

  12. 12

    Palpable lumps

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt any lumps in your neck or armpits?

  13. 13

    Low mood, reduced interest, and sleep changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been feeling down or having trouble sleeping lately?

  14. 14

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about fear of weight gain and vomiting after binge eating

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone about this. Are you very worried about gaining weight, or have you ever made yourself vomit after eating?

  15. 15

    Access to meals and meal preparation, living alone, financial circumstances, and dental problems

    What to say to the patient

    Who prepares your meals? Do dental problems make it hard for you to eat?

  16. 16

    Recent upper endoscopy, colonoscopy, and health screening results

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a health checkup, upper endoscopy, or colonoscopy recently?

  17. 17

    Contact with someone with tuberculosis and history of tuberculosis

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been around anyone with tuberculosis, or had tuberculosis before?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Measure height and weight, compare with previous records, and assess vital signs

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Adult anthropometric assessment, including waist and neck circumference Technique card pending
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess conjunctival pallor, scleral icterus, and the mouth, including teeth and ulcers

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
  3. 03

    Palpate the thyroid and assess for hand tremor and proptosis

    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
    • Thyroid-associated eye disease and eyelid signs Technique card pending
    • Observe for tremor Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Palpate cervical, supraclavicular, and axillary lymph nodes

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Axillary lymph nodes: arm support and palpation by group ↗
  5. 05

    Auscultate the lungs and palpate the abdomen for masses or hepatomegaly

    • Posterior lung auscultation sequence ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
  6. 06

    Perform a digital rectal examination if indicated

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: rectal wall and prostate ↗
    • Assessment of potential bleeding causes as indicated by the circumstances Technique card pending

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
