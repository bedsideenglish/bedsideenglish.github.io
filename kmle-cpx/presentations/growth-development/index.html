Prioritize the patient's condition. Loss of acquired skills or new neurologic abnormalities requires prompt assessment. Do not force walking or coordination tasks that are inappropriate for the child's age; interpret growth curves together with developmental assessment.

Growth and developmental delay: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    What first raised concern (height, speech, walking, or behavior), when it began, and who first noticed it (daycare or a well-child visit)

    What to say to the patient

    What concerns you about your child, and when did you first notice it? Has daycare or a clinician raised any concerns at a checkup?

  2. 02

    Previous height and weight records, including well-child checkups or health records, and annual growth

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have earlier height and weight records? About how many centimeters does your child grow each year?

  3. 03

    Gestational age at birth, birth weight and length, problems during pregnancy (alcohol or smoking, infection, or medications), and neonatal hospitalization

    What to say to the patient

    At how many weeks of pregnancy was your child born, and what was their birth weight in kilograms? Were there any problems during pregnancy or after birth?

  4. 04

    Food intake, eating habits, selective eating, and feeding difficulties

    What to say to the patient

    Is your child eating well? How much do they eat, and are there foods they avoid?

  5. 05

    Clues to chronic disease: recurrent diarrhea or abdominal pain, bloody stools, frequent infections, easy breathlessness or cyanosis, urinary abnormalities, and fatigue

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child often have abdominal pain or diarrhea? Do they get frequent colds or become short of breath quickly when running?

  6. 06

    Clues to an intracranial lesion, including headache, vomiting or visual changes; excessive drinking and urination; cold intolerance, constipation or lethargy

    What to say to the patient

    Has your child complained of headaches, vomited, or seemed to have trouble seeing? Have they been unusually thirsty or urinating often?

  7. 07

    Whether signs of puberty (breast buds, pubic hair, or testicular enlargement) are earlier or later than in peers

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have puberty changes started, such as breast buds or pubic hair?

  8. 08

    Timing of gross and fine motor milestones: head control, sitting independently, walking, using a spoon, and drawing

    What to say to the patient

    When did your child develop head control, and how many months old were they when they walked independently? How are they doing with a spoon or drawing?

  9. 09

    Language development: babbling, first words, combining two words, and understanding speech

    What to say to the patient

    When did your child say their first word? Do they put two words together now? Do they understand simple things you say?

  10. 10

    Social and communication development: eye contact, response to name, pointing, imitation, and repetitive behaviors

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child make eye contact and turn when you call their name? Do they point to things they want? Do they repeat the same behaviors over and over?

  11. 11

    Response to sound, recurrent otitis media, newborn hearing screening results, and vision

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child respond well to sounds? Have they had frequent ear infections? Did they pass their newborn hearing screening?

  12. 12

    Loss of previously acquired language or skills (developmental regression)

    What to say to the patient

    Are there words or activities your child used to manage but can no longer do?

  13. 13

    Parents' heights and timing of puberty, and family history of short stature, developmental delay, or genetic disease

    What to say to the patient

    How tall is each parent? Did either parent go through puberty late and grow later than other children? Has anyone in the family had developmental delays?

  14. 14

    Medications that affect growth, such as long-term steroids or ADHD medications

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child take any long-term medications, such as steroids or medication for attention problems?

  15. 15

    Caregiving environment: primary caregiver, sleep, screen time, and family difficulties

    What to say to the patient

    Who usually looks after your child? Do they sleep well? How much screen time do they have each day? Are there any difficulties at home?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Measure height (recumbent length under age 2), weight, and head circumference

    • Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Plot measurements on pediatric growth charts to assess percentiles, growth velocity compared with previous records, and the expected height range calculated from parental heights

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Body proportions, including limbs relative to trunk, and dysmorphic features such as a webbed neck, broad chest, or distinctive facial features

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
  4. 04

    Assess nutritional status, examine the thyroid and abdomen, and assess for a heart murmur

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
  5. 05

    Assess sexual maturity using Tanner staging

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Observe development (gait, hand use, speech, eye contact, and response to name) and assess muscle tone and reflexes

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Gait and tandem gait ↗
    • Patellar reflex (L2–L4, primarily L4) ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
