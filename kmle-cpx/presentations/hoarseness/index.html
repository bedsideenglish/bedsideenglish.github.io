Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent evaluation of a possible upper airway problem if inspiratory noise or breathing difficulty is present, and do not force an oral examination. Persistent or worsening voice changes need further evaluation even if examination findings are subtle.

Voice changes: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset and duration, including whether symptoms have lasted four weeks or longer

    What to say to the patient

    When did your voice become hoarse? Has it lasted more than a month?

  2. 02

    Sudden or gradual onset; progressive or fluctuating course

    What to say to the patient

    Did your voice change suddenly or gradually? Is it getting worse, or does it come and go?

  3. 03

    Voice quality: rough, breathy, weak, or tremulous

    What to say to the patient

    Does your voice sound rough or cracked, or is it weak and breathy?

  4. 04

    Occupational voice use, such as teaching, singing, or call center work; shouting

    What to say to the patient

    Does your work involve a lot of talking or singing? Do you often need to shout?

  5. 05

    Recent cold, sore throat, or cough

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had a cold, sore throat, or cough?

  6. 06

    Heartburn, acid regurgitation, a lump sensation in the throat, frequent throat clearing, or morning worsening

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have heartburn or acid coming back up? Do you often clear your throat because it feels like something is stuck there?

  7. 07

    Difficulty breathing or harsh, noisy breathing

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had trouble breathing or a harsh, whistling sound when you breathe?

  8. 08

    Difficulty swallowing, painful swallowing, or pain radiating to the ear

    What to say to the patient

    Is it hard or painful to swallow food? Does the pain reach your ear?

  9. 09

    Neck lump or weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt a lump in your neck or lost weight recently?

  10. 10

    Blood-streaked sputum or persistent cough

    What to say to the patient

    Have you coughed up any blood or had a cough that won't go away?

  11. 11

    Recent thyroid, neck, or chest surgery; endotracheal intubation

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had thyroid, neck, or chest surgery, or general anesthesia?

  12. 12

    Chest or back pain; heart or major blood vessel disease

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had an aching pain in your chest or back? Have you been told you have a problem with your heart or a major blood vessel?

  13. 13

    Choking when drinking, slurred speech, hand tremor, fatigable weakness, or drooping eyelids

    What to say to the patient

    Do you choke when drinking water, have slurred speech or shaky hands, or find that your muscles tire easily?

  14. 14

    Inhaled corticosteroid use

    What to say to the patient

    Do you use an inhaler for asthma or another lung condition?

  15. 15

    Smoking amount and duration in pack-years; alcohol intake

    What to say to the patient

    How many cigarettes do you smoke a day, and for how many years? How much alcohol do you drink?

  16. 16

    Symptoms of hypothyroidism, such as cold intolerance, weight gain, or fatigue

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been unusually sensitive to the cold, gaining weight, or feeling tired lately?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

  1. 01

    Check respiratory rate and oxygen saturation and assess for inspiratory stridor

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Work of breathing, accessory muscle use, and tripod positioning ↗
    • Respiratory rate, rhythm, and effort ↗
    • Pulse oximetry: placement, signal quality, and limitations ↗
  2. 02

    Listen to the voice and assess maximum phonation time

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Inspect the mouth and pharynx

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
  4. 04

    Palpate cervical lymph nodes and the thyroid

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
