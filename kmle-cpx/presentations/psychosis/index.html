Prioritize the patient's condition. Rapidly altered consciousness, fever, suspected intoxication, or a specific risk of harm to self or others requires prompt safety and physical assessment. Do not infer violence risk from hallucinations or delusions alone.

Hallucinations and behavior changes: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset, sudden or gradual development, and symptom duration

    What to say to the patient

    When did these experiences start? Did they begin suddenly or develop gradually?

  2. 02

    Content and frequency of auditory hallucinations, including conversational or critical voices, and presence of visual hallucinations

    What to say to the patient

    Do you hear voices or sounds that other people don't hear? What do they say? Do you ever see things that others don't see?

  3. 03

    Command hallucinations and intent to act on them

    What to say to the patient

    Do the voices ever tell you to do something? Do you feel you have to follow what they say?

  4. 04

    Persecutory, referential, or grandiose delusions, and a sense that thoughts are controlled or broadcast

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel that someone is watching you or trying to harm you, or that other people can know your thoughts?

  5. 05

    Disorganized speech or behavior, reduced hygiene or daily functioning, and social withdrawal

    What to say to the patient

    How have you been managing washing, eating, school, or work? Have you been avoiding other people?

  6. 06

    Reduced need for sleep, elevated or irritable mood, rapid speech, and racing thoughts

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel rested despite little sleep? Has your mood been unusually high or irritable, or have your thoughts been racing?

  7. 07

    Excessive spending, reckless investments or driving, and impulsive sexual behavior

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been spending more than usual or taking risks recently?

  8. 08

    Previous depressive episodes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you previously had several weeks of feeling very depressed?

  9. 09

    Whether hallucinations or delusions persist outside mood episodes

    What to say to the patient

    Do you hear the voices even when your mood is neither unusually high nor low?

  10. 10

    Suicidal thoughts, self-harm plans, or attempts

    What to say to the patient

    It sounds like you've been having a very hard time. Have you had thoughts of wanting to die or hurting yourself?

  11. 11

    Thoughts of harming others, violent behavior, and access to weapons

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt angry enough to want to harm someone? Have you been in any physical fights?

  12. 12

    Insight into the need for treatment and willingness to engage in care

    What to say to the patient

    Do you think these experiences may need treatment?

  13. 13

    Use of drugs such as methamphetamine or cannabis, medications such as corticosteroids, alcohol use, and recent cessation

    What to say to the patient

    Have you used drugs such as methamphetamine or cannabis? How much alcohol do you drink, and have you stopped recently?

  14. 14

    Discontinuation of prescribed mood stabilizers or antipsychotics without medical advice

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    If you were prescribed mental health medication, have you recently stopped taking it?

  15. 15

    Headache, seizures, fluctuating consciousness, fever, palpitations, and weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had headaches or seizures? Any fever, racing heartbeat, or weight loss?

  16. 16

    Sleep pattern and major stress before symptom onset

    What to say to the patient

    How many hours do you sleep each day? Was there a major stress before this started?

  17. 17

    Recent childbirth

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    Have you given birth recently?

  18. 18

    Family history of schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, or suicide

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family received treatment for schizophrenia or bipolar disorder?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
