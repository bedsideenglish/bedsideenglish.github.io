Prioritize the patient's condition. Warning signs such as exertional chest pain, unilateral weakness, or acute abdominal pain may indicate acute coronary syndrome, stroke, or acute pancreatitis and require immediate emergency assessment.

Dyslipidemia: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Specific lipid values: total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, and triglycerides

    What to say to the patient

    Which results were abnormal, and what were the numbers? Did you bring the report?

  2. 02

    Fasting status at testing, and heavy alcohol intake or a fatty meal the day before

    What to say to the patient

    Were you fasting before the blood test? Had you had alcohol or a fatty meal the day before?

  3. 03

    Previous lipid results, when the abnormality was first found, and previous treatment, including medication use and reasons for stopping

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been told your cholesterol was high before? Have you ever stopped taking medication for it?

  4. 04

    Chest pain or pressure and shortness of breath with exertion

    What to say to the patient

    When you climb stairs or walk quickly, do you get chest tightness or pain, or feel short of breath?

  5. 05

    Transient unilateral weakness or sensory change, slurred speech, or sudden monocular vision loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever briefly had weakness on one side, slurred speech, or sudden loss of vision in one eye?

  6. 06

    Calf pain with walking that improves with rest

    What to say to the patient

    After walking for a while, do your calves hurt enough that you need to rest? Does the pain go away with rest?

  7. 07

    Assess risk factors needed for risk estimation, including hypertension, diabetes, smoking status, and smoking amount

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been told you have high blood pressure or diabetes? Do you smoke?

  8. 08

    Family history of premature heart disease (men younger than 55, women younger than 65) or markedly elevated cholesterol

    What to say to the patient

    Have your parents or siblings had heart disease at a young age or very high cholesterol?

  9. 09

    Yellow lesions on the eyelids or over tendons of the hands or heels

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed yellow patches on your eyelids or lumps along the tendons at the back of your heels?

  10. 10

    Cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, and low energy

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been unusually sensitive to the cold, gaining weight, or constipated lately?

  11. 11

    Edema, foamy urine, jaundice, and pruritus

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had swelling or foamy urine? Have your eyes looked yellow, or have you been itchy?

  12. 12

    Medications that raise lipids, including corticosteroids, diuretics, acne medications, estrogen or oral contraceptives, psychiatric medications, and immunosuppressants

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take any medications such as steroids, a diuretic, acne medication, or hormones?

  13. 13

    Diet, including fatty meat, fried foods, processed meat, butter, snacks, sugary drinks, and a diet mainly of white rice or noodles; alcohol intake

    What to say to the patient

    Do you often eat meat, fried foods, snacks, or sugary drinks? How much alcohol do you drink in a week?

  14. 14

    Physical activity, weight changes, and increased abdominal adiposity

    What to say to the patient

    How much exercise do you get in a week? Have you gained weight or noticed your waist getting bigger recently?

  15. 15

    Severe upper abdominal pain or vomiting with markedly elevated triglycerides, suggesting pancreatitis

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had severe upper abdominal pain spreading to your back, with vomiting?

  16. 16

    Previous adverse effects from cholesterol medication, such as myalgia, and concerns about treatment

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had muscle pain while taking cholesterol medication? Do you have any concerns about the medication?

  17. 17

    Current pregnancy or pregnancy plans, with individualized review of statin therapy during pregnancy

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    Are you planning a pregnancy? I'm asking because we may need to stop or change your cholesterol medication during pregnancy.

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Blood pressure and pulse

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  2. 02

    Height, weight, BMI, and waist circumference; the Korean source checklist defines abdominal obesity as a waist circumference of at least 90 cm in men or 85 cm in women

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Eyelid xanthelasma, corneal arcus at a young age, and xanthomas of the Achilles or hand extensor tendons

    • Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Carotid bruits, heart sounds, and dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Lower limb peripheral pulses: femoral, popliteal, and pedal ↗
    • Resting ankle-brachial index with Doppler ↗

    Palpate the thyroid and check for edema

    Select as clinically indicated

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].

About this material and its references

Clinical context

Pregnancy and statins: the FDA advises discontinuing statins in most pregnant patients while allowing individualized consideration for those at very high cardiovascular risk. Pregnancy plans therefore require a medication review, rather than a blanket statement about every cholesterol medication. FDA statin pregnancy safety communication.
