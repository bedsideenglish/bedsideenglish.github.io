Prioritize the patient's condition. A persistent lump, new skin dimpling, or bloody nipple discharge requires follow-up even if palpation is reassuring. Explain why examination is needed and respect consent, privacy, choice about a chaperone, and requests to stop.

Breast pain and breast lumps: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    When and how the finding was first noticed

    What to say to the patient

    When did you first notice the lump, and how did you find it?

  2. 02

    Location, size, number, and whether both breasts are involved

    What to say to the patient

    Which breast and where do you feel the lump? About how big is it? Is there one lump or more than one?

  3. 03

    Change in size and changes with the menstrual cycle

    What to say to the patient

    Has it grown since you first noticed it? Does it get bigger or smaller around your period?

  4. 04

    Presence and cyclicity of pain

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt? Does it happen with each period, or is the pain there all the time?

  5. 05

    Skin changes (dimpling, peau d'orange, or redness) and nipple retraction or eczema

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed dimpling or an orange-peel texture on your breast? Has the nipple turned inward or developed a sore?

  6. 06

    Nipple discharge: unilateral or bilateral, spontaneous or expressed, color, and blood

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed anything coming from the nipple? Does it come out on its own without squeezing? Is there any blood in it?

  7. 07

    Axillary lump

    What to say to the patient

    Can you feel any lumps in your armpit, too?

  8. 08

    Fever and breast redness, swelling, or warmth

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever, or has your breast become red, swollen, or hot?

  9. 09

    Breastfeeding, breast engorgement, and nipple injury

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: female patients

    What to say to the patient

    Are you breastfeeding now? Have your breasts felt engorged, or have you had any nipple injuries?

  10. 10

    Weight loss, bone pain, cough, or shortness of breath

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently lost weight without trying, had bone pain, or developed a cough or shortness of breath?

  11. 11

    Breast trauma or procedures

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you injured your breast or had any procedures on it?

  12. 12

    Previous breast disease, biopsy, or chest radiation

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a breast lump before, a breast biopsy, or radiation treatment to the chest?

  13. 13

    Family history of breast or ovarian cancer, particularly close relatives or young age at diagnosis

    What to say to the patient

    Has your mother or any of your sisters had breast or ovarian cancer? How old were they?

  14. 14

    Age at menarche and menopause, childbirth and breastfeeding history, and postmenopausal hormone therapy

    Applies to: female patients

    What to say to the patient

    How old were you when you started your periods? Have you given birth or breastfed? Have you taken hormones after menopause?

  15. 15

    Oral contraceptives, hormonal medications, and medications that raise prolactin

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take birth control pills, hormones, stomach medicines, or psychiatric medications?

  16. 16

    Recent mammography or breast ultrasound screening results

    Applies to: female patients

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had recent breast screening? What were the results?

  17. 17

    In men: medications such as steroids or antiandrogens, alcohol use or liver disease, testicular abnormalities, and decreased libido

    Applies to: male patients

    What to say to the patient

    Have you taken muscle-building drugs or hormones? How much alcohol do you drink? Have you noticed any testicular changes or a lower sex drive?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    With the patient seated, inspect and compare both breasts with the arms at the sides, raised, and with hands on the hips

    • Clinical breast examination — inspection and systematic palpation ↗
  2. 02

    With the patient supine, palpate the entirety of both breasts, assessing mass size, consistency, borders, mobility, and tenderness

    • Clinical breast examination — inspection and systematic palpation ↗
  3. 03

    Inspect the nipples and assess discharge

    • Clinical breast examination — inspection and systematic palpation ↗
  4. 04

    Palpate the axillary and supraclavicular lymph nodes

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Axillary lymph nodes: arm support and palpation by group ↗
  5. 05

    Measure temperature if mastitis or an abscess is suspected

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    For male breast enlargement, palpate the testes and assess for signs of liver disease

    Select as clinically indicated

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
