Prioritize the patient's condition. Changes in a pigmented lesion's shape, border, or color, growth, ulceration, or bleeding require further assessment. Do not determine benignity or malignancy from appearance or a single criterion alone, and do not squeeze or repeatedly irritate a mass.

Skin lumps and changing moles: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    When the lesion was first noticed and its duration

    What to say to the patient

    When did you first notice it?

  2. 02

    Location: sun-exposed areas such as the face or scalp, wrist or near a joint, or back

    What to say to the patient

    Where is it? Is it somewhere that gets a lot of sun, such as your face or hands?

  3. 03

    Change in size and rate of growth

    What to say to the patient

    Has it grown since you first noticed it? How quickly?

  4. 04

    Size, number, and lesions elsewhere

    What to say to the patient

    About how large is it? Have you noticed similar lumps elsewhere?

  5. 05

    Soft or firm consistency, and mobility with gentle pressure

    What to say to the patient

    Does it feel soft or hard? Does it move when you push it gently?

  6. 06

    Pain and tenderness to pressure

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt on its own or when you press on it?

  7. 07

    A new mole or changes in a mole's shape, border, or color

    What to say to the patient

    Has the mole changed shape or color? Have the edges become uneven, or does it contain several colors?

  8. 08

    Bleeding or recurrent crusting without healing

    What to say to the patient

    Does it bleed or repeatedly scab over without healing?

  9. 09

    Redness, warmth, pus, or foul-smelling discharge

    What to say to the patient

    Has it become red, swollen, or warm? Is there pus or foul-smelling discharge?

  10. 10

    Central punctum or thick, whitish material expressed on squeezing

    What to say to the patient

    Is there a small opening in the middle, or does thick white material come out if it's squeezed?

  11. 11

    Fluctuation in size and enlargement with use

    What to say to the patient

    Does the lump get bigger and smaller? Does it get bigger when you use your hands a lot?

  12. 12

    Previous injury, surgery, piercing, or acne at the site

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had an injury, surgery, or piercing at that spot before?

  13. 13

    Fever, weight loss, and lumps elsewhere

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or lost weight recently? Have you felt lumps elsewhere?

  14. 14

    Sun exposure and history of severe sunburn

    What to say to the patient

    Do you spend a lot of time in the sun? Have you had severe sunburns?

  15. 15

    Personal or family history of skin cancer or melanoma, and numerous moles

    What to say to the patient

    Have you or anyone in your family had skin cancer? Do you have many moles?

  16. 16

    Immunosuppressive medications, organ transplantation, and diabetes

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take medication that suppresses your immune system, or do you have diabetes?

  17. 17

    Previous squeezing or treatment, and recurrence after removal

    What to say to the patient

    Have you squeezed it yourself or had treatment? Has it come back after removal?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
