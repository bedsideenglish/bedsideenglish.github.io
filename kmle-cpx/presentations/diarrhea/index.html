Prioritize the patient's condition. With severe dehydration, inability to stand, signs of sepsis, or suspected peritonitis, stop the checklist examination and proceed to immediate emergency assessment of vital signs and clinical care.

Diarrhea: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset and duration: acute or chronic

    What to say to the patient

    When did the diarrhea start?

  2. 02

    Daily stool frequency, amount, and characteristics: watery, greasy, or foul-smelling

    What to say to the patient

    How many bowel movements do you have a day? Is the stool watery, greasy, or unusually foul-smelling?

  3. 03

    Blood or mucus in the stool

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed blood or mucus in your stool?

  4. 04

    Diarrhea that wakes the patient from sleep

    What to say to the patient

    Does diarrhea wake you up at night?

  5. 05

    Location of abdominal pain, relief after defecation, and tenesmus

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have abdominal pain? Does having a bowel movement help? Do you still feel the urge to go afterward?

  6. 06

    Fever and vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or vomiting?

  7. 07

    Dizziness, reduced urine output, dry mouth, and ability to drink fluids

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been dizzy or urinating less? Are you able to drink fluids?

  8. 08

    Suspected food exposure, including raw foods, buffets, or seafood; symptoms in others who ate the same food

    What to say to the patient

    What did you eat before the diarrhea started? Has anyone who ate with you had the same symptoms?

  9. 09

    Recent travel, camping, and potentially contaminated water

    What to say to the patient

    Have you traveled recently, or drunk untreated stream or spring water?

  10. 10

    Recent antibiotic use, hospitalization, and newly started medications

    What to say to the patient

    Have you taken antibiotics or been hospitalized recently? Have you started any new medications?

  11. 11

    Unintentional weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost weight recently?

  12. 12

    Relationship to particular foods, such as milk or wheat products

    What to say to the patient

    Does it get worse after milk or bread?

  13. 13

    Relationship to stress and alternation with constipation

    What to say to the patient

    Does stress make it worse? Does it alternate with constipation?

  14. 14

    Joint pain, skin rash, mouth ulcers, and red eyes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had joint pain or frequent mouth ulcers?

  15. 15

    Palpitations, heat intolerance, and flushing

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a racing heartbeat or felt unusually hot? Does your face often flush?

  16. 16

    In children: urine frequency, reduced activity, and tears when crying

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    Is your child having as many wet diapers as usual? Have they been unusually sleepy or less active, or had no tears when crying?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
