Prioritize the patient's condition. If signs of acute respiratory failure are present, such as severe breathlessness, rapid breathing, or cyanosis, stop the checklist examination and proceed to immediate emergency care and assessment.

Cough: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Cough duration: acute (less than 3 weeks), subacute (3–8 weeks), or chronic (8 weeks or longer)

    What to say to the patient

    How long have you had the cough?

  2. 02

    Trigger for onset, including a preceding cold, and previous similar symptoms

    What to say to the patient

    Did it start after a cold? Have you ever had a cough that lasted this long before?

  3. 03

    Presence, color, amount, and odor of sputum

    What to say to the patient

    Are you coughing anything up? What color is it, and how much is there?

  4. 04

    Time of day when the cough is worse (night, early morning, or morning) and worsening when lying down

    What to say to the patient

    Is there a time of day when the cough is worse? Is it worse at night or in the early morning?

  5. 05

    Paroxysmal cough, an inspiratory whoop after coughing, and posttussive vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have coughing fits that are hard to stop, or make a “whoop” sound when you breathe in afterward?

  6. 06

    Triggers: cold air, exercise, dust, animals, and seasonal changes

    What to say to the patient

    Does it get worse with cold air or exercise, or around dust or animals?

  7. 07

    Wheezing and shortness of breath

    What to say to the patient

    Do you wheeze when you breathe, or feel short of breath?

  8. 08

    Fever and chills

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or felt chilled?

  9. 09

    Hemoptysis: blood in the sputum

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed any blood when you cough?

  10. 10

    Weight loss and night sweats

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been losing weight or waking up sweaty at night?

  11. 11

    Chest pain with breathing or coughing

    What to say to the patient

    Does your chest hurt when you cough or breathe?

  12. 12

    Rhinorrhea, nasal congestion, and a sensation of drainage down the throat (postnasal drip)

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel mucus dripping down the back of your throat, or does your nose often feel stuffy?

  13. 13

    Heartburn, acid regurgitation, a lump-in-the-throat sensation, and frequent throat clearing

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get heartburn or a sour taste coming up into your mouth? Does it feel like something is stuck in your throat?

  14. 14

    Shortness of breath when lying down and leg swelling

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get short of breath when you lie down, or have swelling in your legs?

  15. 15

    Use of blood pressure medications such as ACE inhibitors, and the timing of cough onset

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take blood pressure medication? Did the cough start after you began a new medication or changed one?

  16. 16

    Smoking quantity and duration in pack-years, and recent changes in the cough

    What to say to the patient

    If you've smoked, about how many packs a day did you smoke, and for how many years?

  17. 17

    Contact with someone with tuberculosis and previous tuberculosis treatment

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been around anyone with tuberculosis, or ever been treated for tuberculosis yourself?

  18. 18

    Contacts with a cough, including family, coworkers, and daycare contacts

    What to say to the patient

    Is anyone at home or at work coughing like this?

  19. 19

    Occupational and environmental exposure to dust, chemicals, birds, or mold

    What to say to the patient

    Are you exposed to dust, chemicals, birds, or mold at work or at home?

  20. 20

    History of allergic conditions, including allergic rhinitis and atopic dermatitis

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have allergies, such as hay fever, or eczema?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
