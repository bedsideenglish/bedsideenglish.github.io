Prioritize the patient's condition. With repeated projectile vomiting, severe headache, neck stiffness, or decreased consciousness, consider neurologic emergencies such as raised intracranial pressure, subarachnoid hemorrhage, or meningitis. Ensure airway patency in patients at risk of aspiration and arrange prompt emergency assessment.

Vomiting: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset and whether symptoms are acute or recurrent

    What to say to the patient

    When did you start vomiting? Did it begin suddenly, or has this happened often before?

  2. 02

    Frequency of vomiting and interval between meals and vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    How many times a day do you vomit? How soon after eating does it happen?

  3. 03

    Vomit characteristics: undigested food, bile, feculent odor, or blood

    What to say to the patient

    What did the vomit look like? Was it green, or was there any blood in it?

  4. 04

    Vomiting before eating in the morning, and projectile vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Do you vomit as soon as you wake up? Does it come out forcefully, like a spray?

  5. 05

    Location of abdominal pain and whether vomiting relieves it

    What to say to the patient

    Do you also have abdominal pain? Does vomiting make it feel better?

  6. 06

    Passage of stool and gas, abdominal distention, and diarrhea

    What to say to the patient

    Are you passing stool and gas? Has your abdomen been bloated, or have you had diarrhea?

  7. 07

    Fever and symptoms in others who ate the same food

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever? Has anyone who ate the same food been sick?

  8. 08

    Headache, neck stiffness, dizziness, visual changes, and altered consciousness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a headache or dizziness? Has your vision been blurry?

  9. 09

    Recent head trauma

    What to say to the patient

    Have you hit your head recently?

  10. 10

    Ability to drink fluids, reduced urine output, dry mouth, and dizziness on standing

    What to say to the patient

    Are you able to drink fluids? Are you urinating as much as usual?

  11. 11

    Polydipsia, polyuria, diabetes, and insulin use

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been unusually thirsty or urinating a lot lately? Do you have diabetes?

  12. 12

    Chest pain and shortness of breath

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had any chest pain or shortness of breath?

  13. 13

    Recently started medications, alcohol, and use of drugs such as cannabis

    What to say to the patient

    Have you started any new medications recently? What about alcohol or other drugs?

  14. 14

    Unintentional weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost weight recently?

  15. 15

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the question before asking about self-induced vomiting

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone about this. Have you ever made yourself vomit?

  16. 16

    In infants and young children: feeding, weight gain, and reduced activity

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    How much has your child been feeding? Have they been unusually sleepy, less active, or irritable?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
