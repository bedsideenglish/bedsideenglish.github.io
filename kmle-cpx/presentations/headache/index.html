Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent assessment for sudden severe headache, fever with neck stiffness, new neurologic findings, or altered consciousness. A normal examination is not reassuring on its own when the headache is new or has changed, or when risk factors such as cancer or immunosuppression are present.

Headache: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset: sudden, reaching maximum intensity within seconds to 1 minute, or gradual

    What to say to the patient

    Did the headache start suddenly? Was it at its worst right away?

  2. 02

    Headache duration and frequency; first occurrence or a change from the usual headache pattern

    What to say to the patient

    When did the pain start? Have you had headaches like this before? Is this different from your usual headaches?

  3. 03

    Pain location: one side, both sides, around the eye, back of the head, or temple

    What to say to the patient

    Where in your head does it hurt? Is it on one side or both?

  4. 04

    Pain quality (throbbing, tight, or stabbing) and severity

    What to say to the patient

    What does the pain feel like? Does it throb, or feel tight? How severe is it?

  5. 05

    Duration of each episode and time of day when it is worse, including on waking or waking during the night

    What to say to the patient

    How long does each headache last? Is it bad when you wake up, or does it wake you during the night?

  6. 06

    Aggravating and relieving factors: movement, coughing or straining, improvement when supine, and worsening when upright

    What to say to the patient

    Does coughing or straining make it worse? Does it get better when you lie down or worse when you stand up?

  7. 07

    Aura before headache, including flashing lights, partial visual loss, or tingling

    What to say to the patient

    Before the headache starts, do you see flashing lights or notice changes in your vision?

  8. 08

    Nausea or vomiting, and sensitivity to light or sound

    What to say to the patient

    When you have the headache, do you feel nauseated, or does light or sound bother you?

  9. 09

    Tearing, red eye, rhinorrhea, or eyelid drooping on the side of the headache

    What to say to the patient

    When you have the headache, does the eye on that side water, or does your nose run on that side?

  10. 10

    Fever, neck stiffness, and rash

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or a stiff neck?

  11. 11

    Weakness or sensory changes, slurred speech, altered consciousness, and seizures

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had any weakness in your arms or legs, or any slurred speech?

  12. 12

    Reduced vision, double vision, and eye pain or redness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had blurred or double vision?

  13. 13

    In patients age 50 or older: temporal tenderness, jaw pain with chewing, and shoulder or hip girdle pain

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Does your temple hurt when you brush your hair, or does your jaw hurt when you chew?

  14. 14

    Recent head trauma

    What to say to the patient

    Have you hit your head recently?

  15. 15

    Frequency of analgesic use (days per month) and response

    What to say to the patient

    About how many days a month do you take pain relievers? Do they help?

  16. 16

    Triggers: lack of sleep, stress, menstruation, particular foods, and alcohol

    What to say to the patient

    Is the headache worse when you haven't slept, when you're stressed, or around your period?

  17. 17

    Hypertension, use of antihypertensive medications, and anticoagulant use

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have high blood pressure? Do you take your medication regularly? Do you take a blood thinner?

  18. 18

    Headache in family members sharing the same space, and use of heating appliances

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone you live with have a headache, too? Have you been using a heater?

  19. 19

    History of cancer or immunosuppression, including HIV or immunosuppressive medications

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been treated for cancer, or do you have a condition or take medication that weakens your immune system?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
