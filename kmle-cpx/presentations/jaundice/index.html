Prioritize the patient's condition. Acute cholangitis with fever or chills, jaundice, and right upper quadrant pain (Charcot triad), or acute liver failure with hepatic encephalopathy, can deteriorate rapidly with septic shock or cerebral edema. Prioritize immediate emergency care and specialist assessment over completing the examination checklist.

Jaundice: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset and rate of progression

    What to say to the patient

    When did your eyes start looking yellow? Is it getting worse?

  2. 02

    Urine color: cola-colored or dark brown

    What to say to the patient

    Has your urine become darker?

  3. 03

    Pale or gray stool color

    What to say to the patient

    Have your stools turned pale or gray?

  4. 04

    Pruritus

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been feeling itchy?

  5. 05

    Right upper quadrant pain and episodes of pain after meals

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have abdominal pain, especially in the upper right side?

  6. 06

    Fever and chills

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or shaking chills?

  7. 07

    Altered consciousness, increased sleepiness, and easy bruising or bleeding

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt confused, bruised easily, or had bleeding that was hard to stop?

  8. 08

    Loss of appetite, nausea, and fatigue

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a poor appetite, nausea, or fatigue?

  9. 09

    Unintentional weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost weight recently?

  10. 10

    Dizziness, shortness of breath, and pallor

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been dizzy or short of breath with even a little activity?

  11. 11

    Alcohol intake and duration of use

    What to say to the patient

    How often do you drink alcohol, and for how many years have you been drinking?

  12. 12

    Recent medications, herbal remedies, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently taken any medications, herbal remedies, or supplements?

  13. 13

    Intake of raw food, including shellfish, and contact with someone with jaundice

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently eaten raw shellfish, or been around anyone who developed jaundice?

  14. 14

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about hepatitis B or C, blood transfusions, tattoos, needle sharing, and sexual contact

    What to say to the patient

    These questions can be sensitive, but I ask everyone. Have you ever been told you have hepatitis, or had a blood transfusion or a tattoo?

  15. 15

    History of gallstones or biliary surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had gallstones or gallbladder surgery?

  16. 16

    Abdominal distention and leg edema

    What to say to the patient

    Has your abdomen become swollen, or have your legs been swollen?

  17. 17

    In newborns: age in days, feeding method and amount, and urine and stool frequency

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    How many days old is your baby? Is your baby feeding well, and how many diapers do you change each day?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Check temperature, blood pressure, and pulse to assess for cholangitis or sepsis

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    In good lighting, check for scleral icterus and conjunctival pallor

    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
  3. 03

    Spider angiomas, palmar erythema, scratch marks, and asterixis

    • Asterixis ↗
    • Inspect for skin signs of chronic liver disease, including spider angiomas, palmar erythema, and scratch marks from itching Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Assess right upper quadrant tenderness, Murphy sign, liver and spleen size, and a palpable gallbladder

    • Abdominal inspection ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
    • Murphy sign ↗
    • Splenic palpation in the right lateral position ↗
  5. 05

    Assess for ascites using shifting dullness

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Shifting dullness ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
