Prioritize the patient's condition. Mastoid swelling or tenderness, facial weakness, or a severe headache may indicate an intracranial complication of otitis media and requires immediate specialist ENT assessment.

Ear pain: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset and affected ear

    What to say to the patient

    When did the pain start? Which ear hurts?

  2. 02

    Pain quality and severity; nighttime worsening

    What to say to the patient

    What does the pain feel like: throbbing or stabbing? Is it bad enough to keep you from sleeping?

  3. 03

    Pain with pinna traction or pressure; pain with chewing

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt more if you pull on your ear or press just in front of it? What about when you chew?

  4. 04

    Ear discharge: color, odor, or blood

    What to say to the patient

    Is anything draining from your ear? What color is it, and does it have a smell?

  5. 05

    Hearing loss or ear fullness

    What to say to the patient

    Is it harder to hear on that side, or does that ear feel blocked?

  6. 06

    Tinnitus or vertigo

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had ringing in your ears or dizziness that feels like the room is spinning?

  7. 07

    Fever; recent cold or nasal congestion

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever? Have you recently had a cold or stuffy nose?

  8. 08

    One-sided facial weakness; vesicles around or inside the ear

    What to say to the patient

    Is one side of your face harder to move, or have you noticed blisters around or inside your ear?

  9. 09

    Postauricular swelling or redness; severe headache; neck stiffness

    What to say to the patient

    Has the area behind your ear become swollen or red? Have you had a severe headache or a stiff neck?

  10. 10

    Water entering the ear after swimming or showering; cotton swab or ear pick use

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently been swimming, or do you often clean inside your ears with cotton swabs or ear picks?

  11. 11

    Ear trauma, such as a slap or loud blast; sensation of a foreign body or insect in the ear

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been hit in the ear or injured it? Does it feel like something is inside your ear?

  12. 12

    Pressure changes, such as flying or diving

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently flown or been diving?

  13. 13

    Toothache; jaw joint clicking or pain; teeth clenching

    What to say to the patient

    Do your teeth hurt, or does your jaw click or hurt when you open your mouth?

  14. 14

    Sore throat, painful swallowing, hoarseness, or a neck lump

    What to say to the patient

    Does your throat hurt, or is it painful to swallow? Have you had a hoarse voice or felt a lump in your neck?

  15. 15

    History of recurrent otitis media, tympanic membrane perforation, or ear surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had frequent middle ear infections, a hole in your eardrum, or ear surgery?

  16. 16

    Diabetes or immunocompromise

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have diabetes, or any condition or medication that weakens your immune system?

  17. 17

    For children: ear pulling, irritability, refusal to feed or eat, waking at night crying

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child keep touching or pulling at their ear? Have they been very fussy or not eating well?

  18. 18

    For children: daycare attendance, secondhand smoke, bottle feeding while lying down, immunizations

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    Does your child attend daycare? Does anyone at home smoke? Are their immunizations up to date?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
