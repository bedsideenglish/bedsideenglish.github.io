Low mood and depression: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset and duration of low mood, including whether it has lasted at least 2 weeks and is present most of the day

    What to say to the patient

    When did you start feeling down? Is it there most of the day, nearly every day?

  2. 02

    Precipitating events, including bereavement, a breakup, employment issues, or financial problems

    What to say to the patient

    Were there any difficult events or major changes around that time?

  3. 03

    Reduced interest or pleasure

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost interest in things you used to enjoy, or do they no longer feel enjoyable?

  4. 04

    Sleep changes: difficulty falling asleep, early morning waking, or excessive sleep

    What to say to the patient

    How have you been sleeping? Do you wake up early and have trouble getting back to sleep?

  5. 05

    Changes in appetite and weight

    What to say to the patient

    How has your appetite been? Have you lost or gained weight recently?

  6. 06

    Fatigue, low energy, and reduced ability to concentrate or make decisions

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt low on energy or tired easily? Has it become harder to concentrate or make decisions?

  7. 07

    Guilt and feelings of worthlessness

    What to say to the patient

    Do you often feel worthless or as though everything is your fault?

  8. 08

    Slowed speech or movement, or agitation

    What to say to the patient

    Have people noticed that you're speaking or moving more slowly, or, on the other hand, that you seem restless?

  9. 09

    Ask directly about thoughts of suicide or self-harm

    What to say to the patient

    It sounds like things have been very difficult. Have you had thoughts about wanting to die or wishing you could disappear?

  10. 10

    Specific suicide plan, means, timing, previous attempts, and intent to act

    What to say to the patient

    When you have those thoughts, have you thought about how you would act on them? Have you ever tried before?

  11. 11

    Protective factors, including family, people who can help, and reasons for living; current safety

    What to say to the patient

    Is there someone you can talk to when things are difficult? Are you alone at home right now?

  12. 12

    Functioning in daily life, work or school, and relationships

    What to say to the patient

    Has this affected your work, household tasks, or relationships with other people?

  13. 13

    Periods of unusually elevated mood without fatigue despite little sleep

    What to say to the patient

    Have there also been times lasting several days when your mood was unusually high and you barely slept but didn't feel tired?

  14. 14

    Auditory hallucinations and delusions

    What to say to the patient

    Have you heard sounds that other people don't hear, or felt that someone was trying to harm you?

  15. 15

    Alcohol intake and use of sleeping pills or other drugs

    What to say to the patient

    How much alcohol have you been drinking lately? Do you take anything to help you sleep?

  16. 16

    Symptoms of thyroid disease, including cold intolerance, weight gain, and constipation; recently started medications

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been more sensitive to the cold or had constipation? Have you started any new medications recently?

  17. 17

    Recent childbirth and thoughts or feelings about the baby

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    Have you given birth recently? How have you been feeling as you care for your baby?

  18. 18

    Previous depressive episodes and treatment; family history of mental illness or suicide

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt this depressed before? Did you receive treatment then? Has anyone in your family had depression or died by suicide?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
