Prioritize the patient's condition. Elevated blood pressure, severe headache, visual disturbances, upper abdominal pain, or a marked decrease in fetal movement during prenatal care may indicate preeclampsia or fetal distress and requires immediate detailed obstetric assessment.

Prenatal visit: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Calculate gestational age and estimated due date using the first day of the last menstrual period, cycle regularity, and how the pregnancy was confirmed

    What to say to the patient

    What was the first day of your last period? Were your cycles regular? How was the pregnancy confirmed?

  2. 02

    Whether the pregnancy was planned and feelings about it, such as happiness or worry

    What to say to the patient

    Was this a planned pregnancy? How did you feel when you found out you were pregnant?

  3. 03

    Severity of nausea and vomiting in pregnancy: ability to eat and drink, vomiting frequency, and weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    How has your morning sickness been? Can you keep food and fluids down? Have you lost weight because of vomiting?

  4. 04

    Vaginal bleeding and lower abdominal pain, including unilateral pain

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had bleeding like a period or lower abdominal pain? Is the pain on just one side?

  5. 05

    Severe headache, flashing lights or blurred vision, epigastric pain, and sudden swelling of the face or hands

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a severe headache or seen flashing lights? Have you had pain in the upper middle part of your abdomen or sudden swelling of your face and hands?

  6. 06

    When fetal movement was first felt and any decrease in movement

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    When did you first feel the baby move? Has the baby been moving less than usual lately?

  7. 07

    Vaginal fluid leakage and regular abdominal tightening

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had watery fluid leaking from your vagina, or regular tightening of your abdomen?

  8. 08

    Previous pregnancies and deliveries: miscarriage, preterm birth, cesarean delivery, preeclampsia or gestational diabetes, postpartum hemorrhage, and birth defects or low birth weight

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been pregnant before? Did you have a miscarriage, preterm birth, cesarean delivery, or problems with blood pressure or blood sugar?

  9. 09

    Preexisting conditions affecting pregnancy: hypertension, diabetes, thyroid disease, epilepsy, heart or kidney disease, and depression

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have high blood pressure, diabetes, thyroid disease, epilepsy, or another medical condition?

  10. 10

    Medications taken around conception and during pregnancy, such as acne medication, antiseizure medication, warfarin, some blood pressure medications, or herbal medicine, and folic acid use

    What to say to the patient

    Have you taken any medications or herbal remedies before or after finding out you were pregnant? Are you taking folic acid?

  11. 11

    Alcohol, smoking or secondhand smoke since pregnancy began, radiation-based imaging, contact with raw meat or cat feces, and occupational hazards

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had any alcohol or smoked since becoming pregnant? Have you had X-rays or been exposed to chemicals at work?

  12. 12

    Rubella or varicella vaccination or immunity, hepatitis B, recent fever or rash, and STI history

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had rubella or chickenpox vaccines? Have you ever been told you have hepatitis B? Have you had a fever or rash recently?

  13. 13

    Dysuria or urinary frequency, and malodorous or itchy vaginal discharge

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have pain when you urinate, or discharge that smells unusual or causes itching?

  14. 14

    Both partners' ages and family history of genetic disease, congenital anomalies, chromosomal abnormalities, or twins

    What to say to the patient

    Does your partner or anyone on either side of the family have a genetic condition or a birth defect?

  15. 15

    Prepregnancy and current weight, diet, and caffeine intake

    What to say to the patient

    How much did you weigh before pregnancy? Are you eating well? How many cups of coffee do you drink a day?

  16. 16

    Ask permission before discussing mood, including depression or anxiety, and safety at home

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone these questions. How has your mood been lately? Do you feel safe at home?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Seated blood pressure, weight and gain from prepregnancy weight, and height and BMI at the first visit

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Adult anthropometric assessment, including waist and neck circumference Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Measure from the pubic symphysis to the uterine fundus with a tape; after 20 weeks, the measurement in centimeters roughly corresponds to gestational age in weeks

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Check the fetal heart rate with Doppler (normal range: 110–160 beats/min)

    • Technique card pending
  4. 04

    In later pregnancy, use Leopold maneuvers to assess fetal lie and presentation

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Assess swelling of the hands, face, and legs, and check deep tendon reflexes if indicated

    • Assessment of pitting edema ↗
    • Patellar reflex (L2–L4, primarily L4) ↗
  6. 06

    At the first visit, explain pelvic examination and cervical cytology if indicated

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
