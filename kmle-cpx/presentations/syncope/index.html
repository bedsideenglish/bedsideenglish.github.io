Prioritize the patient's condition. A history of heart disease, syncope during exertion or while supine, severe palpitations or chest pain, or a family history of sudden death suggests high-risk cardiac syncope. Stop further teaching examinations and arrange urgent cardiovascular assessment even if some findings appear reassuring.

Syncope: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Circumstances and posture at the time of collapse: standing, sitting, or lying down, and activity at the time

    What to say to the patient

    What was happening when you passed out? Were you standing or sitting?

  2. 02

    Occurrence during exercise or while lying down

    What to say to the patient

    Did you pass out while exercising or climbing stairs? Has it ever happened while you were lying down?

  3. 03

    Presence or absence of prodromal symptoms: sweating, nausea, darkening vision, or ear fullness

    What to say to the patient

    Before you passed out, did you break into a sweat or feel as though your vision was going dark?

  4. 04

    Palpitations, chest pain, or shortness of breath before the collapse

    What to say to the patient

    Did you have a racing heartbeat or chest pain before you passed out?

  5. 05

    Triggers: prolonged standing, heat, pain or fright, urination or coughing, and standing up suddenly

    What to say to the patient

    Had you been standing for a long time, were you somewhere hot, or had you just stood up quickly?

  6. 06

    Duration of loss of consciousness and witness account

    What to say to the patient

    How long were you unconscious? What did anyone who saw it tell you?

  7. 07

    Stiffening or shaking of the body, tongue biting, and urinary incontinence

    What to say to the patient

    While you were unconscious, did you shake, bite your tongue, or lose control of your bladder?

  8. 08

    Speed of recovery after regaining consciousness and duration of confusion

    What to say to the patient

    Were you alert right away when you woke up, or did you feel confused for a while?

  9. 09

    Injuries sustained during the collapse, particularly head injury

    What to say to the patient

    Were you hurt when you fell? Did you hit your head?

  10. 10

    Unilateral weakness, slurred speech, headache, and visual changes

    What to say to the patient

    After you woke up, did you have any weakness in your arms or legs, or any slurred speech?

  11. 11

    Previous syncopal episodes and their frequency

    What to say to the patient

    Have you passed out like this before? How often?

  12. 12

    Vomiting, diarrhea, reduced food or fluid intake, and bleeding such as black stools

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had vomiting or diarrhea recently? Any black stools?

  13. 13

    Use of diabetes medications or insulin, and food intake before the collapse

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take medication for diabetes? Had you eaten before you passed out?

  14. 14

    Recent addition or change of medications, including antihypertensives, diuretics, prostate medications, and sleeping pills

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently started or changed any blood pressure medication or other medications?

  15. 15

    Preexisting heart disease, including myocardial infarction, heart failure, valvular disease, or a heart murmur

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have a heart condition, or have you ever been told you have a heart murmur?

  16. 16

    Family history of sudden death at a young age or unexplained syncope

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family died suddenly at a young age?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Measure supine and standing blood pressure and pulse to assess for orthostatic hypotension

    • Orthostatic blood pressure and pulse measurement ↗
  2. 02

    Assess pulse rate and regularity, and compare blood pressure in both arms

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  3. 03

    Auscultate the heart for murmurs, including aortic stenosis

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
  4. 04

    Brief neurologic examination: consciousness, facial symmetry, and limb strength

    • Upper limb strength: shoulder and elbow ↗
    • Lower limb strength testing (nerve roots L2–S1) ↗
    • Pronator drift test ↗
    • Facial movements: comparison of the upper and lower face ↗
  5. 05

    Check for head injury and bite marks along the side of the tongue

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Inspect and palpate for head trauma and scalp hematoma Technique card pending

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
