Prioritize the patient's condition. Discharge or bleeding accompanied by severe lower abdominal pain, fever, syncope, or possible pregnancy requires prompt clinical assessment. In an unstable patient, do not delay urgent assessment to finish a detailed examination or specimen collection.

Vaginal discharge and vaginal bleeding: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset, previous similar symptoms, and prior treatment

    What to say to the patient

    When did the discharge increase? Has this happened before?

  2. 02

    Discharge color and consistency: white clumps, thin gray fluid, yellow or green froth, or pus

    What to say to the patient

    What color is the discharge? Is it clumpy like cottage cheese, thin and gray, or yellow or green and frothy?

  3. 03

    Odor, including a fishy smell, and whether it worsens after intercourse

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed an odor? Is there a fishy smell that gets stronger after sex?

  4. 04

    Vulvar itching, burning, and swelling

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have any itching, burning, or swelling around your vulva?

  5. 05

    Relationship to the menstrual cycle

    What to say to the patient

    Does the amount of discharge change during your cycle? Is it worse just before your period?

  6. 06

    Painful urination (dysuria)

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have pain or burning when you urinate?

  7. 07

    Postcoital and intermenstrual bleeding

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had bleeding after sex or between periods?

  8. 08

    Lower abdominal pain and fever

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had lower abdominal pain or a fever?

  9. 09

    Dyspareunia

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have pain during sex?

  10. 10

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about recent intercourse, new or multiple partners, and condom use

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone these questions, so please feel free to answer openly. Have you had sex with a new partner recently? Did you use condoms?

  11. 11

    Partner's symptoms, including urethral discharge or dysuria

    What to say to the patient

    Has your partner mentioned pain with urination or any discharge?

  12. 12

    Recent antibiotics, diabetes, steroid use, or pregnancy

    What to say to the patient

    Have you taken antibiotics recently? Do you have diabetes, or are you pregnant?

  13. 13

    Vaginal douching or cleansers, tight clothing, and tampon use

    What to say to the patient

    Do you wash inside your vagina or use vaginal cleansers? Have you ever forgotten to remove a tampon?

  14. 14

    Contraception, including oral contraceptives or an intrauterine device

    What to say to the patient

    What do you use for birth control?

  15. 15

    Cervical cancer screening and HPV vaccination history

    What to say to the patient

    When was your last cervical cancer screening test? Have you had the HPV vaccine?

  16. 16

    Menopausal status and vaginal dryness

    Applies to: women age 45 or older

    What to say to the patient

    Have you gone through menopause? Have you noticed vaginal dryness?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

    Check vital signs, including temperature

  2. 02

    Assess lower abdominal tenderness

    • Abdominal inspection ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
