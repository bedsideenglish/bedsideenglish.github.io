Prioritize the patient's condition. For signs suggesting major internal bleeding, such as severe headache, melena, hematemesis, or hematuria, or for hemodynamic instability, arrange urgent medical assessment and activate resuscitation support without delay.

Easy bruising: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset, sites, size, and number of bruises, and whether they occur without recalled trauma

    What to say to the patient

    When did you start bruising easily? Where do the bruises usually appear? Do they happen even when you don't remember bumping into anything?

  2. 02

    Whether lesions are pinpoint petechiae, larger bruises, or bleeding into muscles or joints

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get tiny red spots on your skin, or larger bruises? Have your joints ever been swollen and painful?

  3. 03

    Gingival bleeding, recurrent epistaxis, and prolonged bleeding from small cuts

    What to say to the patient

    Do your gums bleed when you brush, or do you get frequent nosebleeds? Do small cuts take a long time to stop bleeding?

  4. 04

    Heavier periods, clots, and prolonged menstrual bleeding

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    Have your periods become heavier, with clots, or lasted longer than usual?

  5. 05

    Dangerous bleeding: black or bloody stools, hematemesis, hematuria, or sudden severe headache with vomiting or visual changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had black stools, vomited blood, or noticed red urine? Any sudden severe headache?

  6. 06

    Prolonged bleeding after dental extraction, surgery, or childbirth

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had bleeding that was hard to stop after a tooth extraction or surgery?

  7. 07

    Clues to bone marrow disease: fever, recurrent infections, marked fatigue or pallor, shortness of breath, bone pain, weight loss, and sweating

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had frequent fevers or infections? Have you been very tired or short of breath, lost weight, or had night sweats?

  8. 08

    Medications or supplements that increase bleeding, including aspirin, antiplatelets, warfarin or other oral anticoagulants, NSAIDs, corticosteroids, ginkgo, or omega-3s

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take aspirin, blood thinners, pain relievers, or steroids? What about supplements such as ginkgo or omega-3s?

  9. 09

    Recent viral infection, newly started medication such as an antibiotic, or heparin exposure

    What to say to the patient

    Did you have a cold or start any new medication in the weeks before the bruising began?

  10. 10

    Liver disease, alcohol intake, jaundice, and abdominal distention

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been told you have liver disease, or do you drink heavily? Have your eyes looked yellow, or has your abdomen become swollen?

  11. 11

    Restricted diet or reduced food intake, low vegetable or fruit intake, prolonged diarrhea or malabsorption, and long-term antibiotic use

    What to say to the patient

    Do you eat a varied diet? Do you eat very few vegetables or fruits, or have ongoing diarrhea?

  12. 12

    Palpable red spots on the legs with joint or abdominal pain

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had raised red spots on your legs along with joint pain or abdominal pain?

  13. 13

    Family history of a bleeding tendency, hemophilia, or von Willebrand disease

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family bleed for a long time or bruise easily?

  14. 14

    Ask permission before discussing whether bruises resulted from someone hurting the patient and whether they are safe at home

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone about this. Could these bruises be from someone hitting or hurting you? Do you feel safe at home?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
