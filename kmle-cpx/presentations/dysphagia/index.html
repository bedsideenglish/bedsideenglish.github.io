Prioritize the patient's condition. In patients with oropharyngeal dysphagia, decreased consciousness, shortness of breath, or severe choking, an unsafe water swallow challenge risks acute airway obstruction and aspiration. Avoid oral provocation and arrange immediate clinical assessment.

Difficulty swallowing: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset and course: progressively worsening or intermittent

    What to say to the patient

    When did swallowing become difficult? Is it getting worse, or does it happen only occasionally?

  2. 02

    Difficulty initiating a swallow versus a sticking sensation in the chest afterward, and the perceived location of obstruction

    What to say to the patient

    Is it hard to start swallowing, or does food get stuck in your chest after you swallow? Where does it feel stuck?

  3. 03

    Difficulty with solids alone or with liquids from the outset

    What to say to the patient

    Is it only foods like rice or meat that are hard to swallow, or is water difficult, too?

  4. 04

    Episodes of food impaction and whether water is needed to help food pass

    What to say to the patient

    Has food ever gotten stuck and wouldn't go down? Do you need to drink water to get it down?

  5. 05

    Choking or coughing with swallowing, nasal regurgitation, and recurrent pneumonia

    What to say to the patient

    Do you choke or cough when you swallow, or does food come out through your nose? Have you had pneumonia?

  6. 06

    Regurgitation of undigested food, halitosis, and gurgling in the neck

    What to say to the patient

    Does undigested food come back up, or have you noticed bad breath? Any gurgling in your neck?

  7. 07

    Odynophagia

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt when you swallow?

  8. 08

    Long-standing heartburn or acid regurgitation

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had heartburn or acid coming back up for a long time?

  9. 09

    Squeezing chest pain

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had squeezing chest pain when swallowing or at other times?

  10. 10

    Weight loss and loss of appetite

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost weight recently? About how many kilograms, and over how many months?

  11. 11

    Hematemesis, black stools, and anemia

    What to say to the patient

    Have you vomited blood or had black stools?

  12. 12

    Hoarseness

    What to say to the patient

    Has your voice become hoarse or changed?

  13. 13

    Slurred speech, unilateral weakness, ptosis or diplopia, weakness worse in the evening, and hand tremor

    What to say to the patient

    Has your speech been slurred, or have your arms or legs felt weak? Have your eyelids been drooping, or do you feel weaker in the evening?

  14. 14

    Asthma, allergic rhinitis, atopic dermatitis, and food allergy

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have asthma, hay fever, eczema, or food allergies?

  15. 15

    Taking pills without water or while lying down, including antibiotics, osteoporosis medication, or iron; immunosuppression

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take pills without water, or lie down right after taking them? Do you have a condition or take medication that weakens your immune system?

  16. 16

    Finger color changes in the cold and skin tightening

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Do your fingers turn white or blue in the cold, or has your skin become tight or hard?

  17. 17

    Neck mass or thyroid enlargement

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt a lump in your neck or been told the front of your neck is swollen?

  18. 18

    Smoking and alcohol intake as risk factors for esophageal cancer

    What to say to the patient

    How much do you smoke? How often do you drink alcohol, and how much?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

    Assess vital signs, weight, nutritional status, and hydration

    • Fluid status: oral mucosa and skin turgor ↗
    • Measure weight and assess nutritional status Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Inspect the mouth, including teeth, white coating, and tongue movements

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
  3. 03

    Palpate cervical and supraclavicular lymph nodes and the thyroid

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
