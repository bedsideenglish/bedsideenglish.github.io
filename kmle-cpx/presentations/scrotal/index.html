Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent assessment for sudden or severe scrotal pain because of possible testicular torsion. A single reassuring finding or waiting for ultrasound must not delay specialist assessment.

Scrotal pain and swelling: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Time and pattern of onset: sudden or gradual, and whether the pain woke the patient from sleep

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Did the pain begin suddenly, or get worse gradually over several days?

  2. 02

    Location: unilateral or bilateral; testis itself, above the testis, or extending into the groin

    What to say to the patient

    Which side hurts? Is it the testicle itself, the area above it, or your groin?

  3. 03

    Pain severity and character, including absence of pain

    What to say to the patient

    How bad is the pain on a scale of 0 to 10? Or is there swelling without any pain?

  4. 04

    Pain radiating to the lower abdomen, groin, or flank

    What to say to the patient

    Does the pain spread to your lower abdomen or side?

  5. 05

    Previous similar pain that resolved spontaneously

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had similar sudden pain before that went away on its own?

  6. 06

    Nausea and vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt nauseated or vomited?

  7. 07

    Changes in swelling or lump size, including differences between standing and lying down

    What to say to the patient

    Is the swelling getting bigger? Does its size change when you stand up or lie down?

  8. 08

    Dragging or heavy sensation and worsening with prolonged standing

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel heaviness or dragging in your scrotum? Is it worse after standing for a long time?

  9. 09

    Painless, firm mass

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt a hard lump in your testicle even though it doesn't hurt?

  10. 10

    Fever, dysuria, urinary frequency, and urethral discharge

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever, pain when urinating, or any pus-like discharge from your urethra?

  11. 11

    Scrotal skin color changes, including redness or darkening, blisters, and foul odor

    What to say to the patient

    Has the skin of your scrotum become red or dark? Have you noticed blisters or an unusual smell?

  12. 12

    Trauma and onset during exercise or sexual activity

    What to say to the patient

    Did you have an injury, or did the pain start while exercising?

  13. 13

    Recent parotid swelling or mumps

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had swelling below your ear or had mumps?

  14. 14

    Whether the testis was originally in the scrotum; history of undescended testis or testicular surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Did you have an undescended testicle as a child, or have you ever had testicular surgery?

  15. 15

    Weight loss, back pain, cough, and breast enlargement

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently lost weight, had back pain or a cough, or noticed your breasts getting larger?

  16. 16

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about sexual activity, new partners, and condom use

    Applies to: adults

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone these questions. Have you been sexually active recently? Have you used condoms?

  17. 17

    Recent urinary catheterization, cystoscopy, or prostate procedures

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had a urinary catheter or a urologic procedure?

  18. 18

    Difficulty conceiving and conditions associated with impaired immunity, such as diabetes

    Applies to: adults

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had difficulty conceiving? Do you have diabetes?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
