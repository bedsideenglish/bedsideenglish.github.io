An absence of visible injuries does not exclude injury. Explain examination, photography, and evidence collection separately and obtain consent for each.

Domestic violence and sexual assault: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Interview the patient in a safe space without the perpetrator or companions present; explain confidentiality and its limits, including mandatory reporting of matters such as child abuse

    What to say to the patient

    It's just the two of us in this room. I won't share what you tell me without your permission. If there's something I must report, such as a child being in danger, I'll let you know beforehand.

  2. 02

    When and where the event occurred, who was involved and their relationship to the patient, and what happened

    What to say to the patient

    If you're comfortable, could you tell me as much as you feel able to about what happened, when and where it happened, and who was involved?

  3. 03

    Injured areas, mechanism (fists, kicks, objects, or weapons), and current pain

    What to say to the patient

    Where were you injured, and how? Were you punched or kicked, or was an object or weapon used? Where does it hurt now?

  4. 04

    Strangulation, head blows or impact, loss of consciousness, vomiting or dizziness, hoarseness, or difficulty swallowing

    What to say to the patient

    Were you strangled or hit on the head? Did you lose consciousness or vomit? Has your voice become hoarse, or is swallowing difficult?

  5. 05

    First or recurrent episode, increasing frequency or severity, threats with weapons, and death threats

    What to say to the patient

    Has this happened before? Is it happening more often or becoming more severe? Have they threatened you with a weapon or threatened to kill you?

  6. 06

    Whether it is safe to return home today, where the perpetrator is now, and whether the patient has a safe place to stay

    What to say to the patient

    Would it be safe for you to go home today? Where is that person now? Do you have somewhere safe to stay?

  7. 07

    Children or dependent family members at home, and whether they experience or witness violence

    What to say to the patient

    Are there children or older adults you care for at home? Are they being hurt, or do they witness the violence?

  8. 08

    Coercive control or surveillance, restricting access to money, and preventing contact with family or friends

    What to say to the patient

    Do they check your phone, restrict your access to money, or keep you from seeing family or friends?

  9. 09

    For sexual assault: time of the assault and whether the patient washed or changed clothes afterward, to inform evidence preservation

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    About what time did it happen? Have you washed or changed clothes since then? If you still have the clothes you were wearing, keep them in a paper bag rather than throwing them away.

  10. 10

    After asking permission, establish the nature of the sexual assault only to the extent needed for treatment, including sites of penetration, condom use, and number of perpetrators

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I need to ask something sensitive to help with your treatment. You don't have to answer anything that's too difficult. Do you remember whether there was penetration? Was a condom used?

  11. 11

    Memory gaps or loss of consciousness after a drink, raising concern for drug-facilitated assault

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had any gaps in your memory after drinking something?

  12. 12

    Sleep, nightmares, intrusive memories, exaggerated startle, anxiety, and depression

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been able to sleep since it happened? Do memories of it keep coming back, or do you find yourself startling easily?

  13. 13

    Thoughts of suicide or self-harm

    What to say to the patient

    Have things felt so overwhelming that you've thought about wanting to die?

  14. 14

    Family or friends who can help, and previous contact with police, counseling services, or shelters

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family or any friends know what happened? Have you asked the police or a counseling service for help before?

  15. 15

    Help the patient wants: treatment only, documentation or a medical certificate, reporting, or a shelter

    What to say to the patient

    What kind of help would you like today? Please let me know whether you'd like treatment only, documentation, or help making a report.

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
