Prioritize the patient's condition. Do not require a routine physical examination or temperature check solely for vaccination in someone who appears healthy. Evaluate current symptoms or previous vaccine reactions when relevant. Acute shortness of breath or altered mental status takes priority and requires urgent assessment.

Vaccination counseling: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Requested vaccine and reason: routine childhood vaccination, travel, employment or clinical placement, pregnancy planning, or age-based recommendation

    What to say to the patient

    Which vaccination did you come in to discuss? Was there a particular reason?

  2. 02

    Previous vaccination records, such as Korea's vaccination portal or the child's health record, and missed vaccinations

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have a record of your vaccinations? Are there any you may have missed?

  3. 03

    Child's age in months and whether they were born preterm

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    How many months old is your child now? Were they born before their due date?

  4. 04

    Current health: fever, cough, vomiting or diarrhea, and lethargy

    What to say to the patient

    Are you feeling ill today, with a fever, cough, or diarrhea, for example?

  5. 05

    Severe reactions after previous vaccination: shortness of breath, hives, lip or facial swelling, collapse, high fever, or seizures

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had shortness of breath, hives, or facial swelling after a vaccination? What about a high fever or a seizure?

  6. 06

    Allergies to eggs, gelatin, antibiotics, or latex, and severity of reactions

    What to say to the patient

    Are you allergic to eggs, gelatin, or any medications? What kind of reaction did you have?

  7. 07

    Conditions or treatments that suppress immunity: chemotherapy, high-dose steroids, immunosuppressants or biologics, and HIV

    What to say to the patient

    Are you being treated for cancer, or taking steroids or other medications that suppress your immune system?

  8. 08

    Current pregnancy or plans to conceive within one month, relevant to live-vaccine contraindications

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    Are you pregnant, or planning to become pregnant within a month? I ask because some vaccines can't be given during pregnancy.

  9. 09

    Recent blood transfusion or immunoglobulin administration, including interval before live vaccination

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a blood transfusion or an immunoglobulin injection in the past few months?

  10. 10

    Seizures, progressive neurologic disease, and Guillain-Barré syndrome after a previous vaccination

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had seizures or a neurologic condition? Have you ever developed weakness in your arms or legs after a vaccination?

  11. 11

    Thrombocytopenia or anticoagulant use, relevant to bleeding at an intramuscular injection site

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have a bleeding disorder, or do you take blood thinners?

  12. 12

    Chronic conditions affecting additional vaccine needs: heart, lung, liver, or kidney disease, diabetes, or splenectomy

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have heart, lung, liver, or kidney disease, or diabetes? Have you had your spleen removed?

  13. 13

    Occupation, such as health care or childcare, and overseas travel plans, including destination and departure date

    What to say to the patient

    Do you work in health care or childcare? If you're planning to travel abroad, where are you going and when?

  14. 14

    Newborns, immunocompromised people, or pregnant people in the household

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Is there a newborn, anyone with a weakened immune system, or anyone who is pregnant in your household?

  15. 15

    Concerns and information heard about vaccines, including adverse effects or receiving several vaccines at once

    What to say to the patient

    Please feel free to tell me about any concerns you have or anything you've heard about vaccines.

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Measure temperature to assess for fever

    Consider when symptoms or a separate assessment warrant it · not a required prevaccination test

    • Technique card pending
  2. 02

    General appearance (lethargy, dehydration, respiratory distress, or rash), pulse, and respiratory rate

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
  3. 03

    Examine the throat and ears and auscultate the chest if symptoms warrant

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Posterior lung auscultation sequence ↗
    • Adult otoscopy: safe approach and tympanic membrane landmarks ↗
  4. 04

    Inspect the injection site: anterolateral thigh in infants and deltoid in older children and adults

    • Technique card pending
  5. 05

    For children, measure height and weight and assess overall development

    Consider when symptoms or a separate assessment warrant it · not a required prevaccination test

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
