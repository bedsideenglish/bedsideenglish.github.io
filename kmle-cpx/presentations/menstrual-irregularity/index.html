Prioritize the patient's condition. Dizziness, syncope, or orthostatic hypotension with heavy bleeding are warning signs of hypovolemic shock and require emergency assessment without delay. Always consider acute pregnancy-related complications, including ectopic pregnancy, in women of reproductive age.

Menstrual abnormalities: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Usual menstrual cycle and recent changes in interval or regularity

    What to say to the patient

    How many days apart were your periods before? How have they changed recently?

  2. 02

    Menstrual flow: number of pads, clots, and leakage

    What to say to the patient

    How heavy are your periods? How many pads do you use each day? Do you pass clots or bleed through onto your clothes?

  3. 03

    Duration of menstruation, including whether it lasts more than 7 days

    What to say to the patient

    How many days does each period last?

  4. 04

    Duration of amenorrhea in months

    What to say to the patient

    Have you missed any periods? What's the longest you've gone without one, in months?

  5. 05

    Intermenstrual and postcoital bleeding

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had spotting between periods or bleeding after sex?

  6. 06

    Dizziness, shortness of breath, palpitations, and severe fatigue

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been dizzy, short of breath, or very tired?

  7. 07

    Menstrual cramps, lower abdominal pain or pressure, and urinary frequency

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have menstrual cramps or a heavy pressure in your lower abdomen? Do you need to urinate often?

  8. 08

    Acne, increased hair growth, and weight gain

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had more acne or hair growth on your chin or abdomen? Have you gained weight?

  9. 09

    Heat or cold intolerance, weight changes, and palpitations

    What to say to the patient

    Are you more sensitive to heat or cold than before? Have you felt your heart racing?

  10. 10

    Galactorrhea, headache, and visual disturbances

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had milky nipple discharge when you weren't pregnant or breastfeeding, headaches, or a feeling that your field of vision is narrowing?

  11. 11

    Recent weight changes, stress, excessive exercise, or dietary restriction

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost or gained a lot of weight recently? Have you been under a lot of stress or exercising excessively?

  12. 12

    Epistaxis, gum bleeding, easy bruising, excessive bleeding after dental extraction or surgery, and heavy periods since menarche

    What to say to the patient

    Do you often have nosebleeds, bleeding gums, or easy bruising? Did you bleed for a long time after a tooth extraction? Have your periods been heavy since they first started?

  13. 13

    Oral contraceptives or hormonal medications, intrauterine device, anticoagulants, and antipsychotics

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take birth control pills or hormones, or have an IUD? What about blood thinners?

  14. 14

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about sexual activity and contraception

    What to say to the patient

    I need to ask this for your care: are you sexually active? What do you use for birth control?

  15. 15

    Childbirth history, current pregnancy plans, or infertility

    What to say to the patient

    Have you given birth before? Are you planning to become pregnant now?

  16. 16

    Menopausal symptoms such as hot flashes and night sweats

    Applies to: women age 45 or older

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had hot flashes or night sweats?

  17. 17

    Endometrial cancer risk factors (obesity, diabetes, and longstanding anovulation) and cervical cancer screening history

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have diabetes? When was your last cervical cancer screening test?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Check blood pressure and pulse to assess bleeding and anemia

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  2. 02

    Check for conjunctival pallor

    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
  3. 03

    Assess height and weight (BMI), hirsutism, acne, and acanthosis nigricans

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Detailed skin inspection for acanthosis nigricans, acne, and hirsutism, with measurements for BMI Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Thyroid palpation

    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
  5. 05

    Palpate the abdomen for an enlarged uterus or pelvic masses

    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
  6. 06

    Explain the need for a pelvic examination, including a speculum and bimanual examination

    • Speculum examination: vaginal approach and cervical view ↗
    • Bimanual pelvic examination: cervix, uterus, and adnexa ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
