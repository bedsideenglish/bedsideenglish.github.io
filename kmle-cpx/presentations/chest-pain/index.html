Prioritize the patient's condition. For persistent or suddenly worsening chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, or signs of shock, prioritize immediate emergency assessment rather than waiting to complete the examination. A normal physical examination does not rule out acute coronary syndrome, aortic dissection, or pulmonary embolism.

Chest pain: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Time and pattern of onset (sudden or gradual), and activity when the pain began

    What to say to the patient

    When did the pain start? Did it come on suddenly? What were you doing at the time?

  2. 02

    Pain location: central chest, one side, or a spot the patient can point to with a finger

    What to say to the patient

    Where does it hurt? Could you point to it?

  3. 03

    Pain quality: squeezing, tearing, stabbing, or burning

    What to say to the patient

    What does the pain feel like? Is it a tightness, a tearing pain, or a stabbing pain?

  4. 04

    Radiation to the left arm, jaw, back, or between the shoulder blades

    What to say to the patient

    Does the pain spread to your arm, jaw, or back?

  5. 05

    Duration of each episode (seconds, minutes, or hours or longer) and recurrence

    What to say to the patient

    How long does the pain last each time? Is it there all the time, or does it come and go?

  6. 06

    Pain severity (0–10) and changes over time

    What to say to the patient

    If the worst pain you've had was a 10, what number would you give it now?

  7. 07

    Worsening with exertion and relief with rest; occurrence at rest or during sleep

    What to say to the patient

    Does it get worse when you walk or climb stairs and better when you rest? Do you also get it when you're resting?

  8. 08

    Changes with breathing, coughing, lying down, or leaning forward; tenderness to pressure

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt more when you take a deep breath or lie down? Does leaning forward help?

  9. 09

    Relationship to meals, worsening when lying down, acid regurgitation, and pain with swallowing

    What to say to the patient

    Does it get worse after meals, or do you get a sour taste coming up into your mouth?

  10. 10

    Associated symptoms: sweating, nausea or vomiting, shortness of breath, dizziness, or fainting

    What to say to the patient

    When you had the pain, did you also feel sweaty, short of breath, or dizzy?

  11. 11

    Fever, cough, and sputum

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever, a cough, or any phlegm?

  12. 12

    Recent viral illness, such as a cold or a flu-like illness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a cold or a flu-like illness recently?

  13. 13

    Unilateral leg swelling or pain, recent surgery, long-haul travel, or prolonged immobility

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had surgery or taken a long flight? Have you noticed swelling in one leg?

  14. 14

    Rash or blisters over the painful area

    What to say to the patient

    Have you noticed a rash or any blisters where it hurts?

  15. 15

    Severe anxiety, fear of dying, and tingling in the hands or feet

    What to say to the patient

    When it hurts, do you feel very anxious or get tingling in your hands or feet?

  16. 16

    Cardiovascular risk factors: hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, smoking, and family history of premature heart disease

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have high blood pressure, diabetes, or high cholesterol? Has anyone in your family had heart disease at a young age?

  17. 17

    Previous similar pain and prior cardiac testing or procedures, including stent placement

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had pain like this before? Have you had any heart tests or procedures?

  18. 18

    Recent chest trauma, strenuous exercise, or repetitive movements

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently injured your chest or done any strenuous exercise?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Measure blood pressure in both arms, pulse, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, and temperature

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Radial, brachial, and carotid pulse locations ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Pulse oximetry: placement, signal quality, and limitations ↗
    • Respiratory rate, rhythm, and effort ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Look for sweating, pallor, distended neck veins, and skin lesions over the painful area

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Jugular venous pressure (JVP) assessment ↗
    • Palpate for focal chest wall tenderness and inspect for skin lesions Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Palpate the chest wall for tenderness and reproduction of the pain

    • Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Cardiac auscultation for murmurs and a pericardial friction rub

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Cardiac auscultation while sitting and leaning forward ↗
  5. 05

    Lung percussion and auscultation for asymmetric breath sounds and crackles

    • Posterior lung auscultation sequence ↗
    • Anterior and lateral lung auscultation ↗
    • Checking tracheal position at the suprasternal notch ↗
    • Posterior chest percussion with the arms crossed in front ↗
    • Posterior chest expansion using the thumbs ↗
  6. 06

    Check for epigastric tenderness, leg swelling, and calf tenderness

    • Assessment of pitting edema ↗
    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Leg circumference measurement and objective assessment for deep vein thrombosis (DVT) Technique card pending

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
