Prioritize the patient's condition. With alcohol withdrawal seizures or delirium tremens, or severe respiratory depression or coma from an opioid overdose, stop further teaching examinations and proceed to airway management, urgent resuscitation, and emergency assessment.

Alcohol and drug use: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Substances used, amount per occasion, frequency, and route, including injection

    What to say to the patient

    What do you usually use, and how often? How much do you use at a time?

  2. 02

    First use and progression to larger amounts (tolerance)

    What to say to the patient

    When did you start using it? Do you now need more to get the same effect?

  3. 03

    Time of last use

    What to say to the patient

    When did you last have any?

  4. 04

    Unsuccessful attempts to reduce use, craving, and using more than intended

    What to say to the patient

    Have you tried to cut down or stop but found it difficult? Do you have cravings that are hard to resist?

  5. 05

    Alcohol screening questions: feeling a need to cut down, criticism from others, guilt, and morning drinking to steady oneself

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt you should cut down on drinking? Has anyone criticized your drinking? Do you drink in the morning to steady yourself?

  6. 06

    Work, family, financial, or legal problems, and use in hazardous situations such as driving

    What to say to the patient

    Has alcohol or drug use caused problems at work or with your family? Have you driven after drinking?

  7. 07

    Withdrawal symptoms: hand tremor, sweating, insomnia, or anxiety; or muscle aches, rhinorrhea, tearing, diarrhea, or goosebumps

    What to say to the patient

    When you don't use it, do your hands shake, do you sweat, or have trouble sleeping? Do you get body aches, a runny nose, or diarrhea?

  8. 08

    Previous withdrawal seizures, hallucinations, or delirium

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had seizures or hallucinations when you stopped using it?

  9. 09

    Previous overdose or loss of consciousness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever taken so much that you lost consciousness or needed emergency care?

  10. 10

    Needle sharing and sexually transmitted infection risk

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone about this. If you've injected drugs, have you ever shared needles?

  11. 11

    Symptoms of alcohol-related complications: jaundice, hematemesis or black stools, upper abdominal pain, limb tingling, and memory problems

    What to say to the patient

    Have your eyes looked yellow, have you vomited blood, or have your stools been black? Any tingling in your hands or feet?

  12. 12

    Concurrent use of other substances, including sleeping pills, pain medications, or tobacco

    What to say to the patient

    Besides alcohol, do you also use sleeping pills, pain medications, or tobacco?

  13. 13

    Symptoms of depression or anxiety, and suicidal thoughts

    What to say to the patient

    How has your mood been lately? Have things been so difficult that you've thought about wanting to die?

  14. 14

    Previous attempts at abstinence or treatment, medications, and mutual-help groups

    What to say to the patient

    Have you received treatment or attended a group to help you stop drinking?

  15. 15

    Readiness to change, including reasons for quitting, importance, and confidence

    What to say to the patient

    What makes you want to quit? On a scale of 0 to 10, how confident are you that you can?

  16. 16

    Family support, other sources of support, and living environment

    What to say to the patient

    Is there a family member or friend who could support you in quitting?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Assess pulse, blood pressure, and temperature for autonomic overactivity associated with withdrawal

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess withdrawal severity by observing tremor, sweating, pupil size, goosebumps, yawning, and rhinorrhea

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Pupillary light reflex and swinging flashlight test (RAPD) ↗
  3. 03

    Check for jaundice, spider angiomas, hepatomegaly, and ascites

    Select as clinically indicated

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Asterixis ↗
    • Liver palpation with respiration ↗
    • Shifting dullness ↗
    • Inspect for peripheral vascular signs such as spider angiomas and palmar erythema Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Check for injection marks, skin infections, and a heart murmur

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Inspection for injection marks and skin or soft tissue infection Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Assess eye movements, gait, and orientation

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Asterixis ↗
    • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic positions of gaze ↗
    • Gait and tandem gait ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
