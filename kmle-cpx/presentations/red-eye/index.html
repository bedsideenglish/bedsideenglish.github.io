Prioritize the patient's condition. Red eye with sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, corneal clouding, a mid-dilated pupil without a light response, or headache with vomiting suggests a sight-threatening emergency such as acute angle-closure glaucoma, a corneal ulcer, or endophthalmitis. Immediate ophthalmologic assessment is needed.

Red eye: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Onset, course, and whether one or both sides are affected

    What to say to the patient

    When did the redness start? Is it in one eye or both?

  2. 02

    Distribution of redness: diffuse scleral surface redness, redness around the cornea, or a focal bright-red patch

    What to say to the patient

    Is the whole white of the eye red, is it redder around the colored part, or is there one sharply defined bright-red patch?

  3. 03

    Presence and character of pain: gritty foreign body sensation, stinging, or deep eye pain

    What to say to the patient

    Does your eye hurt? Does it feel gritty, like there's sand in it, or is the pain deep inside the eye?

  4. 04

    Reduced or blurred vision

    What to say to the patient

    Since the redness started, has your vision become blurry or worse than usual?

  5. 05

    Photophobia: pain on exposure to light

    What to say to the patient

    Does bright light bother your eyes or make them hurt?

  6. 06

    Rainbow-colored halos around lights, headache, and nausea or vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Do you see rainbow-colored rings around lights, or have a headache and feel as though you might vomit?

  7. 07

    Crusting and discharge characteristics: purulent, sticky, or watery; eyelids stuck together on waking

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have any eye discharge? Is it yellow and thick or clear and watery? Are your eyelids stuck together when you wake up?

  8. 08

    Itching

    What to say to the patient

    Are your eyes very itchy?

  9. 09

    Eyelid swelling or lumps, crusting at the lid margins, and burning

    What to say to the patient

    Are your eyelids swollen, or have you noticed a lump? Any crusting or burning along your eyelashes?

  10. 10

    Pain with eye movement, proptosis, diplopia, and fever

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt to move your eye, or does it seem to bulge? Have you had double vision or a fever?

  11. 11

    Recent cold symptoms and contact with someone with an eye infection

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had cold symptoms recently, or has anyone at home or work had an eye infection?

  12. 12

    Contact lens use, sleeping in lenses, and lens care

    What to say to the patient

    Do you wear contact lenses? Have you slept with them in?

  13. 13

    Eye trauma, foreign body, and chemical or welding light exposure

    What to say to the patient

    Have you injured your eye, gotten something in it, had a chemical splash, or been exposed to welding light?

  14. 14

    Seasonal recurrence, allergic rhinitis, and atopic disease

    What to say to the patient

    Does this happen in the same season each year? Do you have allergies, such as hay fever, or eczema?

  15. 15

    Dry or gritty eyes, prolonged screen use, dry environments, and dry mouth

    What to say to the patient

    Do your eyes feel dry or gritty, especially later in the day? Do you also have a dry mouth?

  16. 16

    Associated systemic conditions, including morning back stiffness, joint pain, recurrent oral ulcers, or intestinal inflammation

    What to say to the patient

    Is your back stiff and painful in the morning? Have your joints been swollen, or have you had frequent mouth ulcers?

  17. 17

    Blisters on the forehead or tip of the nose, and previous ocular herpes

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had blisters on your forehead or the tip of your nose? Have you had a herpes infection in your eye before?

  18. 18

    Anticoagulant or antiplatelet use, hypertension, and recent severe coughing, vomiting, or straining

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take blood thinners or have high blood pressure? Have you recently had severe coughing or done a lot of straining?

  19. 19

    Previous eye disease or surgery, glaucoma diagnosis, and recurrent similar symptoms

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with glaucoma or another eye condition, or had any eye surgery?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Measure visual acuity in each eye separately using an eye chart

    • Visual acuity and pinhole testing, one eye at a time ↗
  2. 02

    Use a penlight to inspect the redness pattern, discharge, eyelids, subconjunctival hemorrhage, and corneal clouding

    • Penlight anterior eye inspection: surface, cornea, and shadow ↗
  3. 03

    Assess pupil size and shape and the light reflex, including a fixed mid-dilated pupil

    • Pupillary light reflex and swinging flashlight test (RAPD) ↗
  4. 04

    Assess eye movements, proptosis, and tenderness around the orbit or over the lacrimal sac

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic positions of gaze ↗
    • Orbital or lacrimal sac tenderness and periorbital appearance Technique card pending
  5. 05

    If a foreign body sensation is present, evert the upper eyelid to look for a foreign body

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Use fluorescein staining to assess for corneal abrasions or ulcers

    • Corneal fluorescein staining: epithelial defects and recognition of leakage ↗
  7. 07

    Explain that intraocular pressure measurement is needed if glaucoma is suspected

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
