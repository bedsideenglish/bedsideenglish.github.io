Prioritize the patient's condition. A history of alcohol withdrawal seizures or delirium tremens, or current signs of autonomic overactivity such as tremor, sweating, restlessness, or hallucinations, indicates a risk of life-threatening deterioration. Arrange immediate medical monitoring and emergency assessment.

Smoking and alcohol counseling: interview questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    What the patient wants to reduce (tobacco, alcohol, or both), and the reasons and events motivating change

    What to say to the patient

    Would you like to cut back on smoking, drinking, or both? What led you to consider it?

  2. 02

    Tobacco product type, including cigarettes, heated tobacco, or vaping; daily quantity and duration in pack-years

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    What type of tobacco do you use, and how much each day? How old were you when you started?

  3. 03

    Nicotine dependence: time to first cigarette after waking, difficulty abstaining in smoke-free areas, and smoking when ill

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    How soon after waking do you have your first cigarette? Is it hard to manage in places where you can't smoke?

  4. 04

    Smoking triggers: after meals, coffee, drinking occasions, stress, and other smokers

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    When do you most want to smoke? Do people around you smoke, too?

  5. 05

    Previous attempts to quit smoking or reduce drinking: methods, medications, longest period of abstinence, reasons for restarting, and withdrawal symptoms

    What to say to the patient

    Have you tried to quit before? What did you try, and how long did you stop? What led you to start again?

  6. 06

    Drinking frequency, amount per occasion in glasses of soju or beer, and frequency of heavy drinking

    What to say to the patient

    How many days a week do you drink? If it's soju, how many glasses or bottles do you usually have at a time? How often do you drink a lot in one sitting?

  7. 07

    Alcohol-related problems: blackouts, injuries, impaired driving, and family or work problems

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had memory gaps or injuries after drinking, or driven after drinking? Has alcohol caused problems at home or work?

  8. 08

    Difficulty controlling use, craving, morning drinking, and concerns raised by others

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Once you start drinking, do you end up drinking more than you meant to? Do you drink in the morning to steady yourself, or have others suggested cutting back?

  9. 09

    Withdrawal symptoms such as tremor, sweating, insomnia, or anxiety, and previous withdrawal seizures or hallucinations

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    When you don't drink, do your hands shake, do you sweat, or have trouble sleeping? Have you ever had seizures or hallucinations when you stopped?

  10. 10

    Existing health problems: cough, sputum, breathlessness, chest pain, heartburn, jaundice, or elevated blood pressure or glucose

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a persistent cough or phlegm, or shortness of breath? Any chest pain or heartburn?

  11. 11

    Depression, anxiety, insomnia, use of tobacco or alcohol to cope with stress, and thoughts of wanting to die

    What to say to the patient

    How have your mood and sleep been? Do you turn to alcohol or tobacco when things are hard? Have you felt so overwhelmed that you've thought about wanting to die?

  12. 12

    Other substance use, including sleeping pills, sedatives, or pain medications

    What to say to the patient

    Apart from alcohol, do you often take sleeping pills, sedatives, or pain medication?

  13. 13

    Importance of change and confidence on a 0–10 scale; desire to quit and ambivalence

    What to say to the patient

    On a scale of 0 to 10, how important is quitting to you? How confident are you that you can do it?

  14. 14

    Before selecting smoking cessation medication, ask about seizures, pregnancy or breastfeeding, recent cardiac disease, kidney function, and depression treatment

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I'm asking to help choose a medication: have you ever had a seizure, or received treatment for heart disease recently?

  15. 15

    Smoking or drinking among family and coworkers, work-related drinking culture, and people who can help

    What to say to the patient

    Do many people at home or work smoke or drink with you? Is there someone who would support you in quitting?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?
