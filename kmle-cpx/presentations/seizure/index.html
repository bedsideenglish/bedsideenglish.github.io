Prioritize the patient's condition. If convulsions continue, seizures recur without recovery of consciousness, head trauma is present, or respiratory depression or cyanosis is observed, stop further teaching examination immediately and provide urgent clinical care, including airway protection and emergency assessment.

Seizures: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Seizure features, including witness account: stiffening, shaking, generalized or unilateral involvement, and whether the eyes were open

    What to say to the patient

    What did anyone who was there say they saw during the seizure? Did your whole body shake, or just one side?

  2. 02

    Seizure duration; recurrence without recovery of consciousness

    What to say to the patient

    How many minutes did the seizure last? Did another one happen before you regained consciousness?

  3. 03

    Circumstances and symptoms just before the seizure: unusual smells, a rising epigastric sensation, cold sweats, or dizziness

    What to say to the patient

    What were you doing just before you collapsed? Did you notice an unusual smell or a rising feeling in your stomach?

  4. 04

    Onset in one arm or one side of the face; eye or head deviation to one side

    What to say to the patient

    Did the shaking start in one arm or one side of your face? Did your head turn to one side?

  5. 05

    Lateral tongue biting or urinary incontinence

    What to say to the patient

    Did you bite your tongue or lose control of your urine?

  6. 06

    Duration of postictal confusion; muscle pain, headache, or transient weakness

    What to say to the patient

    How long did you feel confused after you woke up? Did your muscles ache, or did you have weakness on one side?

  7. 07

    Injuries during the seizure, including head or shoulder injury

    What to say to the patient

    Were you injured when you fell?

  8. 08

    First episode; previous seizures, staring spells, or morning myoclonic jerks

    What to say to the patient

    Has this happened before? Have you had staring spells or sudden arm jerks in the morning?

  9. 09

    Fever, headache, neck stiffness, or persistently reduced consciousness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever, headache, or stiff neck?

  10. 10

    Recently worsening headache, personality changes, or limb weakness

    What to say to the patient

    Have your headaches been getting worse lately, or have you had weakness in an arm or leg on one side?

  11. 11

    Triggers: sleep deprivation, heavy alcohol use, flashing lights, stress, or missed medication doses

    What to say to the patient

    Had you been short on sleep or drinking heavily before the seizure?

  12. 12

    Alcohol intake and recent cessation; use of medications that may provoke seizures

    What to say to the patient

    Were you drinking every day and then recently stopped? Have you started any new medications?

  13. 13

    Diabetes medication or insulin use and food intake; vomiting or diarrhea

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take diabetes medicine? Have you recently had trouble eating, or had vomiting or diarrhea?

  14. 14

    History of head trauma, stroke, brain surgery, or brain infection

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever had a serious head injury, brain surgery, or a stroke?

  15. 15

    For children: birth and developmental history, previous febrile seizures, immunizations, and recent febrile illness

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    Were there any problems at birth or with your child's development? Have they had a seizure with a fever before?

  16. 16

    Family history of epilepsy or febrile seizures

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have epilepsy, or did anyone have seizures with a fever as a child?

  17. 17

    Driving; hazardous work, such as working at heights or with machinery

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drive? Do you work at heights or operate machinery?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

  1. 01

    Measure vital signs, temperature, and blood glucose

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Capillary blood glucose · safe fingerstick sampling ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Level of consciousness and neurologic examination: pupils, focal weakness, and reflexes

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Pupillary light reflex and swinging flashlight test (RAPD) ↗
    • Plantar response: flexor and extensor (Babinski) ↗
    • Pronator drift test ↗
  3. 03

    Assess for neck stiffness

    • Neck stiffness: gentle passive neck flexion ↗
  4. 04

    Inspect the sides of the tongue for bite marks and look for head or body injuries

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
