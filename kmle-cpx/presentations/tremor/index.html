Prioritize the patient's condition. Sudden-onset tremor with one-sided weakness, impaired eye movements, slurred speech, or acute gait ataxia suggests a possible acute cerebrovascular disorder of the brainstem or cerebellum. Stop the teaching examination and arrange immediate urgent neurologic assessment.

Tremor and abnormal movements: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    When tremor occurs: at rest, while holding the arms out or holding a cup, or when approaching a target

    What to say to the patient

    When do your hands shake: when you're resting, or when you're holding a cup or writing?

  2. 02

    Affected areas: hands, head, voice, or legs; unilateral or bilateral

    What to say to the patient

    Do both hands shake, or just one? Does your head or voice shake too?

  3. 03

    Time of onset and rate of progression: sudden or gradual

    What to say to the patient

    When did the shaking start? Is it getting worse?

  4. 04

    Sudden-onset tremor with one-sided weakness or slurred speech

    What to say to the patient

    Did the shaking start suddenly? Did you also have weakness in an arm or leg on one side, or slurred speech?

  5. 05

    Aggravating or relieving factors: worsening with anxiety or improvement with alcohol

    What to say to the patient

    Does the shaking get worse when you're nervous? Does it ease up after you drink alcohol?

  6. 06

    Impact on daily activities: using a spoon, holding a cup, writing, or buttoning clothes

    What to say to the patient

    Does the shaking make it difficult to eat with a spoon or write?

  7. 07

    Changes in handwriting: progressively smaller or large and irregular

    What to say to the patient

    Has your handwriting gotten smaller, or has it become large and uneven?

  8. 08

    Slowness of movement, stiffness, short steps or shuffling, and changes in facial expression

    What to say to the patient

    Have your movements slowed down, has your body felt stiff, or have you started taking small, shuffling steps?

  9. 09

    Reduced sense of smell, constipation, shouting or kicking during sleep, or depression

    What to say to the patient

    Has your sense of smell gotten worse, or have you been constipated? Has anyone told you that you shout or kick in your sleep?

  10. 10

    Imbalance, unsteady gait, or slurred speech

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel unsteady or have trouble keeping your balance when you walk?

  11. 11

    Palpitations, heat intolerance, weight loss, or sweating

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a racing heart, trouble tolerating heat, or weight loss?

  12. 12

    Anxiety, stress, sleep deprivation, or tremor when fasting

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been under a lot of stress or had trouble sleeping? Is the shaking worse when you're hungry?

  13. 13

    Coffee and alcohol intake; recent cessation of alcohol use

    What to say to the patient

    How much coffee do you drink each day? Have you recently stopped drinking alcohol?

  14. 14

    Medications that can cause tremor or parkinsonism: bronchodilators, thyroid hormone, lithium, valproate, antidepressants, antipsychotics, or prokinetic agents

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take any medicines, such as asthma inhalers, thyroid medication, psychiatric or neurologic medication, or stomach medicines?

  15. 15

    Family history of tremor or Parkinson's disease

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have shaky hands or Parkinson's disease?

  16. 16

    In younger patients, clues to Wilson disease, such as liver disease or psychiatric symptoms

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever been told you have liver problems? Have you noticed any recent changes in your personality or mood?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
