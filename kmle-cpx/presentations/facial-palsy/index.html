Prioritize the patient's condition. Facial weakness with forehead sparing, or with other neurologic deficits such as limb weakness, slurred speech, or double vision, requires immediate urgent assessment and referral, with acute stroke a priority consideration.

Facial weakness: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset and speed: sudden, upon waking, progression over days, or gradual onset over weeks or longer

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Did it happen suddenly, or gradually worsen over several weeks?

  2. 02

    Ability to wrinkle the forehead and close the eyes completely

    What to say to the patient

    Can you wrinkle your forehead? Can you close your eyes tightly?

  3. 03

    Limb weakness or numbness, slurred speech, dizziness, or double vision

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had weakness or numbness in your arms or legs, slurred speech, or double vision?

  4. 04

    Severe headache, vomiting, or altered consciousness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a severe headache or any vomiting?

  5. 05

    Ear pain; blisters in the ear or mouth

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had ear pain or any blisters in your ear or mouth?

  6. 06

    Pain behind the ear before facial weakness began

    What to say to the patient

    Did you have pain behind your ear before your face became weak?

  7. 07

    Hearing loss, tinnitus, or vertigo

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had hearing loss or ringing in one ear, or dizziness that feels like the room is spinning?

  8. 08

    Altered taste or increased sensitivity to sound

    What to say to the patient

    Does food taste different, or do sounds seem unusually loud?

  9. 09

    Dry or watery eyes; foreign body sensation

    What to say to the patient

    Do your eyes feel dry or gritty, or do they keep watering?

  10. 10

    Drooling or food collecting in one cheek

    What to say to the patient

    Does water leak out of your mouth when you drink, or does food collect in one cheek?

  11. 11

    Recent cold, shingles, or tick bite and rash

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had a cold or shingles, or been bitten by a tick after hiking?

  12. 12

    Previous episodes; involvement of the opposite side or both sides

    What to say to the patient

    Has this happened before? Has the other side of your face been affected, or both sides?

  13. 13

    Lump in front of or below the ear

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt a lump in front of your ear or under your jaw?

  14. 14

    Head trauma; ear or parotid surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently injured your head or had surgery on your ear or face?

  15. 15

    Diabetes, hypertension, or pregnancy

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have diabetes or high blood pressure?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Compare both sides while asking the patient to wrinkle the forehead, close the eyes tightly, puff out the cheeks, and smile showing the teeth

    • Facial movements: comparison of the upper and lower face ↗
  2. 02

    Assess eyelid closure and corneal exposure

    • Lagophthalmos · eyelid closure and corneal exposure ↗
  3. 03

    Inspect the pinna, ear canal, and tympanic membrane for vesicles or otitis media

    • Adult otoscopy: safe approach and tympanic membrane landmarks ↗
  4. 04

    Palpate the parotid gland and neck

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗
    • Palpate the parotid gland Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Assess other cranial nerves, limb strength and sensation, speech articulation, and cerebellar function

    • Pronator drift test ↗
    • Extraocular movements: six diagnostic positions of gaze ↗
    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Whispered voice test — one ear at a time ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
