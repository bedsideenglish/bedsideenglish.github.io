Prioritize the patient's condition. Following a fire, voice change, soot in the mouth, or shortness of breath may indicate inhalation injury that can rapidly obstruct the airway. Proactive assessment of airway patency and immediate emergency assessment are essential. Suspected major neurovascular injury, such as active bleeding, absent distal pulses, or reduced distal sensation, requires prompt surgical assessment.

Wounds and burns: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Mechanism of injury: object or agent involved and how it happened

    What to say to the patient

    How did the injury happen? What cut or burned you?

  2. 02

    Time of injury and elapsed time

    What to say to the patient

    What time did the injury happen? How many hours ago was that?

  3. 03

    Wound location, size, and depth

    What to say to the patient

    Where were you injured? About how large and deep is the wound?

  4. 04

    Contamination with soil, rusty objects, or foreign material; bite wound

    What to say to the patient

    Was there dirt, rust, or other material in the wound? Was it an animal or human bite?

  5. 05

    Amount of bleeding, whether it has stopped, and spurting bleeding

    What to say to the patient

    Was there a lot of bleeding? Did it stop with pressure, or was it spurting?

  6. 06

    Distal tingling or reduced sensation and ability to flex and extend

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have tingling or numbness beyond the wound? Can you bend and straighten your fingers?

  7. 07

    Burn cause (hot water, flame, oil, chemicals, or electricity), contact duration, and first aid, including cooling with water

    What to say to the patient

    What caused the burn? How long was the contact, and did you cool it with water right away?

  8. 08

    Fire in an enclosed space, hoarseness, breathlessness, and soot-stained sputum

    What to say to the patient

    Was the fire in an enclosed space? Has your voice changed, have you been short of breath, or have you coughed up black phlegm?

  9. 09

    Pain severity, including any areas without pain

    What to say to the patient

    How painful is it? Are there any areas where you can't feel anything?

  10. 10

    Duration of the nonhealing wound, change in size, and treatment received

    What to say to the patient

    How long has the wound been slow to heal? Is it getting larger? What treatment have you had?

  11. 11

    Spreading erythema, pus, foul odor, and fever

    What to say to the patient

    Is redness spreading around the wound? Is there pus or an unusual smell? Any fever?

  12. 12

    Prolonged bed rest or sitting, repositioning, and reduced sensation

    What to say to the patient

    Do you spend long periods in bed or in a wheelchair? Do you change position regularly?

  13. 13

    Diabetes, peripheral vascular disease such as calf pain with walking, and smoking

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have diabetes? Do your calves hurt when you walk, or do you smoke?

  14. 14

    Timing of the last tetanus vaccination

    What to say to the patient

    When was your last tetanus shot?

  15. 15

    Anticoagulant or corticosteroid use, immunosuppression, and nutritional status

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take blood thinners or steroids? Have you been eating well?

  16. 16

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking whether the wound is related to violence or self-harm

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone about this. Did someone hit you or hurt you on purpose?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Check blood pressure, pulse, and temperature for hemorrhagic or burn shock and infection

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Assess wound size, depth, contamination, foreign material, and exposed structures such as tendon or bone

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Check distal pulses, capillary refill, sensation, and tendon function beyond the wound

    • Capillary refill time at the fingertip pad ↗
    • Light touch sensation: comparison of both sides ↗
    • Radial, brachial, and carotid pulse locations ↗
    • Lower limb peripheral pulses: femoral, popliteal, and pedal ↗
    • Distal tendon motor function testing Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Assess burn depth and extent using the 1% hand method or rule of nines

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Technique card pending
  5. 05

    Check for soot on the face or in the mouth, singed nasal hairs, and voice changes

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
    • Work of breathing, accessory muscle use, and tripod positioning ↗
  6. 06

    Assess surrounding redness, warmth, fluctuance, and crepitus

    • Technique card pending
  7. 07

    For a chronic wound, assess whether a probe reaches bone and check foot sensation and pulses

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Lower limb peripheral pulses: femoral, popliteal, and pedal ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
