Prioritize the patient's condition. Palpitations with chest pain, syncope, shortness of breath, or low blood pressure may indicate a serious arrhythmia or acute coronary disease and require immediate emergency assessment and an ECG.

Palpitations: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Character of the palpitations: rapid, skipped beats, or irregular

    What to say to the patient

    What does your heartbeat feel like? Does it race or seem to skip a beat?

  2. 02

    Ask the patient to tap out the rhythm of the palpitations to assess regularity

    What to say to the patient

    Could you tap out what your heartbeat feels like when this happens?

  3. 03

    Sudden or gradual onset and termination

    What to say to the patient

    Does the racing heartbeat start and stop suddenly, or does it build up and settle down gradually?

  4. 04

    First onset, duration of each episode, and frequency

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? How long does each episode last, and how often does it happen?

  5. 05

    Triggers: exercise, caffeine, alcohol, stress, and changes in position

    What to say to the patient

    Does it happen after coffee, when you're nervous, or when you exercise?

  6. 06

    What stops the palpitations: breath-holding, drinking cold water, or resting

    What to say to the patient

    What makes it stop? Does holding your breath stop it?

  7. 07

    Syncope or presyncope, and occurrence during exertion

    What to say to the patient

    With the palpitations, have you ever passed out or felt as though you might?

  8. 08

    Chest pain and shortness of breath

    What to say to the patient

    When your heart races, do you have chest pain or feel short of breath?

  9. 09

    Heat intolerance, sweating, weight loss, and hand tremor

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been feeling unusually hot, sweating more, or losing weight lately?

  10. 10

    Fatigue, dizziness, and bleeding, including heavy periods or black stools

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get tired or dizzy easily? Have you had heavy periods or black stools?

  11. 11

    Headache, sweating, or pallor with palpitations; sweating or hand tremor when fasting

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get a headache or break into a sweat when your heart races? Does it happen when you're hungry?

  12. 12

    Intense anxiety, fear of dying, tingling in the hands or feet, and hyperventilation

    What to say to the patient

    When your heart races, do you feel very scared or anxious?

  13. 13

    Recent cold or flu-like illness, and fever

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a cold or a flu-like illness recently?

  14. 14

    Coffee and energy drink intake, and alcohol consumption

    What to say to the patient

    How much coffee or how many energy drinks do you have in a day? What about alcohol?

  15. 15

    Use of medications that may cause palpitations, including cold medications, bronchodilators, thyroid hormone, and weight-loss medications

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently taken any medications, such as cold medicine, weight-loss pills, or thyroid medication?

  16. 16

    Preexisting heart disease, hypertension, and previous ECG abnormalities

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever been told you have a heart condition or an abnormal ECG?

  17. 17

    Family history of sudden death at a young age, arrhythmia, or cardiomyopathy

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family died suddenly at a young age or had an irregular heartbeat?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Palpate the pulse for 30 seconds to 1 minute to assess rate and regularity

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
  2. 02

    Measure blood pressure, temperature, and oxygen saturation

    • Orthostatic blood pressure and pulse measurement ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Pulse oximetry: placement, signal quality, and limitations ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Inspect and palpate the thyroid, and assess for hand tremor and eye signs

    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
    • Elicit and examine hand tremor, and assess eye signs Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Check for conjunctival pallor

    • Inspection of the lower palpebral conjunctiva and eyes for pallor or jaundice ↗
  5. 05

    Auscultate the heart for murmurs and an irregular rhythm

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Precordial palpation: apical impulse, heaves, and thrills ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
