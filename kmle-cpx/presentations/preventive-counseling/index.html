Preventive and health counseling: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Reason for seeking counseling and specific goals, such as desired weight loss or preferred contraceptive method

    What to say to the patient

    What brought you in for counseling? What would you like to achieve?

  2. 02

    Current symptoms, if any; assess those symptoms first

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have any symptoms that are bothering or worrying you right now?

  3. 03

    Timing and results of the last health checkup and cancer screening: upper endoscopy, colonoscopy, mammography, and cervical cytology

    What to say to the patient

    When was your last health checkup, endoscopy, or breast or cervical screening test? What were the results?

  4. 04

    Vaccination history: influenza, pneumococcal, shingles, hepatitis B, tetanus, and HPV vaccines

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had flu, pneumonia, or shingles vaccines?

  5. 05

    Family history of cancer (such as colorectal or breast cancer), early cardiovascular disease, or diabetes, and age at diagnosis

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have cancer, heart disease, or diabetes? How old were they when they were diagnosed?

  6. 06

    Daily meal pattern, snacks and drinks, and physical activity

    What to say to the patient

    What do you eat on a typical day? What about snacks and drinks? How much do you exercise each week?

  7. 07

    Weight trajectory, previous weight-loss attempts and methods, and weight regain

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    When did you start gaining weight? Have you tried to lose weight before? What did you try?

  8. 08

    Secondary causes of weight gain: cold intolerance or constipation, facial and central weight gain with easy bruising, and medications that cause weight gain

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been more sensitive to cold, or gained weight mainly in your face and abdomen? Are you taking any new medications?

  9. 09

    Snoring or witnessed apneas, knee pain, thirst, and increased urination

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Do you snore heavily, or has anyone noticed that you stop breathing in your sleep? Do your knees hurt?

  10. 10

    Current and past contraceptive methods and problems with them, future pregnancy plans, and ability to take medication daily

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    What are you using for birth control now? Do you plan to become pregnant in the future? Would taking a pill every day work for you?

  11. 11

    Check for estrogen contraindications: smoking at age 35 or older, migraine with aura, history of thrombosis, breast cancer, or uncontrolled hypertension

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: female patients

    What to say to the patient

    Do you smoke? Do you get migraines preceded by flashing lights, or have you ever had a blood clot in a leg or lung?

  12. 12

    Last menstrual period and severity of menopausal symptoms: hot flashes, night sweats, vaginal dryness, and changes in sleep or mood

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: women age 45 or older

    What to say to the patient

    When was your last period? Have hot flashes or night sweats been interfering with your daily life?

  13. 13

    Postmenopausal or abnormal vaginal bleeding

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: female patients

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had any vaginal bleeding since your periods stopped?

  14. 14

    Exertional chest pain, syncope, severe shortness of breath, and palpitations

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had chest pain, felt like you might pass out, or felt your heart racing during exercise?

  15. 15

    Family history of sudden death or heart disease before age 50

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family died suddenly at a young age or had heart disease when they were young?

  16. 16

    Previous concussion, serious injury, and exercise-induced asthma symptoms

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever lost consciousness after a head injury? Do you wheeze when you exercise?

  17. 17

    For prescription renewals: adherence, adverse effects, home measurements, and symptoms of hypoglycemia

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Are you able to take your medication regularly? Have you had any problems with it? What have your blood sugar readings been at home?

  18. 18

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about sexual activity and STI prevention

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone these questions, so please feel free to answer openly. If you're sexually active, do you use condoms?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
