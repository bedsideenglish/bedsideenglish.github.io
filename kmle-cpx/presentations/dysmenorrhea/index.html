Prioritize the patient's condition. Sudden severe unilateral lower abdominal pain with dizziness or syncope requires immediate emergency clinical assessment for gynecologic emergencies such as a ruptured ectopic pregnancy or ovarian torsion.

Dysmenorrhea: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset of dysmenorrhea: soon after menarche or newly developing later

    What to say to the patient

    When did your menstrual cramps start? Did they begin with your first periods, or did they start a few years later?

  2. 02

    Timing of pain: just before menstruation, the first 1–3 days, throughout the period, or unrelated to menstruation

    What to say to the patient

    Does the pain start before your period, or is it just during the first day or two? Do you have pain at other times, too?

  3. 03

    Pain quality and location, and radiation to the lower back or thighs

    What to say to the patient

    Where does it hurt, and what does it feel like? Is it cramping? Does it spread to your lower back or thighs?

  4. 04

    Pain severity and impact on daily life, including absence from school or work

    What to say to the patient

    On a scale of 0 to 10, how bad is the pain? Have you had to miss school or work because of it?

  5. 05

    Whether pain is progressively worsening each year

    What to say to the patient

    Has the pain been getting progressively worse?

  6. 06

    How analgesics or oral contraceptives are taken and their effectiveness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you tried pain medication? When did you start it, how did you take it, and did it help?

  7. 07

    Associated nausea, diarrhea, headache, or other symptoms

    What to say to the patient

    Do you also feel nauseated, have diarrhea, or get a headache with the cramps?

  8. 08

    Increased menstrual flow, clots, and intermenstrual bleeding

    What to say to the patient

    Are your periods heavy, or do you pass clots? Do you have spotting between periods?

  9. 09

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the question before asking about pain with intercourse

    What to say to the patient

    This is a sensitive question, but it's relevant to your care. Do you have pain deep inside during sex?

  10. 10

    Pain with defecation or urination during menstruation

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt to have a bowel movement or urinate during your period?

  11. 11

    Attempts to conceive and possible infertility

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been trying to become pregnant? Has it been difficult?

  12. 12

    Fever and abnormal vaginal discharge

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or more foul-smelling discharge?

  13. 13

    Sudden severe unilateral pain, dizziness or syncope, and a late period

    What to say to the patient

    Did you suddenly have severe pain on one side, or feel dizzy or like you might pass out this time? Is your period late?

  14. 14

    Age at menarche and menstrual cycle regularity

    What to say to the patient

    How old were you when you had your first period? Are your cycles usually regular?

  15. 15

    New partner and history of sexually transmitted infections

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had sex with a new partner recently or been treated for a sexually transmitted infection?

  16. 16

    Contraceptive method and intrauterine device use

    What to say to the patient

    What do you use for birth control? Do you have an IUD?

  17. 17

    Endometriosis or severe dysmenorrhea in the patient's mother or sisters

    What to say to the patient

    Does your mother or any of your sisters have severe period pain or endometriosis?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
