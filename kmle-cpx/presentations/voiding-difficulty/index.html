Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent assessment for sudden complete inability to urinate, or new saddle sensory loss or bilateral leg weakness. Do not delay assessment to complete a rectal examination or provocative tests.

Voiding difficulty and urinary incontinence: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Whether the main concern is difficulty voiding or urinary leakage

    What to say to the patient

    Is your main concern difficulty passing urine, or urine leaking?

  2. 02

    Onset and course: sudden or gradual

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Did it come on suddenly, or get worse gradually?

  3. 03

    Hesitancy, weak or interrupted stream, and straining to urinate

    What to say to the patient

    Does it take a while for urine to start, or is the stream weak and stopping and starting? Do you have to strain?

  4. 04

    Sense of incomplete emptying and postvoid dribbling

    What to say to the patient

    Does it feel as though your bladder hasn't emptied completely, or do you dribble after you finish urinating?

  5. 05

    Complete inability to pass urine, and painful lower abdominal distention

    What to say to the patient

    Are you unable to pass any urine, or is your lower abdomen swollen and painful?

  6. 06

    Number of daytime voids and nighttime voids

    What to say to the patient

    About how many times do you urinate during the day? How many times do you wake up at night to go?

  7. 07

    Sudden, difficult-to-defer urinary urgency and leakage before reaching the toilet

    What to say to the patient

    Do you suddenly need to urinate so badly that it's hard to hold it? Do you ever leak before you reach the bathroom?

  8. 08

    Leakage with coughing, sneezing, laughing, or heavy lifting

    What to say to the patient

    Do you leak urine when you cough, laugh, or lift something heavy?

  9. 09

    Amount of leakage, pad use, and impact on daily activities

    What to say to the patient

    How much urine leaks? How many pads do you use a day? How much does it affect your daily life?

  10. 10

    Dysuria, hematuria, and fever

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt when you urinate, or have you noticed blood in your urine? Any fever?

  11. 11

    Back pain, leg weakness or tingling, reduced perianal sensation, and bowel control problems

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have back pain or weakness in your legs? Have you noticed numbness around your anus or trouble controlling your bowels?

  12. 12

    Constipation

    What to say to the patient

    Have your bowel movements been regular? Any constipation?

  13. 13

    Fluid, caffeine, and alcohol intake, including fluids before bedtime

    What to say to the patient

    How much water, coffee, or alcohol do you drink each day? Do you drink much before bed?

  14. 14

    Recent use of cold or nasal medications, antidepressants, or diuretics

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently started any medications, such as cold medicine, medication for a runny nose, or a diuretic?

  15. 15

    History of diabetes, stroke, Parkinson disease, or spinal surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have diabetes, a history of stroke, or Parkinson disease? Have you ever had back surgery?

  16. 16

    History of perineal trauma, urethral procedures, or urethritis

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: male patients

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a hard blow to the groin or a procedure involving your urethra?

  17. 17

    Obstetric history, including number of vaginal deliveries, and pelvic surgery

    Applies to: female patients

    What to say to the patient

    How many vaginal deliveries have you had? Have you had surgery on your uterus or pelvis?

  18. 18

    Sensation of something protruding from the vagina

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: female patients

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel pressure or heaviness, or as though something is coming down or out of your vagina?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Palpate and percuss the lower abdomen for bladder distention suggesting urinary retention

    • Light and deep abdominal palpation ↗
    • Suprapubic bladder assessment: gentle palpation and percussion ↗
  2. 02

    In male patients, assess prostate size, consistency, nodules, and tenderness by digital rectal examination

    • Digital rectal examination: consent, preparation, and left lateral positioning ↗
    • Digital rectal examination: rectal wall and prostate ↗
  3. 03

    Assess lower limb strength, sensation, and reflexes, perineal sensation, and anal sphincter tone

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Lower limb strength testing (nerve roots L2–S1) ↗
    • Patellar reflex (L2–L4, primarily L4) ↗
    • Straight leg raise, including the Bragard maneuver ↗
    • Saddle sensation: selective light touch assessment of the perineum ↗
    • Anal sphincter tone: resting resistance and voluntary squeeze ↗
    • Light touch sensation: comparison of both sides ↗
    • Achilles reflex (primarily S1) ↗
  4. 04

    In female patients, perform a pelvic examination to assess for prolapse, atrophy, and leakage with coughing

    Select as clinically indicated

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
