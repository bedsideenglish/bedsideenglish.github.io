Prioritize the patient's condition. Severe thirst with vomiting, abdominal pain, deep and rapid Kussmaul breathing, or decreased consciousness such as stupor suggests an acute metabolic emergency, including diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). Immediate emergency assessment and a blood glucose check are needed.

Polyuria and polydipsia: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Large urine volume per void versus frequent small voids

    What to say to the patient

    Do you pass a large amount each time, or go often but pass only a little?

  2. 02

    Time and pattern of onset: sudden or gradual

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Did it begin suddenly one day, or increase gradually?

  3. 03

    Daily fluid amount and types of drinks

    What to say to the patient

    How much do you drink in a day? Is it mostly water, or sugary drinks and coffee?

  4. 04

    Number of nighttime voids

    What to say to the patient

    Do you wake up at night to urinate? About how many times?

  5. 05

    Thirst and drinking during the night, especially a preference for cold water

    What to say to the patient

    Do you wake up thirsty and need water during the night? Do you particularly crave cold water?

  6. 06

    Weight loss and appetite changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you lost weight recently? Is it happening even though you're eating more?

  7. 07

    Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid breathing, and lethargy or confusion

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain? Any shortness of breath or feeling confused?

  8. 08

    Fatigue and blurred vision

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been very tired, or has your vision been blurry?

  9. 09

    Recurrent skin or vaginal infections and poorly healing wounds

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had frequent skin or vaginal infections, or wounds that take a long time to heal?

  10. 10

    Dysuria, urgency, and difficulty voiding

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt when you urinate, do you have sudden urgency, or is it hard to empty your bladder?

  11. 11

    Head trauma, brain surgery, headache, and visual changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you recently had a head injury or brain surgery? Any headaches or a sense that your field of vision is narrowing?

  12. 12

    Constipation, bone pain, and urinary stones

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had constipation, bone pain, or kidney stones?

  13. 13

    Muscle weakness and cramps

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had weakness or frequent muscle cramps in your arms or legs?

  14. 14

    Use of lithium, diuretics, corticosteroids, or SGLT2 inhibitors

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take mental health medication such as lithium, a diuretic, steroids, or diabetes medication?

  15. 15

    Coffee, energy drinks, and alcohol intake

    What to say to the patient

    How much coffee, energy drinks, or alcohol do you drink?

  16. 16

    Mental health treatment history and stress

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever received treatment for emotional or mental health difficulties? How has your stress level been lately?

  17. 17

    Family history of diabetes, obesity, and history of gestational diabetes

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have diabetes? Have you ever been told your blood sugar was high?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
