Prioritize the patient's condition. Purpura, widespread blisters or peeling, or mucosal lesions with shortness of breath, altered consciousness, or worsening general condition are warning signs requiring urgent assessment. Do not rub the skin or deliberately cause pain to elicit findings.

Skin rash: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    When and where the rash began, and how it spread

    What to say to the patient

    When did it first appear? Where did it start, and how has it spread?

  2. 02

    Location and distribution: unilateral band, skin folds, palms or soles, or exposed areas

    What to say to the patient

    Where is the rash now? Is it in a band on just one side, or spread over your body?

  3. 03

    Lesion appearance: red patches, raised areas, blisters, scale, or crusts

    What to say to the patient

    What does it look like? Is it red and raised, blistered, or scaly?

  4. 04

    Itching, pain, burning, and pain preceding the rash

    What to say to the patient

    Does it itch, hurt, or burn? Did the area sting or hurt before the rash appeared?

  5. 05

    Individual lesions disappearing within hours and then recurring

    What to say to the patient

    Does each spot disappear within a few hours and then show up somewhere else?

  6. 06

    Previous similar symptoms and treatment received

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had anything like this before? What treatment did you have then?

  7. 07

    Fever, body aches, and generalized weakness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or felt achy and unwell?

  8. 08

    Blisters, ulcers, or redness involving the oral, ocular, or genital mucosa

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had mouth ulcers, red or painful eyes, or any genital sores?

  9. 09

    Painful skin peeling and nonblanching purple lesions

    What to say to the patient

    Has your skin been painful or peeling, or have you had purple spots that don't fade when pressed?

  10. 10

    Swelling of the lips, tongue, or face; shortness of breath, throat tightness, or dizziness

    What to say to the patient

    Have your lips or tongue been swollen, or have you had shortness of breath or a tight feeling in your throat?

  11. 11

    Recently started medications, including antibiotics, gout medications, antiseizure medications, and analgesics

    What to say to the patient

    Did you start any new medications in the weeks before the rash appeared?

  12. 12

    New foods, cosmetics or detergents, metal jewelry, and plant contact

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had any new foods or used new cosmetics or detergent? What about metal jewelry or contact with plants?

  13. 13

    Similar symptoms in family members or close contacts

    What to say to the patient

    Is anyone you live with or spend time with itching like this, too?

  14. 14

    Tick or insect bites, hiking, travel, exercise, and use of shared facilities

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been hiking or had insect bites recently? Any travel or use of shared showers?

  15. 15

    History of atopic dermatitis, asthma, or allergic rhinitis

    What to say to the patient

    Did you have eczema as a child, or do you have asthma or hay fever?

  16. 16

    Joint pain and nail changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had joint pain or changes in your fingernails or toenails?

  17. 17

    Topical treatments tried, including corticosteroids or antifungals, and response

    What to say to the patient

    Have you tried any creams or medications? Did they help or make it worse?

  18. 18

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about recent sexual activity and a history of genital ulcers

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask this as part of your care. Have you recently had a new sexual partner or any genital sores?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
