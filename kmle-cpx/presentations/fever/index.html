Prioritize the patient's condition. With fever and decreased consciousness, shortness of breath, circulatory instability, or rapidly worsening overall condition, prioritize urgent assessment and calling for help over a detailed examination. The absence of clear focal findings does not rule out serious infection.

Fever: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Fever onset and duration

    What to say to the patient

    When did the fever start? Has it been there continuously?

  2. 02

    Maximum temperature, fever pattern (continuous, intermittent, or periodic), and chills

    What to say to the patient

    What was the highest temperature you measured? Does the fever come and go? Have you had shaking chills?

  3. 03

    Use of antipyretics and response

    What to say to the patient

    Have you taken anything for the fever? Does it bring the temperature down?

  4. 04

    Cough, sputum, sore throat, and shortness of breath

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a cough, any phlegm, or a sore throat? Any shortness of breath?

  5. 05

    Dysuria, urinary frequency, and flank pain

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt when you urinate, or have you had pain in your side?

  6. 06

    Abdominal pain, diarrhea, and vomiting

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had abdominal pain, diarrhea, or vomiting?

  7. 07

    Severe headache, neck stiffness, and altered consciousness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a severe headache or a stiff neck?

  8. 08

    Rash, a black scab after an insect bite, and joint pain

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a rash, or a black scab where you were bitten by an insect? Any joint pain?

  9. 09

    Night sweats and weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had drenching night sweats or lost weight?

  10. 10

    Lumps in the neck, armpits, or groin

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt any lumps in your neck or armpits?

  11. 11

    Travel abroad, outdoor activities such as hiking or farm work, and animal contact

    What to say to the patient

    Have you traveled abroad recently, or done outdoor activities such as hiking or farm work?

  12. 12

    Contacts with similar symptoms

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone around you been ill with similar symptoms?

  13. 13

    Chemotherapy, corticosteroid use, and immunosuppressive medications

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had chemotherapy recently, or do you take any medications that suppress your immune system?

  14. 14

    Prosthetic heart valve or heart disease, and recent dental treatment or procedures

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had heart valve surgery, or any recent dental treatment or procedures?

  15. 15

    Recently started medications

    What to say to the patient

    Did you start any new medications before the fever began?

  16. 16

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about new sexual contacts and injection drug use

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone these questions, so please feel free to answer openly. Have you had sex with a new partner recently?

  17. 17

    In children: reduced activity, feeding and urine output, and febrile seizures

    Applies to: children

    What to say to the patient

    Has your child been unusually sleepy or less active, or not feeding well? Have there been any seizures?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
