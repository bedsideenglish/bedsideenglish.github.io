Prioritize the patient's condition. Joint symptoms with fever, or a rapidly red and swollen single joint with pain so severe that movement is not possible, raise concern for septic arthritis, which can cause permanent joint damage. Arrange urgent clinical assessment without delay.

Joint pain and swelling: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Location and number of painful joints, and symmetry

    What to say to the patient

    Which joints hurt? Is it one joint or several? Are both sides affected?

  2. 02

    Pattern of onset: sudden or gradual, and duration

    What to say to the patient

    When did the pain start? Did it start suddenly or get worse gradually?

  3. 03

    Pain quality and severity

    What to say to the patient

    What does the pain feel like? If the worst it's been was a 10, what number would you give it now?

  4. 04

    Joint swelling, redness, and warmth

    What to say to the patient

    Has the joint been swollen, red, or warm?

  5. 05

    Morning stiffness and its duration

    What to say to the patient

    Are your joints stiff when you wake up? How long does it take for them to loosen up?

  6. 06

    Worsening with movement and improvement with rest

    What to say to the patient

    Does it hurt more with use and improve with rest, or is it stiffer after resting?

  7. 07

    Limitations in walking, stairs, or hand use, and ability to bear weight

    What to say to the patient

    Is walking, climbing stairs, or fastening buttons difficult? Can you put weight on that leg?

  8. 08

    Fever and chills

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a fever or felt chilled?

  9. 09

    Trauma, twisting injury, a popping sound, or knee locking or giving way

    What to say to the patient

    Did you injure or twist it? Was there a popping sound, or does your knee feel as though it catches?

  10. 10

    Previous similar joint attacks

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had sudden joint swelling and pain like this before?

  11. 11

    Fatigue, low-grade fever, and weight loss

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been very tired, had a low-grade fever, or lost weight lately?

  12. 12

    Rash such as a facial eruption or psoriasis, oral ulcers, hair loss, red eyes, and dry mouth or eyes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a rash on your face or body, mouth ulcers, hair loss, or red eyes?

  13. 13

    Morning-predominant back pain, heel pain, and swelling of an entire finger or toe

    What to say to the patient

    Is your back especially painful in the morning, or do your heels hurt? Have any fingers become swollen all the way along, like a sausage?

  14. 14

    Recent diarrhea, dysuria or urethral discharge, and sore throat

    What to say to the patient

    In the past few weeks, have you had diarrhea, pain with urination, or a sore throat?

  15. 15

    Alcohol, large amounts of meat or seafood, and diuretic use

    What to say to the patient

    Before the pain started, had you had alcohol or eaten a lot of meat or seafood? Do you take a diuretic?

  16. 16

    Immunosuppressants or corticosteroids, joint injections or surgery, and a prosthetic joint

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take immunosuppressive medication or steroids? Have you had injections or surgery in that joint?

  17. 17

    Acknowledge the sensitive nature of the questions before asking about recent sexual activity and new partners

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone this as part of their care. Have you had sex with a new partner recently?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
