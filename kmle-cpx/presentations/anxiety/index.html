Prioritize the patient's condition. With exertional chest pain, syncope, persistent arrhythmia, or severe breathlessness, do not assume anxiety; arrange immediate cardiovascular emergency assessment. If signs of self-harm risk are identified, secure support and address safety.

Anxiety: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Focus of anxiety: multiple daily concerns, particular situations, or health

    What to say to the patient

    What are you mainly anxious about? Do you worry about many things, or particular situations?

  2. 02

    Onset and duration, including whether symptoms have persisted for at least 6 months

    What to say to the patient

    When did this start? Do you have more anxious days than days without anxiety?

  3. 03

    Persistent anxiety versus sudden attacks that peak within minutes

    What to say to the patient

    Is the anxiety there throughout the day, or does it suddenly surge and then settle down?

  4. 04

    Palpitations, breathlessness, chest tightness, tremor, dizziness, and fear of dying

    What to say to the patient

    When that happens, does your heart race, do you feel you can't breathe, do your hands shake, or do you feel as though you might die?

  5. 05

    Anticipatory anxiety about recurrence and avoidance of places or situations

    What to say to the patient

    Do you worry about it happening again, or avoid any places or situations because of it?

  6. 06

    Anxiety when speaking in public or being evaluated

    What to say to the patient

    Are you especially anxious when presenting in front of people or when others are watching you?

  7. 07

    Muscle tension, restlessness, easy fatigability, poor concentration, and difficulty falling asleep

    What to say to the patient

    Do you often feel tense or restless? Is it hard to concentrate or fall asleep?

  8. 08

    Impact on daily life, work or school, and relationships

    What to say to the patient

    Has anxiety been affecting your work or daily life?

  9. 09

    Recent stress related to work, family, finances, or health

    What to say to the patient

    Has anything in particular been stressful lately?

  10. 10

    Nightmares or flashbacks after trauma, and repetitive thoughts or behaviors

    What to say to the patient

    Have you experienced a frightening or traumatic event? Do you find yourself repeating thoughts or actions?

  11. 11

    Low mood and thoughts of wanting to die

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt very down, or so overwhelmed that you've thought about wanting to die?

  12. 12

    Weight loss, heat intolerance, sweating, and hand tremor

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been losing weight, feeling unusually hot, or sweating more?

  13. 13

    Exertional chest pain or syncope

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had chest pain or passed out while exercising or exerting yourself?

  14. 14

    Coffee and energy drink intake

    What to say to the patient

    How much coffee or how many energy drinks do you have each day?

  15. 15

    Alcohol use, sedative or sleeping pill use and recent discontinuation, and other substances

    What to say to the patient

    How much alcohol do you drink? Have you recently stopped a sedative or sleeping pill?

  16. 16

    Coping strategies tried and previous treatment

    What to say to the patient

    What do you do to cope when you're anxious? Have you had treatment before?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Check pulse, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and temperature

    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
    • Respiratory rate, rhythm, and effort ↗
    • Temperature measurement Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Observe appearance, behavior, speech, and affect

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Mental status examination (MSE) and structured screening tools such as the GAD-7 Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Palpate the thyroid and assess for hand tremor and eye signs

    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗
    • Assess hand tremor and proptosis Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Auscultate the heart and lungs

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Cardiac auscultation areas (aortic, pulmonic, tricuspid, mitral) ↗
    • Posterior lung auscultation sequence ↗
  5. 05

    Assess symptom severity with a screening tool such as the GAD-7

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
