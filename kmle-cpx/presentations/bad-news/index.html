Prioritize the patient's condition. When the patient is deeply distressed, assess their response and the support they need. If fainting, chest pain, shortness of breath, or a specific risk of self-harm emerges, prioritize the relevant safety assessment.

Breaking bad news: interview questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Sit at eye level in a quiet, private space and ask whether the patient wants someone with them

    What to say to the patient

    Please have a seat. Before we go over the results, is there anyone in your family you'd like to have here with you?

  2. 02

    Current physical and emotional state, and the experience of waiting for the results

    What to say to the patient

    How have you been feeling physically? Has waiting for the results been worrying you?

  3. 03

    The patient's understanding of why testing was done and the symptoms at that time

    What to say to the patient

    Could you tell me, in your own words, why you had these tests?

  4. 04

    Explanations already received from other clinicians

    What to say to the patient

    What have the other doctors told you about the tests so far?

  5. 05

    Expectations about the results and the patient's main concerns

    What to say to the patient

    What are you expecting the results to show? What worries you the most?

  6. 06

    Changes in symptoms since testing, such as weight loss or pain, and the patient's thoughts about them

    What to say to the patient

    How have your symptoms been since the test? Has anything gotten worse?

  7. 07

    Preferred level of detail about the results: all details, including numbers, or an overview

    What to say to the patient

    Would you like me to go through all the details, or would you prefer a brief overview of the main findings first?

  8. 08

    Whom the patient wants to share the results with, and whether they would like the clinician to explain the results to family

    What to say to the patient

    How would you like to share the results with your family? Would you like me to explain them to your family directly?

  9. 09

    Whether the patient is ready to hear the results now

    What to say to the patient

    Is now an okay time for me to go over the results with you?

  10. 10

    The patient's or family's previous experience of serious illness and memories of that experience

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family been treated for a serious illness? What was that like for you?

  11. 11

    Responsibilities for supporting and caring for family, employment, and financial circumstances

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have a lot of responsibilities at home or at work right now? Is there anyone in your family you care for?

  12. 12

    People the patient can turn to for support

    What to say to the patient

    Is there someone who can support you when things are hard?

  13. 13

    Usual ways of coping with stress and mental health history, including depression or anxiety

    What to say to the patient

    How do you usually cope when things are difficult? Have you ever received treatment for depression or anxiety?

  14. 14

    What matters most to the patient, upcoming plans such as family events or work, and religious beliefs

    What to say to the patient

    What matters most to you right now? Are there any important events or plans coming up?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?
