Prioritize the patient's condition. If recent self-harm, ingestion of medication, altered consciousness, or immediate risk of self-harm is suspected, prioritize physical and psychosocial safety assessment and access to help. Assess the individual circumstances; do not decide on admission or discharge solely from physical examination findings or a risk score.

Suicide risk assessment: interview questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Nature of thoughts about death, from wishing to disappear to thoughts of ending one's life; onset, frequency, duration, and whether the thoughts are present now

    What to say to the patient

    Thank you for telling me. When did those thoughts start? How often do they happen, and how long do they last? Are you having them now?

  2. 02

    Specific plan (method, timing, and location), intent to act, and preparatory behavior such as a note, putting affairs in order, saying goodbye, or rehearsing

    What to say to the patient

    Have you thought specifically about how you would do it? Have you made any preparations?

  3. 03

    Access to the contemplated means, including stockpiled medications, charcoal briquettes, cords, pesticides, or high places

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have any medications or other items at home that you could use in the way you've been thinking about?

  4. 04

    Previous suicide attempts and self-harm: number, method, most recent occurrence, and treatment received

    What to say to the patient

    Have you ever tried to hurt yourself before? What happened at that time?

  5. 05

    Precipitating events, including loss, a breakup, financial problems, work or school difficulties, health concerns, or legal issues

    What to say to the patient

    Was there anything that led up to things becoming this difficult?

  6. 06

    Depressive symptoms: low mood, reduced interest, sleep or appetite changes, poor concentration, and worthlessness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been feeling down and finding less pleasure in things you used to enjoy? How have your sleep and appetite been?

  7. 07

    Hopelessness, feeling like a burden, and feeling alone

    What to say to the patient

    Do you feel as though things won't get better, or that your family would be better off without you?

  8. 08

    Periods of elevated mood, high energy despite little sleep, and impulsive behavior

    What to say to the patient

    Have there been periods lasting several days when your mood was unusually high and you had lots of energy despite barely sleeping?

  9. 09

    Auditory hallucinations, especially voices telling the patient to harm themselves, and delusions

    What to say to the patient

    Have you heard voices that other people don't hear? Have those voices told you to do anything?

  10. 10

    Alcohol intake, whether drinking intensifies the thoughts, and use of medications or drugs such as sleeping pills

    What to say to the patient

    How much alcohol have you been drinking lately? Do those thoughts get stronger when you drink?

  11. 11

    Agitation, impulsivity, anger, and thoughts of harming others

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt very restless or had sudden bursts of anger lately? Have you had thoughts of harming anyone else?

  12. 12

    Ask permission before discussing experiences of violence or abuse and whether the patient is currently unsafe

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    I ask everyone about this. Is anyone hurting, threatening, or harassing you?

  13. 13

    Chronic pain or a recently diagnosed serious illness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had a long-term illness or recently been diagnosed with a serious condition?

  14. 14

    Suicide of a family member or someone close, and exposure to news of a suicide

    What to say to the patient

    Has anyone in your family or anyone close to you died by suicide?

  15. 15

    Previous and current mental health treatment, medication use, and recent discontinuation without medical advice

    What to say to the patient

    Have you received mental health treatment before? Have you recently stopped taking any of your medications?

  16. 16

    Protective factors, including reasons for living, children or family, faith, and people to ask for help; current living arrangements

    What to say to the patient

    What helps you keep going? Is there someone you can contact when things are hard? Who do you live with now?

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?
