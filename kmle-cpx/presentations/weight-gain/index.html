Prioritize the patient's condition. Rapid weight gain with shortness of breath, orthopnea, or severe generalized edema raises concern for fluid overload and pulmonary congestion from acute heart or kidney failure. Arrange immediate emergency assessment.

Weight gain and obesity: history-taking questions and sample wording

  1. 01

    Amount and duration of weight gain, rate of gain, and adult weight history

    What to say to the patient

    When did the weight gain start, and how much have you gained? Did it happen quickly or gradually?

  2. 02

    Circumstances around the start of weight gain: job change or night shifts, marriage or childbirth, smoking cessation, menopause, injury, or stress

    What to say to the patient

    Did anything change in your life around the time you began gaining weight, such as quitting smoking or changing jobs?

  3. 03

    Daily eating pattern, including skipped meals, late-night meals, restaurant or delivery food, sugary drinks, and alcohol with snacks

    What to say to the patient

    What do you usually eat over the course of a day? How often do you have late-night meals, delivery food, or sugary drinks?

  4. 04

    Physical activity, sedentary time, and sleep duration

    What to say to the patient

    How much physical activity do you get? Do you spend much of the day sitting? How many hours do you sleep?

  5. 05

    Loss of control over eating, eating when depressed or stressed, and guilt after eating

    What to say to the patient

    Are there times when you eat a large amount and find it hard to stop even when you're full? Do you eat more when you're feeling down or stressed?

  6. 06

    Cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, low energy, and voice changes

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been sensitive to the cold, constipated, or noticing dry skin or low energy?

  7. 07

    Predominantly facial and abdominal weight gain with thin limbs, easy bruising, purple striae, proximal thigh weakness, and newly developed hypertension or hyperglycemia

    What to say to the patient

    Has your face become rounder and your abdomen larger while your arms and legs have become thinner? Do you bruise easily or have purple stretch marks on your abdomen?

  8. 08

    Irregular periods, acne, and increased hair growth

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    Are your periods regular? Have you had acne or increased hair growth?

  9. 09

    Headache, narrowed visual fields, and difficult-to-control increased appetite

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had frequent headaches or trouble seeing off to the sides? Has your appetite increased so much that it's hard to control?

  10. 10

    Whether the weight increase reflects fluid retention: leg or facial edema, orthopnea, abdominal distention, or foamy urine

    What to say to the patient

    Does it feel like weight gain, or could it be swelling? Have your legs been swollen, have you been short of breath lying down, or has your urine been foamy?

  11. 11

    Medications associated with weight gain, including corticosteroids, psychiatric medications, insulin or some other diabetes medications, antiseizure medications, and hormones

    What to say to the patient

    Did you start any new medication before the weight gain, such as steroids, mental health medication, or diabetes medication?

  12. 12

    Loud snoring, witnessed apneas, and daytime sleepiness

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been told you snore loudly or stop breathing during sleep? Are you very sleepy during the day?

  13. 13

    Symptoms of obesity-related conditions: exertional chest pain or breathlessness, knee or back pain, thirst or polyuria, and heartburn

    What to say to the patient

    Do you have chest pain or shortness of breath when walking? Any knee pain, unusual thirst, or frequent urination?

  14. 14

    Previous weight-loss attempts: methods, medications or procedures, amount lost, and weight regain

    What to say to the patient

    Have you tried to lose weight before? What did you try, and how did it go?

  15. 15

    Reasons and goals for weight loss, and readiness to make lifestyle changes

    What to say to the patient

    What's your main reason for wanting to lose weight? What goal do you have in mind?

  1. 01

    Measure height, weight, BMI, and waist circumference midway between the lower ribs and the top of the pelvis at the end of expiration

    • Technique card pending
  2. 02

    Measure blood pressure using a cuff appropriate for the arm circumference

    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  3. 03

    Assess for a rounded face, dorsocervical fat pad, wide purple striae, and bruising; assess proximal strength by asking the patient to rise from a chair

    • Lower limb strength testing (nerve roots L2–S1) ↗
    • Inspect for Cushing features: purple striae, a rounded face, and a dorsocervical fat pad Technique card pending
  4. 04

    Palpate the thyroid and assess for dry skin, acanthosis nigricans (dark, thickened skin on the neck or in the armpits), and hirsutism

    • Thyroid palpation from behind while the patient swallows ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
