Prioritize the patient's condition. Prioritize urgent evaluation over a detailed nasal examination if there is persistent heavy bleeding, difficulty breathing, fainting, or circulatory instability. Check for blood running down the back of the throat and assess the patient's overall condition.

Nosebleeds: history questions and examples

  1. 01

    Onset, amount of bleeding, and duration

    What to say to the patient

    When did the nosebleed start? How much does it bleed, and for how long?

  2. 02

    Unilateral or bilateral bleeding; anterior drainage or flow down the back of the throat

    What to say to the patient

    Is the bleeding from one nostril or both? Does it run out the front or down the back of your throat?

  3. 03

    First episode or recurrent episodes; frequency

    What to say to the patient

    Is this the first time, or has it happened often before?

  4. 04

    Home measures to stop bleeding, including duration of nasal pressure and effectiveness

    What to say to the patient

    What have you tried at home to stop the bleeding? How many minutes did you pinch your nose?

  5. 05

    Dizziness, cold sweats, presyncope, or swallowing or vomiting blood

    What to say to the patient

    Have you felt dizzy, sweaty, or as if you might faint? Have you swallowed or vomited any blood?

  6. 06

    Nose picking, forceful nose blowing, dry air, or nasal trauma

    What to say to the patient

    Did it start after picking or forcefully blowing your nose? Did you hit your nose?

  7. 07

    Unilateral nasal obstruction, foul-smelling discharge, facial numbness or pain, or ear fullness

    What to say to the patient

    Is one side of your nose always blocked, or do you have smelly discharge? Have you had facial numbness or a blocked feeling in one ear?

  8. 08

    Easy bruising, bleeding gums, heavy menstrual bleeding, blood in urine or stool, or prolonged bleeding from cuts

    What to say to the patient

    Do you bruise easily or have bleeding gums? Is it hard to stop the bleeding when you get a cut?

  9. 09

    Anticoagulants, antiplatelet agents, NSAIDs, or ginkgo supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Do you take blood thinners, aspirin, anti-inflammatory pain relievers, or ginkgo supplements?

  10. 10

    History and control of hypertension

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been told your blood pressure is high? Are you taking your medication regularly?

  11. 11

    Liver disease; heavy alcohol use

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been told you have liver problems, or do you often drink heavily?

  12. 12

    Fever, weight loss, severe fatigue, or pallor

    What to say to the patient

    Have you had fever, weight loss, or severe tiredness, or has anyone said you look pale?

  13. 13

    Family history of bleeding disorders or recurrent nosebleeds; small red spots on the lips or fingertips

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family get frequent nosebleeds or have trouble stopping bleeding? Do you have small red spots on your lips or fingertips?

  14. 14

    Nasal crusting or blood crusts; cough or blood-streaked sputum; foamy or bloody urine

    Select as clinically indicated

    What to say to the patient

    Do you often get blood crusts in your nose or cough up blood? Has your urine been very foamy or red?

  15. 15

    Nasal spray use, including corticosteroids; intranasal substance use

    What to say to the patient

    Do you use any nasal sprays? Have you used any other substances through your nose?

  16. 16

    Recurrent unilateral nosebleeds with nasal obstruction in an adolescent boy

    Select as clinically indicated

    Applies to: male patients

    What to say to the patient

    Do you get repeated nosebleeds from just one side, with persistent blockage on that side?

Which physical examinations should I consider?

These are the app's examination items for this clinical presentation. Use the available technique cards to review how to perform them. Specific examinations without a card remain in the checklist.

  1. 01

    Check vital signs, including blood pressure and pulse, for instability due to bleeding

    • General inspection from the end of the bed ↗
    • Radial pulse: rate and rhythm ↗
    • Orthostatic blood pressure and pulse measurement ↗
    • Upper-arm blood pressure: standard measurement and comparison of both arms ↗
  2. 02

    Have the patient sit leaning slightly forward and apply continuous pressure to the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes

    Treatment procedure rather than examination · study separately

    • Technique card pending
  3. 03

    Use a nasal speculum to identify the bleeding site, including the anterior septal vascular area, and any lesions

    • Anterior rhinoscopy: nasal vestibule and visible landmarks ↗
  4. 04

    Check for blood running down the back of the throat, suggesting posterior bleeding

    • Oral and pharyngeal inspection and soft palate movement ↗
  5. 05

    Inspect for bruises or petechiae and telangiectasias on the lips and fingertips

    • Rash blanching assessment: petechiae and purpura ↗
    • Inspect the distribution and appearance of bruises and look for telangiectasias on the lips and fingertips Technique card pending
  6. 06

    Palpate cervical lymph nodes

    Select as clinically indicated

    • Cervical lymph node palpation ↗

Common consultation and examination flow

Expand the opening, general history, and pre- and post-examination items

Opening the consultation

  1. 01

    Greet the patient and confirm their name

    What to say to the patient

    Hello. Could you tell me your name?

  2. 02

    Introduce yourself as a medical student and ask for consent to proceed

    What to say to the patient

    My name is [name], and I'm a medical student. Is it okay if I talk with you about your health today?

  3. 03

    Help the patient feel at ease and ask them to tell you right away if they are uncomfortable

    What to say to the patient

    Please let me know right away if you're uncomfortable at any point.

Before taking the history

  1. 01

    Ask an open-ended question about the reason for the visit

    What to say to the patient

    What brings you in today?

  2. 02

    Check for other concerns and agree on the issues to address today

    What to say to the patient

    Is there anything else that's been bothering you?

After taking the history

  1. 01

    Summarize the main symptoms during the interview and confirm your understanding with the patient

    What to say to the patient

    Let me make sure I've understood. You came in because of [main concern], and you've been experiencing [summary]. Is that right?

  2. 02

    Ask about medical history, including diagnoses, hospitalizations, and surgery

    What to say to the patient

    Have you been diagnosed with or treated for any medical conditions? Have you ever had surgery?

  3. 03

    Ask about current prescription medications, over-the-counter medications, and supplements

    What to say to the patient

    Are you taking any medications or supplements?

  4. 04

    Ask about relevant social history, including alcohol, smoking, work, and lifestyle

    What to say to the patient

    Do you drink alcohol or smoke? What do you do for work?

  5. 05

    Ask about relevant family history

    What to say to the patient

    Does anyone in your family have a similar condition?

  6. 06

    Ask about the last menstrual period and the possibility of pregnancy

    Applies to: women of reproductive age

    What to say to the patient

    When did your last period start? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?

Before examination · when indicated

  1. 01

    Explain the area and method of examination and obtain consent

    What to say to the patient

    I'd like to examine you now. Is that okay?

  2. 02

    Clean your hands before the examination and tell the patient you are doing so

    What to say to the patient

    I'm going to clean my hands first.

  3. 03

    Protect privacy by exposing only the area being examined and keeping the rest covered

    What to say to the patient

    I'll only uncover the area I need to examine, and I'll cover you back up right afterward.

After examination · if performed

  1. 01

    Summarize and explain the examination findings to the patient

    What to say to the patient

    When I examined you, there was tenderness when I pressed on [area].
