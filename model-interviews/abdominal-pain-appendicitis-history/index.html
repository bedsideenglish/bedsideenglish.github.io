Model interview

Full transcript

  1. Doctor

    Mr. Irwin, I'm Dr. Bennett. I can see you're in a lot of pain. While the nurse gets your vital signs, tell me what's been going on.

  2. Patient

    I've had stomach pain for about eighteen hours. It started as a dull ache around my belly button, but now it's a sharp pain low down on my right side.

  3. Doctor

    When exactly did the pain first start?

  4. Patient

    About eighteen hours ago, sometime yesterday evening. It was mild at first, so I didn't think much of it.

  5. Doctor

    How has the pain changed since then?

  6. Patient

    Over the last six hours it moved from around my navel down to my lower right side, and it's become much sharper and more constant.

  7. Doctor

    Can you point with one finger to exactly where it hurts the most right now?

  8. Patient

    Right here, low on my right side, about halfway between my hip bone and my belly button.

  9. Doctor

    On a scale from zero to ten, how bad is it, and does it come and go or stay constant?

  10. Patient

    It's about an eight out of ten, and it hasn't let up at all. It's constant now.

  11. Doctor

    Have you lost your appetite, felt nauseated, or vomited?

  12. Patient

    I haven't wanted to eat all day, I've felt sick to my stomach, and I threw up once after the pain moved to my right side.

  13. Doctor

    Have you had a fever or felt chilled?

  14. Patient

    I felt hot and a bit shaky earlier. I think I had a low fever, but I didn't check my temperature.

  15. Doctor

    Does moving around, coughing, or pressing on your belly make the pain worse?

  16. Patient

    Yes, all of that. Even the bumps in the taxi on the way here made it much worse.

  17. Doctor

    Have you had any diarrhea or constipation, or any burning or urgency when you urinate?

  18. Patient

    No, my bowel movements have been normal, and I haven't had any problems urinating.

  19. Doctor

    Have you ever had pain like this before, or hurt your abdomen recently?

  20. Patient

    No, I've never had anything like this, and I haven't injured myself.

  21. Doctor

    Have you ever had abdominal surgery, or do you have any other medical conditions?

  22. Patient

    No surgery, and I've always been healthy.

  23. Doctor

    Do you take any regular medicines, and do you have any medicine allergies?

  24. Patient

    No regular medicines, and no allergies that I know of.

  25. Doctor

    What do you think might be causing this, and what's worrying you most?

  26. Patient

    I think it's my appendix, and I'm scared it's about to burst if we don't do something soon.

  27. Doctor

    What were you hoping would happen today?

  28. Patient

    I want something for the pain, antibiotics if I need them, and if it really is my appendix, I want it taken out safely.

  29. Doctor

    Let me make sure I have this right. For eighteen hours you've had pain that began as a dull ache around your navel and moved over the last six hours to a sharp, constant pain in your lower right abdomen. It's worse with movement, coughing, and pressure, and you've had loss of appetite, nausea, one episode of vomiting, and a low fever. You've had no diarrhea, urinary symptoms, prior surgery, or similar pain before. Is that accurate, and is there anything else I should know?

  30. Patient

    That's exactly right. Nothing else.

  31. Doctor

    Thank you. I'll examine your abdomen now, and we'll get blood tests and likely a CT scan moving right away. Until then you'll stay off food or drink, and we'll start IV fluids and pain relief. If this is appendicitis, we'll begin antibiotics and get the surgical team involved promptly so nothing is delayed.

  32. Patient

    Okay, thank you. That makes me feel a little better about what happens next.

Flow at a glance

The route through the history

  1. Invite the patient's story while assessing
  2. Establish exact onset and duration
  3. Track the pain's migration from periumbilical to right lower quadrant
  4. Characterize severity and associated GI symptoms
  5. Screen aggravating factors and mimicking symptoms
  6. Review background, medicines, and allergies
  7. Explore the patient's own ideas and expectations
  8. Summarize and move to examination and same-day surgical planning

Do not miss

Questions that change the next step

  • Migration of pain from periumbilical to the right lower quadrant
  • Anorexia, nausea, vomiting, and fever
  • Pain worsened by movement, coughing, or pressure
  • Diarrhea, urinary symptoms, or a gynecologic mimic to consider
  • No prior abdominal surgery or similar episode
