Model interview

Full transcript

  1. Doctor

    Ms. Brown, I'm Dr. Osei. What's going on today?

  2. Patient

    This morning my urine came out dark, almost like cola, and my face looks puffy around my eyes. It's really scared me.

  3. Doctor

    When exactly did you first notice the dark urine?

  4. Patient

    Just this morning, when I went to the bathroom. I've never seen it that color before.

  5. Doctor

    Has the color changed at all since then, or stayed the same?

  6. Patient

    It's stayed about the same, dark brown, like tea or cola.

  7. Doctor

    Tell me about the puffiness around your eyes. When did that start, and is it worse at any particular time?

  8. Patient

    I noticed it this morning too, and it does look worse right when I wake up.

  9. Doctor

    Have you had any headache or noticed anything about your blood pressure?

  10. Patient

    I've had a mild headache today, but I haven't checked my blood pressure.

  11. Doctor

    Has the amount of urine you're passing changed? More, less, or about the same?

  12. Patient

    I think it's a bit less than usual, now that you mention it.

  13. Doctor

    Do you have any pain when you urinate, or pain in your back or side?

  14. Patient

    No burning, and no back pain.

  15. Doctor

    Have you been sick recently? A sore throat, fever, or skin infection?

  16. Patient

    Yes, actually, I had a really bad sore throat with a fever about two weeks ago. I never went to the doctor for it, and it eventually went away on its own.

  17. Doctor

    Any swelling in your legs or ankles, or shortness of breath?

  18. Patient

    My ankles look a little swollen too, now that I think about it. No trouble breathing.

  19. Doctor

    Any joint pain, rash, or blood in your stool?

  20. Patient

    No, none of that.

  21. Doctor

    Do you have any other medical conditions, or take any regular medicines?

  22. Patient

    No, I'm generally healthy and don't take anything regularly.

  23. Doctor

    Any medicine allergies?

  24. Patient

    No, none that I know of.

  25. Doctor

    What do you think is going on?

  26. Patient

    I thought maybe I have a bad urinary tract infection and I'm bleeding internally.

  27. Doctor

    What's worrying you most?

  28. Patient

    The dark urine and my face swelling up really scared me. It doesn't feel normal at all.

  29. Doctor

    What were you hoping we'd do today?

  30. Patient

    I was hoping you could give me antibiotics to clear up the kidney infection.

  31. Doctor

    Let me make sure I have this right. This morning you noticed dark, cola-colored urine along with puffiness around your eyes that's worse on waking, a mild headache, slightly reduced urine output, and some ankle swelling. About two weeks ago you had an untreated sore throat with fever. You have no burning with urination, back pain, joint pain, or rash. Is that accurate, and is there anything else?

  32. Patient

    That's exactly right, nothing else.

  33. Doctor

    Thank you. This isn't a bladder infection. The pattern of dark urine, facial swelling, and a sore throat about two weeks earlier points to your kidneys' filters reacting to that infection, called post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis. We'll check your blood pressure, kidney function, and urine under the microscope right away, and treat the swelling and blood pressure while your kidneys recover over the next several weeks.

  34. Patient

    Okay, that's a relief to understand what's actually happening.

Flow at a glance

The route through the history

  1. Invite the patient's description
  2. Establish the timing and color of the dark urine
  3. Characterize the facial swelling pattern
  4. Screen kidney-related symptoms and urine output
  5. Rule out a simple urinary infection
  6. Ask about a recent throat or skin infection
  7. Review background, medicines, and allergies
  8. Summarize and explain the real diagnosis

Do not miss

Questions that change the next step

  • Dark, cola-colored urine and periorbital edema worse in the morning
  • A sore throat or skin infection one to three weeks earlier
  • Reduced urine output or leg swelling
  • Absence of dysuria or flank pain arguing against a simple urinary infection
  • New or worsening high blood pressure and headache
