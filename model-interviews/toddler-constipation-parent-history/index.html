Model interview

Follow the clinical thread

No commentary between lines. Listen first, or read straight through.

  1. Doctor

    Hi, Sophia. I'm Dr. Chen. I'm going to talk with your mom about your tummy, and you can tell me anything you want. Mom, what has been happening?

  2. Parent

    She only has a bowel movement about once every five days, and it is very hard and painful.

  3. Doctor

    When did this pattern begin?

  4. Parent

    About four months ago, when we started toilet training. Before that she went more regularly.

  5. Doctor

    What do the stools look like, and how does Sophia act when she needs to go?

  6. Parent

    They are large, hard pieces. She crosses her legs, squeezes her bottom, and hides in a corner to hold it in.

  7. Doctor

    Does she cry with a bowel movement or have any blood on the stool or toilet paper?

  8. Parent

    She cries because it hurts. I've seen a tiny streak of bright red blood twice, after a very hard stool.

  9. Doctor

    Does stool ever leak into her underwear between bowel movements?

  10. Parent

    Sometimes there is a small smear, and I thought she wasn't wiping well.

  11. Doctor

    Does she have belly pain, swelling, vomiting, fever, or a poor appetite?

  12. Parent

    Her belly aches before she goes, but it settles afterward. No vomiting or fever, and she eats normally.

  13. Doctor

    Has she been growing as expected or losing weight?

  14. Parent

    She has been growing normally and hasn't lost weight.

  15. Doctor

    Thinking back to when she was born, did she pass her first dark stool within the first day or two?

  16. Parent

    Yes, the hospital said everything was normal.

  17. Doctor

    Has constipation been a problem since infancy, or is this the first period like this?

  18. Parent

    This is the first time. She had normal soft stools as a baby.

  19. Doctor

    Any weakness in her legs, trouble walking, urine infections, or loss of bladder control?

  20. Parent

    No. She runs everywhere and hasn't had urine infections.

  21. Doctor

    What does she usually eat and drink in a day? How much milk does she have?

  22. Parent

    She loves milk and probably drinks four cups. She is picky with vegetables, but she eats some fruit and drinks water.

  23. Doctor

    Have you tried any stool softeners, suppositories, or other remedies?

  24. Parent

    Only prune juice. I haven't given her medicine.

  25. Doctor

    How are you approaching toilet training right now?

  26. Parent

    We ask her to sit after dinner, but she gets upset. Sometimes we push because we know she needs to go.

  27. Doctor

    This can become a painful cycle, and it is not her fault or yours. Has there been any major change at home or at daycare?

  28. Parent

    She moved to a new daycare five months ago. She refuses to use their toilet.

  29. Doctor

    Does anyone in the family have severe constipation, thyroid disease, celiac disease, or Hirschsprung disease?

  30. Parent

    No, nothing like that.

  31. Doctor

    Does Sophia take any regular medicine, vitamin, or iron? Does she have any medicine allergies?

  32. Parent

    No regular medicines and no known allergies.

  33. Doctor

    Let me summarize. Sophia had normal stools from birth until toilet training began four months ago. She now passes a large, hard, painful stool about every five days and tries to hold it in. She sometimes has a small smear in her underwear and a streak of blood after a hard stool, but no persistent vomiting, swollen abdomen, poor growth, leg symptoms, or problems since birth. Is that right?

  34. Parent

    Yes, that's right.

  35. Doctor

    Thank you. I'll examine her, including her growth and abdomen. Then we'll make a plan to soften the stools and take the pressure out of toilet time, with clear signs for when to contact us urgently.

  36. Parent

    That would be a relief.

Flow at a glance

The route through the history

  1. Speak to the child first
  2. Ask the parent for the pattern
  3. Describe stool and withholding behavior
  4. Look for pain, soiling, and complications
  5. Check onset from birth and growth
  6. Explore diet, toilet training, and environment
  7. Remove blame and summarize

Do not miss

Questions that change the next step

  • Delayed passage of meconium or symptoms from early infancy
  • Vomiting, marked abdominal distention, fever, weight loss, or poor growth
  • Blood not clearly limited to a hard-stool episode
  • Leg weakness, abnormal walking, or bladder symptoms
  • Stool withholding, fecal soiling, diet, and toilet-training stress
