Model interview

Follow the clinical thread

No commentary between lines. Listen first, or read straight through.

  1. Doctor

    Mr. Yoon, I'm Dr. Adams. We'll keep checking your alertness while we talk. Tell me exactly what happened four hours ago.

  2. Patient

    Another player and I collided during basketball, and his shoulder hit the side of my head. I fell down and felt dazed.

  3. Doctor

    Did you lose consciousness at any point?

  4. Patient

    No. I never blacked out.

  5. Doctor

    What do you remember from just before and just after the hit?

  6. Patient

    I remember the play and sitting on the court afterward. My teammate said I seemed confused for about a minute, but I don't have a gap in my memory.

  7. Doctor

    How have the headache, dizziness, and nausea changed since then?

  8. Patient

    The headache is still mild, about four out of ten, and hasn't been getting worse. I'm a little dizzy and nauseated.

  9. Doctor

    Have you vomited or had a seizure?

  10. Patient

    No.

  11. Doctor

    Since the hit, have you noticed any new problem with vision, speech, strength, feeling, or walking?

  12. Patient

    No. Bright light bothers me a little, but I can see, speak, move, and walk normally.

  13. Doctor

    Have you become more confused or unusually drowsy, or been difficult to wake?

  14. Patient

    No. I've stayed awake and feel clearer than I did right after the collision.

  15. Doctor

    Do you have pain or tenderness in your neck?

  16. Patient

    No neck pain.

  17. Doctor

    I ask because these can change the bleeding risk: do you take a blood thinner, have a bleeding disorder, or use alcohol or drugs before the injury?

  18. Patient

    No to all of those. I don't take any regular medicine.

  19. Doctor

    Have you ever had a concussion or another significant head injury?

  20. Patient

    No, this is the first time.

  21. Doctor

    If you go home, who could stay with you and watch for a change?

  22. Patient

    My roommate can stay with me tonight. I'm worried about a brain bleed and wonder if I need a scan. I also want to know when I can practice again.

  23. Doctor

    You were struck on the side of the head four hours ago and were briefly confused but did not lose consciousness or lose memory. Your mild headache, dizziness, and nausea are not worsening, and you report no repeated vomiting, seizure, new neurologic problem, increasing drowsiness, neck pain, or bleeding risk. Is that correct, and is there anything else I should know?

  24. Patient

    That's correct. Nothing else.

  25. Doctor

    We'll complete a neurologic and neck examination and use the full findings to decide whether imaging is needed. You must not return to play today. If you go home, your roommate will receive clear written danger signs such as a worsening headache, repeated vomiting, confusion, weakness, seizure, or trouble waking you. We'll also give you instructions for classes, driving, follow-up, and a gradual return to sport only after medical clearance.

  26. Patient

    Understood. I won't go back to practice until I'm cleared.

Flow at a glance

The route through the history

  1. Confirm current alertness while taking the history
  2. Reconstruct mechanism and timing
  3. Check loss of consciousness and memory
  4. Track symptom severity and direction of change
  5. Screen for neurologic and cervical-spine danger signs
  6. Assess bleeding risk, substances, and prior concussion
  7. Confirm safe observation and patient concerns
  8. Summarize, prohibit same-day return, and give a concrete safety net

Do not miss

Questions that change the next step

  • Loss of consciousness, amnesia, confusion, and change in alertness
  • Worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, focal neurologic change, or unequal pupils
  • Neck pain or tenderness
  • Anticoagulant or antiplatelet use, bleeding disorder, and alcohol or drug use
  • A responsible observer, written return precautions, and no same-day return to play
