Full transcript

  1. Doctor

    Ms. Baker, I'm Dr. Whitfield. Tell me what's happening with your vision.

  2. Patient

    There's a dark shadow spreading across my right eye. It started this morning, and it's getting worse.

  3. Doctor

    Can you describe the shadow? Where did it start, and is it still spreading?

  4. Patient

    It started low, near my nose, like a curtain rising from the bottom. Now it's covering more of the center of my vision.

  5. Doctor

    Did you notice anything before the shadow appeared, like flashes of light or new floaters?

  6. Patient

    Yes, yesterday afternoon I started seeing lots of little dark specks, like a swarm of gnats, and bright flashes of light off to the side.

  7. Doctor

    Is there any pain or redness in the eye?

  8. Patient

    No, no pain at all, and it doesn't look red.

  9. Doctor

    Are you very nearsighted, or do you wear a strong glasses prescription?

  10. Patient

    Yes, I'm extremely nearsighted, I've worn a strong prescription my whole life.

  11. Doctor

    Have you had any eye surgery before, like cataract surgery?

  12. Patient

    Yes, I had cataract surgery on that same eye about two years ago.

  13. Doctor

    Has your vision in the other eye changed at all?

  14. Patient

    No, my left eye seems completely normal.

  15. Doctor

    Have you had any head or eye injury recently?

  16. Patient

    No, nothing like that.

  17. Doctor

    Do you have any other medical conditions, like diabetes or high blood pressure?

  18. Patient

    No, I'm generally healthy.

  19. Doctor

    Any regular medicines or medicine allergies?

  20. Patient

    No regular medicines, and no known allergies.

  21. Doctor

    What are you most afraid of right now?

  22. Patient

    I'm terrified I'm going blind in that eye, and that it's happening right now while we're talking.

  23. Doctor

    What were you hoping we could do today?

  24. Patient

    I want to see a specialist immediately and get whatever surgery or laser treatment I need to fix it.

  25. Doctor

    Let me make sure I understand. Starting yesterday afternoon you noticed new floaters and flashes of light in your right eye, and this morning a painless dark curtain began rising from the lower inner part of your vision and has continued to spread toward the center. You have high myopia and prior cataract surgery in that eye, with no pain, redness, injury, or change in the other eye. Is that accurate?

  26. Patient

    Yes, that's exactly right.

  27. Doctor

    Thank you. Everything you're describing is very concerning for a retinal detachment, which is a genuine eye emergency, especially since your risk factors, high myopia and prior cataract surgery, make it more likely. We need to get you to a retina specialist right away. Until then, please try to keep your head still and avoid bending over or straining, and we'll give you specific positioning instructions once the specialist has assessed exactly where the detachment is.

  28. Patient

    Okay, I'll do exactly what you say. I just don't want to lose my sight.

Flow at a glance

The route through the history

  1. Invite the patient's description of the shadow
  2. Characterize the visual field defect and its progression
  3. Ask about preceding floaters and flashes
  4. Confirm the absence of pain or redness
  5. Establish risk factors: myopia and prior eye surgery
  6. Check the unaffected eye and rule out injury
  7. Review background, medicines, and allergies
  8. Summarize and activate the emergency referral

Do not miss

Questions that change the next step

  • A progressive, painless dark curtain or shadow in the visual field
  • New floaters and flashes of light preceding the shadow
  • High myopia and prior cataract surgery as major risk factors
  • Absence of pain or redness (a painless white eye)
  • The urgency; this needs same-day retina specialist evaluation
