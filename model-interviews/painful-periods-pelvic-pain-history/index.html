Model interview

Follow the clinical thread

No commentary between lines. Listen first, or read straight through.

  1. Doctor

    Ms. Yoon, I'm Dr. Morgan. What would you like help with today?

  2. Patient

    My periods have become very painful, and my husband and I have been trying to get pregnant for a year without success.

  3. Doctor

    Tell me how the pain has changed over that time.

  4. Patient

    It used to be manageable, but for the past year the cramps have become severe. I sometimes have to miss work.

  5. Doctor

    Where do you feel the pain, and when does it begin in relation to your period?

  6. Patient

    Deep in my pelvis and lower back. It starts the day before my period and is worst for the first two days.

  7. Doctor

    How long are your cycles, and when did your last period start?

  8. Patient

    They're regular, about twenty-eight days. My last period started twelve days ago.

  9. Doctor

    How long do you bleed, and has the amount changed?

  10. Patient

    About five days. It is a little heavier than it used to be, but I'm not soaking through a pad every hour.

  11. Doctor

    Do you have bleeding between periods or after sex?

  12. Patient

    No.

  13. Doctor

    I ask everyone with pelvic pain about sex because it can help us understand the pain. Is it okay if I ask you a few questions?

  14. Patient

    Yes, that's okay.

  15. Doctor

    Do you have pain during sex? If so, does it feel near the entrance or deeper in the pelvis?

  16. Patient

    It's a deep pain, especially with deeper penetration. That has also become worse this year.

  17. Doctor

    Any unusual vaginal discharge, fever, or history of a sexually transmitted infection or pelvic infection?

  18. Patient

    No discharge or fever, and I've never had an STI or pelvic infection.

  19. Doctor

    Do bowel movements or urination become painful around your period?

  20. Patient

    Bowel movements are more painful during my period. I have IBS, but this pain feels more tied to my cycle.

  21. Doctor

    Have you ever been pregnant, had pelvic surgery, or been treated for any gynecologic condition?

  22. Patient

    No pregnancies and no surgery. I haven't had any gynecologic treatment.

  23. Doctor

    Are you and your husband having sex regularly without birth control, and has either of you had fertility testing?

  24. Patient

    Yes, usually two or three times a week without birth control. Neither of us has had testing.

  25. Doctor

    Are there any other partners or pregnancy possibilities that would change which tests we should discuss?

  26. Patient

    No, just my husband.

  27. Doctor

    What medicines, supplements, or pain relievers do you use? Any medicine allergies?

  28. Patient

    I take ibuprofen during my period and a prenatal vitamin. I don't have any known allergies.

  29. Doctor

    Does anyone in your family have endometriosis or difficulty becoming pregnant?

  30. Patient

    My aunt had endometriosis. I don't know of fertility problems in anyone else.

  31. Doctor

    What are you most concerned about, and what were you hoping would happen today?

  32. Patient

    I'm worried the pain means I may never get pregnant. I've read about laparoscopy and want to know if I need it.

  33. Doctor

    Let me make sure I have this right. Over the past year your period pain has become severe enough to disrupt work. You also have deep pain during sex and bowel pain around your period. Your cycles are regular, and you have been having regular sex without birth control for a year without becoming pregnant. Your main concern is future fertility. Is that accurate?

  34. Patient

    Yes, exactly.

  35. Doctor

    Thank you. We can now discuss an examination and a stepwise evaluation of both the pelvic pain and fertility. We will decide together which tests are useful and whether a procedure would add value.

  36. Patient

    Thank you. I appreciate that.

Flow at a glance

The route through the history

  1. Open with both concerns
  2. Map pain to the menstrual cycle
  3. Characterize bleeding
  4. Ask permission before sexual history
  5. Separate bowel and bladder patterns
  6. Clarify pregnancy intention and fertility timeline
  7. Summarize the patient's priority

Do not miss

Questions that change the next step

  • Last menstrual period and pregnancy possibility
  • Bleeding between periods or after sex
  • Pain during sex and whether it is superficial or deep
  • Cyclic bowel or bladder pain
  • Pelvic infection history
  • Pregnancy intention and duration of unprotected intercourse
