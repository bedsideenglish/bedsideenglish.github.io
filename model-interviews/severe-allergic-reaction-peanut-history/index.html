Full transcript

  1. Doctor

    I'm Dr. Marsh. We're giving you epinephrine right now. While the nurse does that, tell me what happened.

  2. Patient

    I ate pad thai... it had peanuts... I'm so allergic.

  3. Doctor

    How long ago did you eat it?

  4. Patient

    About fifteen minutes ago.

  5. Doctor

    Is it hard to breathe or swallow right now?

  6. Patient

    Yes... my throat feels tight... hard to swallow.

  7. Doctor

    Does your voice sound different to you?

  8. Patient

    Yeah... it feels hoarse.

  9. Doctor

    Do you have hives or itching anywhere on your body?

  10. Patient

    All over... my chest, my arms... everywhere.

  11. Doctor

    Do you feel dizzy or like you might faint?

  12. Patient

    Yes... really dizzy... lightheaded.

  13. Doctor

    Have you had a reaction like this before?

  14. Patient

    Yes... I've been allergic to peanuts since I was a kid.

  15. Doctor

    Do you carry an epinephrine auto-injector?

  16. Patient

    I have one... but I left it at home today.

  17. Doctor

    Do you have asthma, or take any inhalers?

  18. Patient

    Yes... mild asthma... I use albuterol sometimes.

  19. Doctor

    Any other medical conditions or medicine allergies?

  20. Patient

    No... just the peanut allergy.

  21. Doctor

    What are you most afraid is happening right now?

  22. Patient

    I feel like my throat is closing... I'm scared I can't breathe.

  23. Doctor

    Let me make sure I have this. About fifteen minutes ago you ate pad thai with peanuts, and now your throat feels tight, your voice is hoarse, you have hives all over, and you feel very dizzy. You have a known peanut allergy and mild asthma, but you didn't have your epinephrine with you today. Is that right?

  24. Patient

    Yes... that's right.

  25. Doctor

    Thank you. The epinephrine we just gave you is working right now to open your airway and bring your blood pressure back up. We're also giving you oxygen and fluids, and we'll watch you closely for several hours, because these reactions can sometimes come back even after they start improving. Before you go home, we'll make sure you leave with two new epinephrine auto-injectors and a clear action plan.

  26. Patient

    Okay... thank you... it does feel a little easier already.

Flow at a glance

The route through the history

  1. Start treatment while taking a rapid history
  2. Confirm the trigger food and timing
  3. Screen airway symptoms
  4. Screen skin symptoms
  5. Screen circulatory symptoms
  6. Confirm known allergy and epinephrine access
  7. Check asthma and other background
  8. Summarize briefly and explain ongoing emergency treatment

Do not miss

Questions that change the next step

  • Airway symptoms: throat tightness, hoarseness, or stridor
  • Diffuse hives or angioedema
  • Dizziness or low blood pressure signaling circulatory involvement
  • Known allergy history and whether epinephrine was available
  • That epinephrine must not be delayed for history-taking
