How to ask for and confirm bus directions in English

Turn a fast set of directions into one clear route before you get off the bus.

Don’t guess the missing detail.

Catch it, ask for it again, and confirm it before the conversation moves on.

Quick answer

For bus directions, ask about the missing step, then repeat the route in order. Confirm the stop, street, bus number, and destination only when each one matters. A short route summary helps the driver correct one turn before you get off, instead of leaving you to guess after the bus has moved on.

  1. Missed one step

    “Sorry, which street do I cross?”

    Ask for the exact part you missed.

  2. Heard the route

    “Do I take the 14 toward Central Station?”

    Confirm the route and direction together.

  3. Ready to move

    “Get off at the museum, cross King Street, then take the 14—right?”

    Repeat the steps in order.

Use the smallest useful repair

A three-step response ladder

Move from a broad repeat to a precise confirmation, then say the important detail back once.

1

Locate

One landmark or street was unclear.

“Sorry, which street do I cross?”

A specific question repairs one step.

2

Confirm

You have the bus and destination.

“Do I take the 14 toward Central Station?”

Route number and direction belong together.

3

Say it back

You are ready to get off.

“Get off at the museum, cross King Street, then take the 14—right?”

The order matters as much as the words.

What should you say next?

  1. You missed the stopAsk for the landmark“Which stop do I get off at?”
  2. You missed the turnAsk for the street“Which street do I cross?”
  3. You missed the routeConfirm number and direction“Is it the 14 toward Central Station?”

One complete repair

Driver

“Get off at the museum, cross King Street, then take the 14 toward Central Station.”

The route contains three ordered steps.
You

“Sorry, the 14 toward Central Station?”

Confirm the final bus and direction.
Driver

“That’s right. Cross King Street first.”

The reply restores the order.
You

“Museum, King Street, then the 14. Thanks.”

A short route summary closes the loop.

Common failure points

Repair the interaction, not your confidence

A useful response gives the other person a clear way to help.

Remembering the bus number but not the direction

Ask for both together: “Which bus toward where?”

Confirming steps out of order

Use “then” when you say the route back.

Asking for every detail again

Name the one step that disappeared.

Same move, new setting

Transfer the skill

Do not memorize one airport sentence. Reuse the confirmation pattern wherever a detail matters.

Train station

The platform sequence was unclear.

“So I change at Oak Street, then take Platform 6?”
Museum

The entrance direction was unclear.

“Do I turn left after the ticket desk?”
Walking tour

The next landmark was unclear.

“Which building do we meet in front of?”

Short answers

Questions learners ask

How do I ask which bus to take?

Say, “Which bus should I take toward Central Station?”

Should I repeat every step?

Repeat the steps that affect your next move; keep it short.

What if I miss the street name?

Ask directly: “Sorry, which street do I cross?”
