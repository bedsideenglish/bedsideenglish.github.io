How to confirm a changed meeting time and room in English

When someone says “not” or corrects a time, confirm the new plan—not the first number you heard.

Don’t guess the missing detail.

Catch it, ask for it again, and confirm it before the conversation moves on.

Quick answer

When a meeting detail changes, listen for the correction and confirm the final value. Do not repeat the first time or room just because it arrived first. Ask which detail is current, then say the room and time back together so you can act on one clear plan.

  1. Heard two values

    “Sorry, was it 2:45 or 3:00?”

    Ask the speaker to choose the current value.

  2. Heard a correction

    “So the meeting is at 2:45, not 3:00?”

    Make the correction explicit.

  3. Need the plan

    “Room 108 at 2:45—right?”

    Say both usable details back.

A three-step response ladder

Move from a broad repeat to a precise confirmation, then say the important detail back once.

1

Separate

You heard two possible times.

“Sorry, was it 2:45 or 3:00?”

A choice question makes the correction clear.

2

Confirm

You heard the new time.

“So the meeting is at 2:45, not 3:00?”

It checks which value replaced the other.

3

Say it back

Common failure points

Repair the interaction, not your confidence

A useful response gives the other person a clear way to help.

Keeping the first number you hear

Listen for “not,” “instead,” or “changed to.”

Confirming only the time

When both matter, say the room and time together.

Asking for the full message again

Ask which time or room is current.

Same move, new setting

Transfer the skill

Do not memorize one airport sentence. Reuse the confirmation pattern wherever a detail matters.

Doctor’s office

The appointment day changed.

“Just to confirm, Friday instead of Thursday?”
Restaurant

The reservation time changed.

“So the table is for 7:00, not 7:30?”
Video call

The meeting link changed.

“Could you send the new link once more?”

Short answers

Questions learners ask

How do I check which time is correct?

Ask, “Which time is correct?” or offer the two values as a choice.

Is “not 3:00?” polite?

Yes, when your tone is neutral and you are checking a clear correction.

Should I repeat the room too?

Yes, if going to the wrong room would cause a problem.
