How to take a back pain history in English

Master precise English questions to evaluate back pain, explore patterns of joint stiffness, and ask about related symptoms clearly.

Quick answer

What can I ask in this scenario?

Ask if the back pain is worse in the morning and whether gentle movement helps, how long it has lasted, if there is heel or foot tenderness, past eye redness, and whether family members have similar joint or spine conditions.

“Is your back pain worse when you first wake up in the morning, and does moving around or exercising help relieve it?”

Communication context

A realistic patient scenario

Imagine this consultation: A young adult patient presents with persistent lower back pain and morning stiffness that has been present for several months. Your task is to explore the history in natural English without overwhelming the patient with clinical jargon.

Useful questions for this scenario

Natural English questions, grouped by purpose

These are selected questions for this particular practice case. They are not an exhaustive clinical checklist.

The symptom story

“Is your back pain worse when you first wake up in the morning, and does moving around or exercising help relieve it?”

Purpose: Ask whether the back pain is worse in the morning and whether gentle movement or exercise helps.

Why this wording

Contrasting morning rest with movement helps patients describe whether physical activity relieves or worsens their stiffness.

Open exploration
“How does your back feel when you first get out of bed compared to later in the day?”
Movement focus
“Does gentle exercise tend to make the back pain better or worse?”

“How long have you been experiencing this back pain?”

Purpose: Ask how long the back pain and stiffness have been present.

Associated symptoms

“Have you had any tenderness or pain at the back of your heel or under your feet?”

Purpose: Ask about pain or tenderness around the heels or the bottom of the feet.

Why this wording

Specifying “the back of your heel or under your feet” translates the medical concept of enthesitis into everyday anatomy that patients recognize immediately.

Health background

“Have you ever had a painful, red eye or sudden blurriness in your vision?”

Purpose: Ask if the patient has ever had episodes of painful, red eyes or blurred vision.

“Has anyone in your family been diagnosed with chronic back conditions, psoriasis, or arthritis?”

Purpose: Ask if family members have been diagnosed with chronic back conditions, psoriasis, or arthritis.

Why this wording

Including related conditions like psoriasis or chronic back conditions broadens the family inquiry beyond generic arthritis.

Put the phrases together

Move from open to focused questions

  1. Open

    “Could you tell me more about what has been happening?”

  2. Focus

    Choose a relevant question from the groups above.

  3. Check

    “Is there anything else about this that you think I should know?”

Language note: Short, direct questions are often easier to answer. Ask one idea at a time, then follow the patient's wording when you need more detail.
